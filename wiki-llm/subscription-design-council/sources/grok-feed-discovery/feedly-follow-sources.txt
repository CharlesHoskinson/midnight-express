Follow sources in Feedly
Feedly gives you multiple ways to find and follow sources that matter to your research or threat intelligence workflows. You can search by keyword, paste a specific website URL, or explore curated bundles created by the Feedly team.
You’ll find all source discovery options under the 
“
Follow Sources
”
 section in the left-hand menu.
You can:
Search for topics like “Malware” or “CyberSecurity” to discover relevant feeds
Use hashtags for trend-based discovery (e.g., 
#cybersecurity
 )
Paste a URL to follow a specific website or blog
Browse curated bundles grouped by themes like cybersecurity, CSO publications, or vulnerabilities
These bundles are dynamic and updated regularly, offering a quick way to follow dozens or even thousands of sources at once.
Using RSS feeds and Feedly’s RSS builder
Feedly automatically detects RSS feeds for most websites. Simply paste the website URL, and Feedly will offer any available RSS feeds, including sub-feeds (e.g., a feed just for data breaches).
If a site doesn’t offer an RSS feed, Feedly can create one using the 
RSS builder
. This tool analyzes pages with structured lists of links (like blog archives or news pages) and builds a usable feed from them.
Once generated, you can follow the new feed and save it to a folder just like any other source.
Curating sources with folders
A good practice is to create folders that separate raw source feeds from your AI feeds. For example:
sources - cybersecurity
sources - foreign policy
ai - threat intel
ai - geopolitics
This structure keeps unfiltered sources organized and avoids clutter in your AI Feeds. When you add sources to AI Feeds, you’ll be able to easily identify and include only those with relevant, high-quality content.
Following non-traditional sources
Newsletters
Feedly can ingest email newsletters directly into your feeds. You’ll be provided with a custom Feedly email address, which you can:
Use to subscribe to new newsletters
Forward existing newsletter emails
Feedly will convert each email into a readable article format and treat it like any other source in your account.
Reddit
You can follow subreddits by pasting the subreddit URL or simply entering the name (e.g., 
r/cybersecurity
 ) into the source search. Reddit content will appear as a stream of articles in your Feedly feeds.
X (formerly Twitter)
Feedly supports ingesting tweets from X via the official API. This requires an API key, which typically costs $100–200 per month. Once authenticated, you can follow X accounts or hashtags and display tweets alongside your other sources.
Adding sources to AI Feeds
Once you’ve curated your source folders, you can use them as the foundation for your AI Feeds.
To do this:
Open any AI Feed
Click the pencil icon → 
refine AI Feed
Add sources from your folders using the search bar
Thanks to consistent naming conventions (e.g., folders that start with 
sources -
 ), it’s easy to find and reuse relevant content across feeds.
Filtering and refining content
Top sources only
This filter narrows your results to only the most trusted, high-quality sources. Feedly uses a three-tier ranking system based on popularity and editorial credibility. With this option enabled, only the top third of sources are used in the feed.
Top stories only
This filter surfaces only stories that appear in at least five different publications—helping you focus on what’s gaining traction across the web. It’s especially useful for identifying signals of emerging trends or high-impact incidents.
You can combine both filters for maximum precision.
Using curated bundles for specialized use cases
In addition to building your own source sets, you can lean on Feedly’s curated bundles. These include:
Cybersecurity
Vulnerability intelligence
National newspapers
Foreign affairs
Threat intelligence
Tech and innovation
Bundles are especially useful for jumpstarting new use cases or quickly aligning to a new research focus. You can preview the top sources in each bundle by hovering over the tooltips and clicking 
“view feeds.”
Best practices for source curation
Keep sources and AI feeds separate:
 This prevents unfiltered RSS content from muddying your AI feed results.
Use consistent naming:
 Prefix folders with “sources -” or “ai -” so they’re easy to identify and reuse.
Layer bundles with custom sources:
 You can mix Feedly-curated bundles with your own hand-picked websites or newsletters to tailor feeds to your unique requirements.
Regularly audit sources:
 Remove outdated, inactive, or overly noisy sources to keep your feeds focused and actionable.
