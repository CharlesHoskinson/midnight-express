## D1 — Event format

(a) **s4-evidence-auditor:** one 4,096-byte off-chain cell, sealed session headers, 48-hour life, at most 16 fragments. **s1-privacy-theorist:** one `Misc` event, constant 32-byte name, 256-byte ChaCha20-Poly1305 payload, 205-byte body, 14-day indexer life. **o4-governance-operations:** a 304-byte visible header, four size classes from 512 B to 32 KiB, a routing tag, RLN fields, and a franking slot. **o1-midnight-integrator:** four classes from 1 KiB to 64 KiB, a 16-byte tag, a 68-byte clue on every envelope, and an RLN proof planned at about 3 KiB.

(b) `Misc` is tag 10 and 288 bytes, `name: Bytes<32>` and `payload: Bytes<256>` (`minokawa-compact/compiler/midnight-events.ss:71-74`). `emit` takes a standard event type and is forbidden in the constructor (`minokawa-compact/doc/compact-reference.mdx:3497-3507`). The disclosed-only rule is CoIP-0003’s draft rationale at `coips/coip-0003.md:107-109`, not those reference lines. The CoIP lists encryption, topic filters, and recipient keys as a different problem it is not solving. On tag `ledger-9.1.0.0-rc.5`, `MAX_LOG_SIZE` is 512 KiB and `MAX_LOG_EMITTED` is 1 KiB, and `decode_event` drops anything larger (`onchain-vm/src/vm.rs:39-43`, `:274-275`). A 288-byte `Misc` sits under that drop. `global_ttl` is 1,209,600 seconds (`midnight-node/res/mainnet/ledger-parameters-config.json:176`). The stack notes bind it to intent TTL (`tblock <= ttl <= tblock + global_ttl`), not to event deletion. MLS `PrivateMessage` carries `group_id` and `epoch` in the clear (`2023-barnes-rfc9420`, text around the struct at lines 2046-2051), so s4’s reason to seal MLS is real. Beck’s flag ciphertext is 68 bytes for `p = 2^-n` up to `n = 24`, with a 0.548 ms test in that paper’s benchmark (`2021-beck-fmd`, lines 123-124). Hecate’s Table 4 lists 380 B of communication overhead (`2021-issa-hecate`, lines 3476-3479). The Waku RLN proof object is `merkle_root`, `share_x`, `share_y`, `nullifier`, and a proof of 128 bytes compressed or 256 uncompressed (`2021-vac-waku2-rln-relay-spec`, lines 327-330). o1’s `hash.rs:23` is the `Sha256` import; `persistent_hash` is SHA-256 at lines 93-94 of that tag. Len et al. show ChaCha20-Poly1305 is not key-committing, with a narrower attack than AES-GCM (`2021-len-partitioningoracle`, lines 38-39 and 59-64). The Bitmessage guide documents the 2018 `eval()` removal at lines 352-358. The Zcash catalog note for `2018-hopwood-zcashprotocol` says wallets trial-decrypt with ChaCha20-Poly1305; the plain-text extract was not in `graph/text`, so I did not re-read the PDF.

(c) Vote **s1-privacy-theorist**. One fixed ciphertext in the event the chain already has. Size classes, clues, tags, RLN proofs, and franking tokens are visible structure that splits the set or adds a proof to every object. Sealing MLS matters only after a group session exists, and version one has no group session.

(d) Strongest objection: “gone after 14 days” treats `global_ttl` as a lifetime, and a 205-byte body is a product limit s1 does not measure. Block-body retention is unknown, which s1 says. Any archive that keeps the transaction keeps the ciphertext for as long as it keeps the block.

## D2 — Definition of “private”

(a) **s4:** content confidentiality and subscriber-interest privacy for a client that fetches the whole stream; publisher unlinkability, timing privacy, and relationship privacy against a global observer are refused. **s1:** confidentiality, topic privacy, and “chain-level publisher unlinkability” because a transaction has no Substrate signer; forward secrecy, volume privacy, and global anonymity are out of scope. **o4:** nine properties, including event-to-membership unlinkability against everyone via RLN, and IP unlinkability only statistically, with a global passive adversary out of scope. **o1:** content, topic, bucket-level interest, RLN publisher unlinkability, and Dandelion++ source privacy against a colluding relay fraction; timing privacy and relationship anonymity against a global observer are refused.

(b) A `DustSpend` is `v_fee`, `old_nullifier`, `new_commitment`, and `proof` (`ledger-9.1.0.0-rc.5` `ledger/src/dust.rs:469-474`). The output value is reduced by `v_fee` at line 1765. That supports “no cleartext payer.” It does not support a proved unlinkability game. The trilemma’s abstract and Theorem statement exclude strong anonymity when `2βℓ < 1 − ε(η)` in the synchronized model (`2017-das-trilemma`, lines 30-36 and 279). s1 uses the slogan. s4’s `ℓ = 10`, `β = 0.01` gives `2ℓβ = 0.2`, which matches that inequality and is model arithmetic, not a ten-second engineering bound. Dandelion++ studies a fraction `p` of spy nodes, distinguishes mass deanonymization from targeted attacks, and places ISP/AS adversaries outside the paper (`2018-fanti-dandelionpp`, lines 250-277). Precision and recall are the paper’s metrics under that model, including an unknown random 4-regular graph in the theorem discussion. o1’s leakage table says a global observer defeats the stem. The property name P5 still reads as a launch guarantee the deployment has not inherited. Taheri et al. claim zero-knowledge membership with no trace to identity public keys in their model (lines 407-414) and list many registrations as an open problem (lines 488-493). o4’s “holds against everyone” is stronger than that paper and stronger than o4’s own row, where the chain sees the depositing address. RLN v1 uses one nullifier per peer per epoch, so two messages in an epoch share it (spec lines 140-146). RLN v2 says a large per-epoch limit avoids repeated internal nullifiers (`2024-vac-rln-v2-spec`, lines 52-54). o4’s 20-per-epoch tier follows v2. Seres et al. report that the server can recover a large part of the social graph (lines 95-96). RFC 9180 states that HPKE has no forward secrecy against recipient compromise (line 1525). Guerraoui et al., Theorem 5: for `f > 1`, `ε ≥ ln(f − 1)`, and if vertex connectivity is at most `f` then no finite worst-case `ε` exists (lines 587-609). That bounds gossip anonymity. It does not require building a mesh.

(c) Vote **s1-privacy-theorist**. The ledger path can support confidentiality and a topic that is absent from the public fields, against a relay, an indexer, and a chain observer who sees the `DustSpend` above. It correctly leaves forward secrecy, volume privacy, and global anonymity unclaimed.

(d) Strongest objection: the label “chain-level publisher unlinkability” is larger than the struct. A block with one bus transaction has one publisher. s1 says this, and a network observer still sees the RPC that accepted the transaction.

Those lines are cursor comments. The wallet does apply local secret keys at `:266` and `:295-302` on `zswapLedgerEvents` (`:250-258`). s4’s line cites match. `ledger_9/mod.rs:460-470` returns a state root, tx hash, addresses, and UTXO deltas, and the struct has no logs. (c) Vote **s1-privacy-theorist**. The subscription is the whole contract, so the indexer learns “this client watches this contract,” which every honest subscriber does. Tags, shards, and decoy fetches buy bandwidth by handing the store a narrower interest. Aztec’s own note says that leak is why OMR and PIR were proposed, and both are impractical there.

(d) Strongest objection: a contract that later acts in public re-links the consumer to the moment the event appeared. s1 states that and does not hide it. o1’s `checkRoot` pattern is the smallest later addition that hides which event, and it stays behind a trigger because it adds an anchor tree and a second proof.

## D4 — Sustainable model

(a) **s4:** sponsors buy relay and retention capacity; launch admission is an issuer-signed single-use permit; DUST pays only chain work. **s1:** the publisher pays DUST; no relay market, no proof-of-work, no RLN. **o4:** RLN-v2 deposits plus a non-refundable fee, grants then a probe-scored pool, about 86 DUST/day for anchors. **o1:** a DUST-priced Compact RLN membership proved off chain, Foundation-funded seeds, a cover floor, Privacy Pass later, about 390 DUST/day and about 550 NIGHT for anchors.

(b) DUST is shielded and non-transferable gas (`midnight-docs/docs/concepts/dust-architecture.mdx:23`). The same page sets the cap at 5 DUST per NIGHT and time-to-cap at `5,000,000,000 / 8,267 ≈ 604,800` seconds (lines 94-111). The genesis ratio and decay rate are `ledger-parameters-config.json:165-166`. `STARS_PER_NIGHT` and `SPECKS_PER_DUST` are `structure.rs:3362-3363` on the tag. s4’s 604,814 seconds is the unrounded quotient. `FeePrices::overall_cost` charges the maximum of normalized read, compute, and block usage, then adds write and churn (`base-crypto/src/cost_model.rs:408-416`). Genesis `overallPrice` is ten in 2^64 fixed point (`ledger-parameters-config.json:169-174`). At factor 1, block usage is 0.01 DUST per KB. That is arithmetic, not a live quote. The block-reward hook returns `(0, None)` in both feature configurations (`midnight-node/runtime/src/lib.rs:691` and `:695`). Schaub et al. say their change halves the harm and is defined to avoid extra cost for legitimate users, after finding the deployed formula made legitimate sending harder (lines 28-34). Taheri et al. report about 30 ms verification and about 0.5 s to prove a 2^32 membership on an iPhone 8, cited to their library, in a proof of concept (lines 428-430). o1’s 3.27 ms is `proof_verify_constant` 3,273,586,253 picoseconds (`ledger-parameters-config.json:128`; `CostDuration` is picoseconds at `cost_model.rs:36` and `:562`). o1 labels this a price weight and then budgets 0.25 core from it. o4’s 86 DUST/day assumes a 6 KB anchor and block-usage only: `6 × 0.01 × 1,440 = 86.4`. s4’s 0.08192 DUST for an 8,192-byte anchor is the same class of inference, and s4 labels it.

(c) Vote **s1-privacy-theorist**. The publisher pays the fee the chain already computes. RLN’s own paper leaves multi-registration spam open and reports a 30 ms verify on a different stack. Proof-of-work does not separate spammers from users in the Schaub result, and it does not pay an indexer.

(d) Strongest objection: a flood that fills the cap raises the price of every Midnight transaction, and nothing in this model pays the indexer for egress. s1 accepts both. That holds only while an indexer that already stores contract events will carry these rows.

## D5 — Performance requirements

(a) **s4:** 10 records/s sustained, 100/s for 60 seconds, 10,000 full-stream consumers, about 1.8 GB/day at the planning rate for a 2,048-byte record. **s1:** at most 4 events per block and at most 5% of `blockUsage`; a 60-second p50 gate; 56 MiB/day as an assumption. **o4:** 50 events/s and 500/s peak, assumed; relays serve at most two shards; overlay p99 of 5 seconds; contract-visible p99 of 90 seconds. **o1:** 50/s and a 250/s burst, mean wire size about 5.2 KiB because the proof is planned at 3 KiB, mobile under 15 MB/day, relay CPU 0.3 core.

(b) `blockUsage` is 1,000,000 bytes (`ledger-parameters-config.json:158`). The runtime sets a 1 MiB block length and a 75% normal ratio (`runtime/src/lib.rs:300-313`) and a 6-second slot (`:292`). s1’s cap uses those limits. The 8 KiB transaction is an inference: `DUST_SPEND_PROOF_SIZE` is 2,912 (`dust.rs:2158`) and `PROOF_SIZE` is defined as the Zswap input-proof size, with a comment that it varies and is an upper bound (`structure.rs:619` and `:1908`). Four such transactions are 3.3% of `blockUsage` only if that size holds. The indexer’s 400-block constant is a finality safety margin for hash versus height fetch (`subxt_node.rs:83-86` and `:423-429`), not a delivery delay. `subscribe_finalized_blocks` starts at line 128. MPS-0028 says finality is usually about three blocks, around 18 seconds, in a proposal (`mps-0028-pre-finality-state-visibility.md:36-38`). GossipSub v1.1 mentions “a `D` of 6” as the reference for a scoring default (line 553). It does not set the mesh degree. Beck’s 0.548 ms and Liu’s 0.065 s/message and about $1.02 per million messages (`2021-liu-omr`, lines 315-318) are those papers’ machines and parameters. UnifOMR’s about 25 seconds and 4 MB is for 2^19 messages of 612 bytes (abstract, lines 38-39).

(c) Vote **s1-privacy-theorist**. The block cap is the one budget that is an input to the chain. The overlay rates are assumptions multiplied by unmeasured amplification and, for o1, by a price weight used as a CPU time.

(d) Strongest objection: prove time for this circuit is unknown, and the 60-second gate may already fail. s1 marks D5 low confidence and names that failure as a course change. Until Phase 0 replaces the 8 KiB inference, the 56 MiB/day figure is a planning assumption at 1,024 wire bytes per event.

## D6 — Storage requirements

(a) **s4:** every relay keeps the stream 48 hours; at 100 records/s that is about 3.3 GiB raw for a 2,048-byte record; archives are separate; three receipts are an obligation, not a proof of future availability. **s1:** ciphertext is log churn, contract state stays empty, indexer retention is 14 days at an assumed 512-byte row. **o4:** store nodes keep at most 7 days, about 72.6 GB at the launch rate, at least three operators, no protocol archive. **o1:** store nodes keep 7 days by default, about 63 GiB stripped, and the ledger keeps membership and anchors with no rent.

(b) On the tag, a log increments both bytes written and bytes deleted (`vm.rs:595-604`). Genesis limits are 50,000 persistent bytes and 50,000,000 churn bytes per block (`ledger-parameters-config.json:159-160`). At equal fee factors, one persistent byte is priced like twenty transmitted bytes, because `1/50,000` over `1/1,000,000` is 20. That is the argument against an on-chain mailbox. It is arithmetic from the fee formula, not a measured rent schedule. The stack notes say no rent was found; I did not re-derive that. `global_ttl` prunes intent replay and past Zswap roots in those notes. It does not delete `Misc` rows or block bodies.

(c) Vote **s1-privacy-theorist**. The contract stores nothing. The ciphertext rides the transaction log, which the fee system already treats as churn. A store network, a 48-hour replica set, and an anchor tree are new storage products.

(d) Strongest objection: indexer retention is not deletion. s1’s “after that the event is gone” describes a policy s1 is proposing. Copies in block bodies, and any indexer that ignores the policy, remain. With no forward secrecy, a later compromise of the topic key reads every copy that was kept.

## D9 — Threats and open risks

(a) **s4:** ingress attribution, omission, and key compromise stay as residuals; Dandelion++ is refused as a global-observer defense; fuzzy detection is refused as a substitute for a full stream. **s1:** the fee market, the full stream, a second indexer, and out-of-band key rotation; Dandelion, OMR, fuzzy detection, and peer scoring are cut. **o4:** RLN, graylisting, decoy tags, franking, and tombstones; global timing and light-client interest are marked high residual. **o1:** GossipSub scoring, a Dandelion++ stem, fuzzy detection for first contact, ratchets and MLS, and the ledger fallback against censorship.

(b) Dandelion++’s own adversary section supports s4 and s1: mass rather than targeted deanonymization, and no guarantee once an ISP-level adversary eclipses a node (lines 265-277). Seres et al. support refusing fuzzy detection as equivalent privacy (lines 95-96). o1 keeps it for first contact and says the privacy is weak. Pung’s evaluation finds 4.5–36 MB per message for multi-retrieval and says full download wins for small tuples and large `k` (lines 1584-1588). Chor et al. state that perfect privacy against one server requires downloading the database (lines 20-22) and that this linear bound does not hold for computational privacy (line 221). UnifOMR’s abstract supports leaving OMR out of version one. Kumar et al. report score-function violations and an Ethereum disclosure (lines 129-157); I did not re-read the proofs. o1 cites `lib.rs:292` for predictable Aura leaders. RFC 9180 supports s1’s key-compromise residual.

(c) Vote **s1-privacy-theorist**. The defenses match the properties in D2. A stem phase, a detection server, and a scoring mesh defend threats version one has refused to claim, and each adds an attack surface the papers already bound.

(d) Strongest objection: a client of one indexer cannot tell omission from silence. s1’s defense is a second indexer, and an inclusion proof is cut until equivocation is shown. That order is right. It leaves a single-indexer user with no check.

## D10 — Build and verification plan

(a) **s4:** pin revisions, model the narrow service, measure the stated workload, require identical retrieval behavior for different interests, and try mixnets and PIR only as separate profiles. **s1:** test vectors, one-day soak, a scanner that never sends a viewing key, then stop; four triggers reopen a new design. **o4:** five phases, Quint invariants of a registry contract, Tamarin or ProVerif, legal memos, and no broadcast-anonymity claim before organic load. **o1:** about eight weeks of spikes, including an off-chain Compact RLN verifier, a 1,000-node simulation, and a fuzzy-detection simulation, then an overlay MVP.

(b) s1’s vectors test facts already in the tree: the name is constant, the payload is fixed, `publish` writes no ledger cell, and `contractEvents` requires the contract address. o1’s first spike is a public API to verify a contract proof outside a transaction, which o1 marks unknown. That is a plan to build RLN. o4’s six Quint invariants are invariants of a contract that does not exist yet. s4’s paired-client test is the one addition that falsifies interest privacy on s1’s indexer queries, and it needs no new protocol. MPS-0028’s “about 18 seconds” and the transplanted RLN, OMR, and Dandelion numbers are already being used as targets by o1 and o4. s4’s refusal to treat them as acceptance criteria matches the passages checked above.

(c) Vote **s1-privacy-theorist**. Build the convention and measure it. Stop. An overlay, an RLN circuit, a franking scheme, or a mix starts as its own design when T1 (p50 over 60 seconds on an unloaded preview, or a written requirement for sub-slot latency), a measured body need above 205 bytes at a payable rate, a break of a property D2 actually claims, or indexer storage over the stated monthly bound says the convention failed.

(d) Strongest objection: Phase 0 never requires the differential test. Two subscribers with different topic keys must produce the same `contractEvents` queries, cursors, and volumes. Without that record, topic privacy is a diagram. I would add that test, and a record of whether block bodies still contain the ciphertext after the indexer window. I would not add a governance model, a 1,000-node gossip run, or a fuzzy-detection simulation to this version.

## Where the group will split

The group is most likely to disagree on three decisions.

**D8.** Three of the four proposals build a sidecar. s1 uses the ledger and the indexer. The sidecar is a real way to get a larger body and a store that can expire. The anonymity citations attached to it, Dandelion++ in particular, describe a different adversary and a different graph.

**D4.** s1 prices publication as an ordinary DUST fee. o4 and o1 sell RLN memberships. s4 sells issuer permits for an overlay. The RLN paper’s open problem is many paid identities, and its reported verify time is 30 ms on a proof-of-concept library. The fee function is already in `cost_model.rs`.

**D2.** o1 and o4 claim a publisher-unlinkability property for version one, inside a membership set and, for o1, against a colluding relay fraction. s1 claims a chain observer sees no payer and a network observer sees the RPC. s4 refuses the property. The `DustSpend` struct supports the narrower statement. The papers cited for the wider one do not transfer to these deployments.

The fact that would settle the most of this is a measurement, on a network that actually runs ledger 9, of one `publish` of a 288-byte `Misc`: encoded transaction size, DUST fee at that network’s parameters, prove time on the proof server that would ship, and delay from submit until a second machine sees the event on `contractEvents` for a finalized block. If the size stays inside 5% of `blockUsage` and the median delay is within 60 seconds, the overlay, the RLN header, and the fuzzy clue have not earned a place in version one. If the size or the delay misses those gates, s1’s tether is out and the remaining argument is which overlay, with Dandelion++ and fuzzy detection still excluded by their own papers. The support matrix still lists mainnet on toolchain 0.31.1 and on-chain runtime 3.0.0, so the measurement has to wait for a ledger-9 network. That date is unknown. It blocks every design that calls `emit`. It does not justify operating the sidecar while waiting.
