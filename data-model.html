DATA MODEL / AN AGREEMENT BETWEEN APPLICATIONS

Agree on what
a message means.

A buyer and a dealer need to agree on what a quote means before either application can use it. Midnight Express proposes shared rules for that exchange, with reviewed translators, called adapters, connecting each participant’s existing format.

A quote needs a shared price basis; an approval needs a specific scope. The model provides common event fields and lets each workflow define the terms it needs.

01 / THE PROBLEM

A message can arrive perfectly
and still be misunderstood.

Two applications can accept the same structured message while disagreeing about the business it describes. A number such as “100” needs context: dollars, cents or shares? A status such as “approved” needs an exact action and scope.

Integration teams resolve these assumptions in documents, custom connectors and conversations with partners. Flexible formats can leave conventions implicit; a universal format can require agreement on terms the workflow never uses.

Midnight Express proposes a separate agreement for each supported workflow. A quote needs a price basis; an invoice observation needs payment evidence; an approval needs its own limits. Partners still negotiate those meanings. They can then review and test the resulting rules instead of leaving them implicit in a connector.

INTEGRATION

Make assumptions visible.

A partner can review supported terms, units and refusal conditions before connecting. A missing price basis becomes an explicit mapping issue.

AUTOMATION

Check meaning before acting.

Deterministic rules check the interpretation. Authentication and current business permission then govern whether an application may act on it.

GROWTH

Reuse the mechanism.

A new workflow can use the shared core while owning its own business terms. Adding an invoice contract does not require expanding every quote.

These are benefits to test with partners. Integration hours, manual corrections and turnaround baselines have not yet been collected.

02 / ONE QUOTE, TWO DECLARED FORMATS

How two quote formats
reach the same meaning.

A request for quote (RFQ) asks a dealer to state terms for a possible trade. Consider the same dealer’s quote for a buyer purchasing 100 shares, represented in two source formats. Currency, quantity, perspective and the no-fee convention are declared in both.

FORMAT A

$123.45

US dollars per one share.
Buyer purchases 100 shares.

FORMAT B

1,234,500 cents

US cents per 100 shares.
Dealer sells those 100 shares.

AGREED RFQ MEANING

100 shares × $123.45 = $12,345

The adapters apply the declared unit and perspective conversions, then check the price and cash total exactly. Missing price basis, a contradictory total or an unsupported fee convention is refused.

Follow the conversion.

Format B prices a block of 100 shares. Its adapter divides 1,234,500 cents by 100 to obtain 12,345 cents per share, or $123.45. It also translates the dealer’s selling perspective into the buyer’s purchasing perspective. These are declared conventions, so the receiver can check both the arithmetic and direction.

If Format B omits “per 100 shares”, the number still looks valid, but the adapter cannot know what quantity it covers. It refuses that mapping. The source owner must supply the missing convention before the quote can proceed; the adapter cannot infer it.

Two encodings have the same intent fingerprint only when the complete source-bound intent matches. Quotes from different dealers keep different source identities even when their prices agree. The checked quote is an off-chain observation. The application must check source authentication and current policy before deciding a next step; trade execution and settlement need their own authority.

How the reference keeps numbers exact

The USD price is represented as the integer coefficient 12345 with scale 2, giving 12345 × 10⁻² = 123.45. Quantity is 100 whole shares; cash is 1,234,500 cents. Money and quantities use bounded decimal strings, and ambiguous numeric forms reject.

Independent Python, Rust and TypeScript interpreters check the same RFQ cases. Canonicalization gives an agreed interpretation deterministic bytes; it cannot supply a missing currency, prove identity or turn a quote into an authorized trade.

03 / THE DESIGN CHOICE

How workflow contracts
define shared meaning.

Participants agree on the common event mechanism and choose a workflow contract with explicit terms. The reference refuses meanings that contract cannot represent. New terms require a reviewed contract.

This choice trades some flexibility for a reviewable integration agreement. Domain design and partner negotiation still matter. A pilot must show that the supported terms cover useful work and that adding a partner requires acceptable effort.

Model, schema and protocol answer different questions.

The data model describes what information means: which party is buying, how a quantity is measured and what a status claims. A schema defines the permitted fields, types and values. The workflow contract combines that structure with business rules, such as requiring cash to equal quantity multiplied by price.

A workflow contract here is a data agreement. The delivery protocol carries the message and has its own membership and security mechanisms; it cannot determine whether an unstated price basis is correct. The reference calls each bounded workflow variant a profile.

SHARED CORE

Identify the event.

Who the event names as its source, its identity, time, exact type and the contract it uses. These common fields stay inside the proposed encrypted message body.

WORKFLOW CONTRACT

Define its meaning.

Required terms, units, roles and rules. A quote declares price basis and expiry; a payment observation declares amount, evidence and status; an approval binds a specific proposal.

PARTICIPANT ADAPTER

Map the source format.

A reviewed translator for one declared convention. It preserves source meaning and refuses missing terms, unsupported fees or a mapping that loses required information.

Applications install a reviewed bundle containing the contract’s definitions and rules. The adapter translates source data into those terms, and the resulting event belongs inside the proposed encrypted message body. Adapters and bundles stay in the application. An invoice contract can therefore add evidence rules without making every quote carry invoice fields.

An exact agreement

A contract commitment is a fingerprint of the agreed definitions and rules. It identifies which meaning to use. The contract must be locally reviewed and installed; its fingerprint alone does not authenticate a sender or grant permission.

What is deliberately bounded?

The pilot quote contract uses whole shares, USD cents, an explicit buyer/seller perspective and no fees. Fractional shares, other currencies and fee structures need separately reviewed contracts. Each current contract admits only its declared fields; unknown terms cause refusal.

Domain owners can propose new profiles independently. The proposed governance rule promotes a term into the common core only after it proves useful in at least two independent profiles and its maintenance cost is understood.

04 / PILOT WORKFLOWS

What each pilot
message can establish.

Each contract checks a different business claim. Quote interpretation is compared independently in Python, Rust and TypeScript. Payment observation and sandbox approval have their own reference conformance checks; their interpretation is outside that three-language campaign.

QUOTE OBSERVATION

Compare an explicit offer.

A buyer sees quantity, currency, price per share, direction, fees and expiry. The reference refuses an inconsistent total or expired quote.

Value to test: fewer convention mismatches during quote review and integration.

Current scope: whole-share USD quotes for off-chain coordination.

PAYMENT OBSERVATION

Explain what was reported.

A $500 invoice receives an observation of a $250 payment. Amount, payment identity, source status and clocks remain explicit. Pending, Final and Reversed have distinct meanings.

Value to test: clearer reconciliation of partial payments and status changes.

Final remains a source assertion. This contract neither marks an invoice paid nor executes a payment.

SANDBOX APPROVAL

Keep approval tied to its action.

A human approval fixture binds one report-writing proposal to its target, input, execution scope, budget and expiry. Changing the target or replaying a consumed action cannot renew it.

Value to test: reviewable limits and recoverable progress for agent workflows.

Validation produces a candidate. A separately authorized test host writes one database report row.

The validator returns executes:false. Production human identity, source authentication and current permission require their own checks. Cross-language agreement currently covers RFQ; it does not establish independent interpretation of every workflow.

From a message to permitted work.

Before performing work, a receiving application needs to interpret the message, establish trust in its source, check current permission and preserve the outcome. Each decision answers a different question.

  1. Understand the message. Select the installed contract and check its required terms, units and rules. A missing price basis or conflicting amount stops here.
  2. Establish the source and evidence. A source name says whom the message claims to represent. Authentication and supporting evidence must establish whether that claim is trusted. A matching fingerprint identifies content, so source authentication remains a separate check.
  3. Check permission now. The proposed work must still fit its target, scope, policy, budget and expiry. An approval may expire or be revoked after it arrives.
  4. Preserve the outcome. At the effect boundary, record the permitted change and progress needed for recovery. The local sandbox demonstrates one report-row write; remote destinations need their own recovery agreement.

The current release is runnable local reference software with synthetic source and authority fixtures. Production identity, permission services and the complete private protocol remain further work.

Walk through a payment observation and its three clocks

Effective time: when the source says the payment took effect. Observed time: when it was seen. Record time: when this observation event was created.

For example, a source says a payment took effect at 10:00, an observer sees it at 10:05 and records the observation at 10:06. If the record instead claimed to precede the observation, the reference would refuse the inconsistent timeline.

The reference requires effective time ≤ observed time ≤ record time ≤ trusted current time, and a positive amount no greater than the invoice payable for every status. Matching fixture evidence supports the observation; accounting allocation and bank-finality verification remain separate.

Pending, Final and Reversed record different source claims. Reversed is a reported status, not an instruction to send money back. The supplied status fixtures use separate payment identities; they do not demonstrate one payment progressing through a complete lifecycle.

05 / RECOVERY & CHANGE

A retry must not become
a second action.

Delivery can repeat. Applications still need to know which occurrence they saw, which work was already consumed and which version of meaning governed the decision.

Keep identities distinct.

An event identity answers “Have I seen this occurrence?” A scoped action identity answers “Has this work been consumed?” A new event or transport envelope cannot make an old approval into new authorized work.

For chain data, a physical transfer location distinguishes one fact from repeated observer deliveries. Re-inclusion on another branch receives a new location; invalidations preserve the earlier history. The journal can show what an observer knew at an earlier cutoff, while gaps remain explicit.

Preserve the original meaning.

Meaning-changing edits produce a new immutable contract. Historical events keep their original commitment, so replay does not silently adopt today’s rules.

Business meaning and software releases have separate identities. An implementation fix that preserves the agreement need not redefine its terms. The offline reference can distinguish current and historical bundles. v0.1 is preserved read-only. Authenticated distribution and live workflow migration still need a deployment process.

Why an exact contract matters beyond a version label

Two applications saying “v0.2” is not enough if they installed different definitions. The contract fingerprint identifies the exact reviewed rules. The offline reference checks it against installed bundles; it refuses an unknown contract instead of choosing a similar version or downloading a sender’s definition.

A software fix can preserve the same business agreement. Changing a unit, role or status meaning creates a new agreement. Historical messages remain tied to their original contract, and historical interpretation does not grant permission for a new effect. Live migration requires a reviewed deployment decision.

Recovery has an effect boundary

The Umbra/PostgreSQL sandbox commits a report row and its processing progress in one transaction. A lost destination reply remains uncertain until reconciliation by the original key. Real external effects require the actual destination’s idempotency and reconciliation contract.

What happens after a lost reply?

Suppose the sandbox worker commits its report row and then crashes before acknowledging completion. On restart, durable records identify the consumed action, so a retry retains one local report write. If it crashed before committing, the incomplete transaction cannot leave progress claiming the effect succeeded.

A separate destination creates another boundary. If it commits a request but its reply is lost, the sender cannot assume failure. It preserves the uncertain outcome and reconciles using the original idempotency key—the stable identifier the destination uses to recognize repeated work—and matching request evidence. The test uses a separate-transaction acknowledgement fixture; a live destination must supply its own verified duplicate and reconciliation behavior.

How this fits the Midnight Express stack
  1. Participant adapterTranslate a declared source convention.
  2. Workflow contractCheck exact shape and business meaning.
  3. Private deliveryCarry the agreed event inside the proposed encrypted MPE body.
  4. Application policy & recoveryAuthenticate, authorize and preserve progress at the effect boundary.

GossipSub supplies the proposed transport; membership/admission and group security have separate protocol roles. They cannot decide a price basis or payment meaning. The data model handles interpretation; the application retains business authority. Live signing, sealing, admission and transport integration remain protocol work.

06 / ETHEREUM & SOLANA APPLICATIONS

Chain evidence keeps
its own meaning.

The proposed vocabulary inventories 291 common messages and events: queries, subscriptions, transaction outcomes, state, tokens, wallet permissions and application-specific activity. It guides future contracts; each chain keeps its native units and evidence rules.

An RPC request asks a chain node for information or a service. A projection extracts a bounded record from supplied evidence instead of treating an entire response as one business fact. The current transfer projections record amounts, source context and physical locations. The broader vocabulary maps work still to define and validate.

Ethereum

Requests and RPC results, blocks and reorgs, receipts and logs, balances and contract state, token/NFT transfers and approvals, wallet signing and network changes.

Safe and finalized remain Ethereum-specific source assertions. Removed logs can invalidate observations. Contract and decoder identity explain what a decoded event claims.

Implemented read-only slice: bounded transfer projections for the ERC-20 token standard from supplied receipt/log fixtures, with exact raw amounts and physical inclusion identity.

Solana

Requests and RPC results, slots and roots, account/program updates, signatures and errors, instructions and inner calls, SOL/SPL balances, wallet signing and program-specific activity.

Processed, confirmed and finalized retain Solana’s meanings. An inner program call needs instruction-level success evidence; transaction success alone can hide a caught inner failure.

Implemented read-only slice: legacy Solana Program Library (SPL) Token Program TransferChecked projections from supplied fixtures. Token-2022 is outside this slice.

291 entries are proposed vocabulary, not 291 deployed adapters or executable contracts. Current projections use synthetic source/context fixtures. Live authenticated RPC, deployed program bindings and independently verified finality remain promotion gates.

Follow one transfer through changing evidence.

Polling and a subscription can both report the same transfer. Those are two observer deliveries of one physical fact. If its block is later invalidated, the journal preserves the earlier observation and adds the invalidation. If the transfer appears at another physical location, that new inclusion has its own identity.

This history lets an application ask what it knew at an earlier cutoff and which evidence supports its current view. The journal exposes missing coverage and conflicting assertions instead of reporting a total from incomplete evidence. The fixture tests exercise this history. Complete chain coverage and consensus finality still require independent evidence.

Why a token transfer has a different quantity contract

Chain amounts preserve zero-inclusive uint256/u64 raw units and token-specific decimals. They do not reuse the quote pilot’s positive-only whole-share and USD primitives. Large RPC responses need bounded projections and explicitly authorized evidence retrieval.

Raw observations, decoded program claims and derived business conclusions retain separate provenance. A bridge initiation is not destination completion; a source’s “final” label is not a consensus proof or permission to execute.

08 / FROM A PARTNER FORMAT TO AN AGREED CONTRACT

Prepare one partner workflow
for a contract review.

Begin with representative messages from one useful partner workflow, including examples that should be refused. Ask the source owner to declare units, buyer/seller perspective, status meanings, identifiers and timing. Compare those conventions with the contract before building an adapter; a missing term is a question to resolve together.

The pilot should answer practical questions: which cases map without losing meaning, which need a new contract, and how much effort a second participant requires. Refusal rates can expose a contract that is too narrow or source data that needs clarification. Measure that tradeoff alongside manual corrections and review time.

  1. Choose a bounded contract. Identify the terms, roles, units and status transitions your workflow needs.
  2. Declare the source conventions. Build and review a deterministic adapter. Keep source evidence and record transformations.
  3. Test agreement and refusal. Check valid cases, missing terms, replay and unsupported versions against the exact installed contract.
  4. Integrate authority and recovery. Add authenticated sources, current permission and the destination’s effect/reconciliation rules. Measure real integration effort.

Keep a record a partner can review.

For the proposed pilot, keep three lists: fields copied unchanged, fields produced by a declared conversion, and messages refused. For each refusal, identify the missing or contradictory term and whether the remedy is a source correction, an adapter declaration or a new contract.

Record time spent clarifying conventions, reviewing mappings and handling exceptions alongside the partner’s existing process. That record helps decide whether the supported terms cover useful work and whether another source or profile is worth adding. The synthetic corpus supplies examples for that exercise. Partner data and a measured baseline are needed to judge savings.

Do partners have to replace their data models?

Partners can keep their internal formats behind explicit adapters. They must supply every required business term and use a supported contract. A source convention that cannot be represented safely needs a new agreement.

Can AI interpret a format we have not seen?

AI can draft a proposed mapping for review. Actionable messages depend on deterministic, reviewed adapters and exact rules; an inferred currency, role or permission cannot authorize work.

Does valid data mean an action is approved?

Validation establishes a checked interpretation. Source identity, current authority, evidence quality, freshness and replay policy still govern action. The reference validator itself never executes.

How do we add another workflow?

Define a separately owned contract, preserve its version and test its adapters and business rules. The proposed governance rule permits shared terms only where workflows agree on their meaning. A meaning change gets a new commitment and a reviewed migration decision.

A short guide to the terms
Adapter
A reviewed translator for one declared application format.
Workflow contract / profile
The precise structure, definitions and rules for one bounded workflow.
Contract bundle
The definitions and rules installed together for an exact workflow contract.
Commitment
A fingerprint identifying exact content or an agreement. It identifies what is being compared; it does not authenticate who supplied it.
Canonicalization
Producing deterministic bytes for the same declared interpretation, so independent implementations can compare it.
Replay
Processing previously seen information again after a retry, reconnect or recovery.
Effect
The actual change an application makes. In the demonstrated sandbox, one database report-row write.
Projection
A bounded record extracted from supplied evidence, retaining its source and interpretation context.
Fixture
A supplied test example or trusted test context, rather than a live authenticated source.
