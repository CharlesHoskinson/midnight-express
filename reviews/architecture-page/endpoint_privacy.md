# Endpoint privacy and session security review

Sources reviewed: `recommended-stack-and-use-cases.md`, `gossipsub-signal-option.md`, and architecture `dist/index.html`. The referenced `dist/app.js` was absent when reviewed, so interactive component and journey text could not be assessed.

The page correctly separates encrypted coordination from contract authority, labels OpenMLS optional, preserves whole-shard local recognition, and identifies Signal as an alternative. Tighten these points:

1. **State the privacy boundary beside the endpoint principle.** Suggested copy: “Source, type, correlation and role information stay inside authenticated encryption. Receivers take the whole shard and recognize relevant events locally. Connections, shard participation, timing, traffic volume and envelope size classes can remain observable.” Do not imply that hidden business semantics mean hidden traffic metadata or ingress anonymity.
2. **Make the recognition invariant explicit.** Suggested copy: “Recognition does not automatically send acknowledgements, narrow upstream selectors, trigger confirmation queries or fetch referenced blobs. Pubsub control traffic follows opaque byte receipt and inventory.” Explicit signed business acknowledgements remain application actions governed by policy and the selected privacy profile; they are not automatic recognition receipts.
3. **Name launch-profile limits.** The initial coordination pilot retains the original symmetric profile. It does not claim forward secrecy or ingress anonymity. A compromised stable outer/recognition secret can expose matching interests for historical and future messages in that generation even when an inner session ratchets.
4. **Position OpenMLS as a selected future extension, not pilot infrastructure.** Suggested node detail: “OpenMLS is the selected session/group extension; the first coordination pilot uses the original symmetric profile. Deployment awaits credential/identity, metadata, wire-fit, epoch/rekey, crash/recovery and key-erasure tests.” Session encryption sits inside the MPE composition at sending and receiving endpoints, preserves local recognition, and supplies neither admission nor business authority. Removing members from future epochs does not delete already received plaintext.
5. **Expand the extension gate slightly.** Current “identity, epoch and recovery tests” omits metadata, byte fit, rekey and erasure. Suggested roadmap copy: “OpenMLS needs identity, metadata, wire-fit, rekey and crash/erasure validation.”
6. **Keep Signal as an evaluated alternative.** The deliberate-choice text is sound; add that Signal is not a dependency alongside OpenMLS. If exposed in a detail panel: the proposed one-device pairwise experiment nests serialized Signal ciphertext inside existing MPE sealing; it is not implemented or interoperable with the Signal app. Pin the exact suite/library before claiming forward secrecy, compromise recovery or post-quantum properties. Inner ratcheting does not heal compromised outer-recognition metadata. Nested contract consumption additionally needs proof that the claimed instruction belongs to the anchored ciphertext.
7. **Preserve mobile and recovery boundaries.** Whole-shard/private reception does not automatically extend to push/webhooks or per-recipient retrieval. Private mobile discovery/retrieval remains a separate PIR/OMR experiment. Retained ciphertext alone does not guarantee decryptable history; key lifecycle, skipped-key limits and backups matter.

No broad rewrite is needed: add a compact privacy boundary note, one no-automatic-recognition-ack statement, and fuller OpenMLS acceptance text near the existing map/roadmap.
