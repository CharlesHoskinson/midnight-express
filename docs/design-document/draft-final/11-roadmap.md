# Open questions, roadmap and risks {#ch11}

The proof of concept is the next step, and it has not been built. It will be one Rust implementation of the system specified in Chapter 8. Its first milestone will deliver a deterministic simulator and a runnable harness (Chapter 9). Exploratory runs helped shape the design and the choice of what the proof of concept should be. Chapter 10 says what they suggested, and nothing in this chapter rests on them. Even if the proof of concept passes every gate of Chapter 9, it will show only that the pipeline of Chapter 8 works at the prototype parameters of Chapter 7 on the harness's machines. It will not show that the pipeline works across a wide-area network, with a real Admission Proof, on a real Midnight ledger, or with independent Bus Operators who are paid by someone. The ledger interface has also been written in Compact as exploratory code, compiled and costed: the Bus Registry, the ledger-lane contract and a consuming contract. None of it has run on a Midnight network, and the design does not depend on those contracts being final.

Their fees are therefore derived from the ledger's cost model, not measured. A derived fee applies the ledger's published pricing rules to the compiled circuits. A measured fee is what a network charges for a transaction that has actually been built, proved and submitted.

## The next experiments

Twenty experiments follow from the specification of Chapter 8, the open decisions of the requirement set and the compiled ledger interface. Each closes an open decision, tests a choice that the exploration prompted but did not establish, or checks the design against Midnight's own event standards. They are to start in the order listed. Experiments 1, 3 to 8, 11, 13 and 18 will run on the deterministic simulator and the harness from the proof of concept's first milestone. Experiment 2 also needs phone hardware. Experiment 9 needs one host per Bus Node and a measured verifier, and experiment 10 needs a 48-hour soak. Experiments 12 and 14 to 17 need a ledger-9 network or a proof system that generates real proofs. Experiment 19 cannot start until the Foundation publishes its specification of private events. The last experiment is a method rule that binds every gated result.

Each expected result is a hypothesis, so any experiment can fail, and the "Settles" column names what changes when one does. Experiments 2, 3, 5 to 14 and 16 settle the open decisions that their `DEC-` identifiers name. IHAVE, IWANT and IDONTWANT are the GossipSub control messages that advertise, request and decline a message. A circuit of size k has room for 2^k rows.

In experiment 1, Bus Nodes publish under StrictNoSign. This GossipSub signature policy sends no author field (`from`, `seqno`, `signature`, or `key`, the author's public key) and rejects any message that carries one, even `key` alone. An author field would name the Bus Node that first published the message while authenticating no Publisher, since Publisher authentication sits inside the sealed body. The 0.51.0 source rejects a `key`-only message in its anonymous validation mode.^[libp2p/rust-libp2p, protocols/gossipsub/src/protocol.rs, `impl Decoder for GossipsubCodec`, branch `ValidationMode::Anonymous` returning `ValidationError::KeyPresent`; libp2p-gossipsub 0.51.0, master branch, accessed 2 October 2026.]

For experiment 2, a Subscriber that holds 256 keys and watches a Shard carrying 100 MPE Envelopes per second tests 25,600 pairs of key and MPE Envelope every second. With the Recognition Tag, each test recomputes a 16-byte HMAC-SHA-256 value, which Chapter 8 counts at three SHA-256 compressions once the key's pad states are precomputed. That gives 76,800 compressions per second (25,600 × 3). Trial decryption instead tries to open every MPE Envelope under every key. Under the 5% threshold, each attempt may cost at most 0.05 s ÷ 25,600 ≈ 1.95 µs of one core. Experiment 2 measures both costs on the two hardware classes and compares them with that budget.

In experiments 7 and 8, each Bus Node scores every peer in two parts. One part is the per-topic values (one per Shard), summed and capped at 32 in the Midnight Express score profile. The other is an application score that the Bus Node sets itself, with a weight of 1. At every heartbeat the Bus Node prunes from all its meshes any peer whose total is negative [@2020-gossipsub-v11-spec]. P3 penalizes a mesh peer that delivers too few messages on a topic, so on a Shard with no traffic every honest peer falls short. P4 penalizes invalid messages and sits inside the capped sum. Rewards on seven Shards (up to 210) can therefore absorb a penalty on the eighth and leave the total at 32. A relay is a Bus Node whose identity is on the Bus Registry relay list, and while the allow-list is active only relays join meshes. A relay removed from that list keeps its topic score, so removal needs its own enforcement. The application score of −33 brings even a relay at the cap to 32 − 33 = −1, so the relay is pruned at the next heartbeat, and the Bus Node also refuses its GRAFT and prunes it directly rather than relying on the score alone.

Experiment 14 tests the ledger lane. On the lane, a Message is split into parts, one `Misc` event per part, each carrying 256 B of payload. With its 32-byte name, a part has 288 B of struct data. Classes 0, 1 and 2 take 1, 4 and 16 parts. Two cost dimensions matter, and each block has a limit on both: `block_usage`, the bytes a transaction occupies in a block, and `bytes_written`, the bytes it writes to ledger storage.^[midnightntwrk/midnight-ledger, spec/cost-model.md, struct `SyntheticCost`; main branch, accessed 2 October 2026.]

Block usage is the first limit. `block_usage` is 1,000,000 B in the Midnight node's network configuration files and 200,000 B in the ledger's initial limits, and which of them is live is unmeasured. Ten percent of 1,000,000 B is 100,000 B per block. The derived ledger-lane transactions are about 8,330, 9,248 and 12,920 B for classes 0 to 2 (Chapter 10, with 4,368 B contract proofs as an upper bound). Class 3 cannot use the lane, so the 5% of the baseline mix in class 3 is removed and the rest renormalised: (0.60 × 8,330 + 0.25 × 9,248 + 0.10 × 12,920) / 0.95 ≈ 9,055 B of block usage per Message. That gives about 11.0 Messages per block (100,000 / 9,055), so a 1,000-Message burst needs about 91 blocks, or about 9 minutes at 6 s per block. At 200,000 B a 10% share is 20,000 B, about 2.2 Messages per block, and the same burst takes about 453 blocks: about 45 minutes, five times as long.

The second limit is `bytes_written`, 50,000 B per block. MIP-0002 sizes event capacity against it at an event's serialized size (MIP-0002's reading). Each part costs its 288 bytes of struct data plus about 40 bytes of overhead, about 328 B, so a class-1 Message (4 parts) costs about 1,312 B and a class-2 Message (16 parts) about 5,248 B [@midnight-mip-0002]. The ledger-9 cost model counts each logged byte twice, once as written and once as deleted. An event therefore adds nothing to net written bytes, the storage a transaction leaves behind. It shows up only as churn (bytes written and deleted again), which the derived fee prices apart from the 50,000 B limit. A 16-part call shows 224 B of net written bytes against 26,931 B of churn. The churn exceeds the events' 5,248 B because it covers everything the call writes and then deletes. The block limit applies to net written bytes, so under the code an event adds nothing to `bytes_written` and block usage binds (the code's reading). Which reading governs a live ledger-9 network is unmeasured (`MPE-PRF-040`).

Under MIP-0002's reading, with no other writers, a block holds 38 class-1 Messages (38 × 1,312 B = 49,856 B) or 9 class-2 Messages (9 × 5,248 B = 47,232 B). The whole chain then carries about 6.3 class-1 Messages per second under MIP-0002's `bytes_written` sizing (3.5 if `block_usage` is 200,000 B) and about 1.5 class-2 Messages per second. A 10% share (P-ECO-8, `MPE-ECO-011`) is a tenth of that: about 0.63 class-1 or 0.15 class-2 Messages per second, or about 3.8 and 0.9 per block. A 1,000-Message burst of class 1 then needs about 263 blocks (26 minutes), and one of class 2 about 1,111 blocks (111 minutes). Class 2 would reach 0.15 Messages per second against the 1 per second that the pass condition of experiment 14 requires.

Proving cost rises with the number of parts. One part needs 166,241 rows for class 0, within the 262,144 of k = 18. Four parts in one call need k = 20 (663,950 rows). Sixteen parts need 2,651,779 rows, which exceeds 2^21 = 2,097,152 and so needs k = 22. Proving time at these sizes has not been measured. For fees the order reverses: sixteen separate part calls cost about 0.866 DUST, 4.6 times one 16-part call at 0.188 DUST (derived at genesis prices with 4,368 B for every contract proof, an upper bound; with the modelled 2,800 B ledger-lane proofs the sixteen calls cost about 0.62 DUST, 3.3 times one call). The shape that is cheaper in fees is the one that is harder to prove.

A 16-part call also exceeds the guaranteed-segment budget, the largest modelled cost a transcript may have and still run in the guaranteed segment. A Midnight transaction has one guaranteed segment, which must succeed for the transaction to succeed. It also has fallible segments, which can fail while the guaranteed segment stays applied.^[midnightntwrk/midnight-architecture, components/WalletEngine/Specification.md, description of guaranteed and fallible sections; main branch, accessed 2 October 2026.] The whole transcript, events included, therefore lands in the fallible segment. MIP-0019 builds a package from the parts of one intent, the sealed unit of a transaction that groups its calls, provided that all of them run in one phase, as they do here. A reader must still count the parts only if that segment succeeded, or it would rebuild a Message from a call the ledger did not apply [@midnight-mip-0019].

If the measured figures are far worse than these derived ones, the ledger lane cannot carry a real burst, and its role in Chapter 6 has to shrink.

Experiment 20 follows from the threats to validity of Chapter 9. A seed fixes a run's random choices, such as which Bus Nodes connect. In the deterministic simulator the same seed will replay the same run exactly, so a difference between two runs points to the code or the configuration. In the harness, real swarms will share a host's scheduler and clock, so one seed can still give different timings, and five seeds show how far a figure moves by chance alone. A 60-second run cannot show eviction, which needs 90 loaded heartbeats (at least 90 s at the 1 s heartbeat, longer than the whole run). Nor can it show seen-set growth (the identifiers that a Bus Node keeps until expiry so that it can drop copies) or store pruning. Local swarms run real library code but reproduce neither deployment latency, bandwidth and address diversity nor real failure behaviour. Published GossipSub latency figures describe their own experiments [@2020-vyzovitis-gossipsub] and are not guarantees. Until each gated result rests on isolated runs, five seeds and run lengths that reach the time-based rules, a pass is a hypothesis, not a result.

Four kinds of evidence lie beyond any simulator, and each blocks a decision.

The first is a measured Admission Proof. The stand-in in the proof of concept will cost a synthetic 4.5 ms and will reveal member secrets to every Bus Node. `DEC-007` needs an encoded Admission Slot of at most 4,096 B, that is `roundUp64(104 + proof length) ≤ 4,096` (a proof of at most 3,992 B), and verification of at most 10 ms on one core of a 4-vCPU machine (`MPE-ECO-048`), shown by a working off-chain verifier. The Admission Slot width follows from that number, and with it the class table of `DEC-013`. Every MPE Envelope carries the slot at full width. With a 512 B slot, a class-0 MPE Envelope is 776 B on the wire for 86 B of payload; a 1,024 B slot would make it 1,288 B for the same 86 B. The proof system must also state whether it runs locally on the client hardware classes, because an admission secret must never reach an unattested third-party prover, which receives the private witness [@midnight-mps-0004]. Experiment 9 tests two candidates: RLN (Rate-Limiting Nullifier) and Groth16, a proof system with fixed-size proofs.

RLN also produces the evidence that experiment 13 spreads. A member's secret, with the genesis hash, the Bus Registry address, the admission window, class and credit, fixes a line whose value at zero is the secret. Each MPE Envelope reveals one point on that line, a share, placed by its MPE Envelope Identifier. One share reveals nothing. Two shares under one admission nullifier fix the line and expose the secret, and that pair is the equivocation evidence. Version 1 fixes these inputs, and the admission proof profile fixes one field and one hash into it that Midnight documents as stable (`MPE-ECO-057`), used alike by the Admission Proof and by the revocation circuit. The compiled revocation circuit's `transientHash`, which Midnight does not promise to keep stable across upgrades, is an exploratory placeholder.

The second is a set of measurements on a ledger-9 network. The three contracts compile with Compact 0.35.0 (language 0.27.0) against a ledger-9 release candidate, and the compiler's circuit model gives their sizes. In that model every proof is at most 4,368 B and every verifying key at most 2,119 B. The derived cost of one registration is about 8.7 KB and 0.246 DUST (0.308 DUST with the wallet's fee margin), inside the gate of 16 KiB and 0.5 DUST. For one Anchor post the derived figure is 0.195 DUST (0.244 with margin), or about 280 DUST per day at one Anchor per 60 s (1,440 × 0.195). The 0.195 DUST per Anchor assumes no net state growth; each retained Anchor-tree root adds about 32 B, about 0.006 DUST per Anchor or about 9 DUST per day (derived: 32 × 0.0002; 1,440 × 0.0064), until a reset rule bounds the tree. Each Anchor needs a k = 18 proof (152,138 rows) every minute. None of these figures has been measured on a network. `MPE-VER-030` requires the serialized size, cost dimensions, DUST charge and proving, inclusion, finality and indexing times of each operation on an integration network. `DEC-024` needs the reachable Anchor-state bytes, and `DEC-022` needs the signature-based consumer of experiment 16, which has not been written.

The third is a first-spy run. A first-spy estimator guesses that the publisher is the peer from which a spying Bus Node first received the MPE Envelope. `DEC-023` keeps the publish-ingress design only if a coalition of 1 to 20% of Bus Nodes guesses publishers less precisely than it can under direct publication. Whatever the result, the ingress itself sees the submitting connection, so the run bounds only what other Bus Nodes can estimate. Until that run exists, the design makes no claim about publisher network-level anonymity.

The fourth is a reproduction of CVE-2022-47547 against the pinned GossipSub release. The score-manipulation attack was found by formal analysis of GossipSub's scoring [@2023-kumar-gossipsub-acl2s]. `MPE-VER-026` requires the attack traces, and the cross-Shard reward and penalty cases of experiment 7, to be run and seen to fail before any Bus Node peers outside a test network. Until then it is unknown whether the pinned release and the complete score profile close the attack. A changelog entry does not count.

## Stages from prototype to production

The plan has five stages. A stage starts only when every exit gate of the stage before it has passed with published evidence (`MPE-OPS-042`). The gates are measurements, not dates, because a stage opened on a date could extend unchecked claims to more Bus Operators and users. The numbering follows the operations area (`DEC-028`), and the proof of concept belongs to Stage 0.

Two features of the plan follow from the state of Midnight, not from Midnight Express. The first is that contract events, the Indexer's contract event subscription and cross-contract calls belong to the ledger-9 generation. A ledger generation is the set of ledger rules that a network enforces, and a network moves to a new one through a runtime upgrade. As of 4 August 2026, mainnet ran ledger 8.0 while the preview network had moved to ledger 9.1 [@midnight-coip-0005]. MIP-0002, the only Accepted event document, is live on Stagenet [@midnight-mip-0018], a shared pre-release network like the preview network; a devnet is one that a team runs for itself. The Midnight node code pins ledger 9, but the published documentation lists the public networks on the earlier generation.^[midnightntwrk/midnight-node, Cargo.toml (ledger dependency) and runtime/src/lib.rs (ledger 8 to 9 migrations); midnightntwrk/midnight-docs, docs/relnotes/support-matrix.json; main branches, accessed 1 October 2026.] The newest Compact toolchain, 0.35.0, targets ledger 9 and states that ledger 9 is not yet deployed on mainnet.^[LFDT-Minokawa/compact, doc/release-notes/toolchain-0.35.0.md; main branch, accessed 1 October 2026.]

With the ledger-8 toolchain 0.31.1, the Bus Registry compiles once its two `emit` statements are removed, so Anchors and governance events are its only ledger-9 dependency. The consumer does not compile at all, because the ledger-8 cross-contract model needs the callee's compiled contract description. Whether mainnet has moved since is unknown. The runtime `spec_version` that a mainnet node reports, recorded under `MPE-OPS-043`, answers this. While the target network runs the earlier ledger, Midnight Express makes no production claim for a feature that needs ledger-9 contract events or cross-contract calls (`MPE-OPS-044`), and Stage 1 runs against a devnet.

The second feature is the audit gate. PyBitmessage 0.6.2 shipped a remote code execution flaw in its handling of malformed objects [@2018-bitmessage-wiki-main-rce-notice]. A Bus Node parses bytes chosen by strangers before any proof check or score, so anyone who can send a single MPE Envelope can reach a flaw in its parser. An independent parser audit therefore comes before the overlay opens.

No stage makes the ledger lane the main carrier. The lane stays a labelled last resort that never switches on automatically (`DEC-008`), because sealed bodies in public transaction history outlive every key that protects them. Its derived limits point the same way: MIP-0002's `bytes_written` sizing allows the whole chain about 6.3 class-1 Messages per second (3.5 if `block_usage` is 200,000 B), and every lane transaction needs a proof of k = 18 to k = 22.

## Bus Operator and governance questions

No experiment can settle the questions of organization, money, law and standards, and each can block a stage gate.

The first is who runs the relays, and how independent they are. The launch default is a permissioned allow-list of at least 8 organizations, with none above 20% of listed relays, and every diversity metric counted per verified organization, not per peer identity (`MPE-OPS-011`). No protocol mechanism can prove that two organizations are independent. Without a trusted party to vouch for identities, one entity can present many [@2002-douceur-sybil], so the gate rests on attestations that can be false. A procurement exercise has to show that 8 organizations will sign before Stage 2 is planned against that number.

The second is who pays. Midnight has no fee or reward stream that could pay a relay. DUST is shielded and non-transferable, and the Midnight node's block-reward hook returns zero with no beneficiary in both feature configurations.^[midnightntwrk/midnight-node, runtime/src/lib.rs, `impl Get<BlockReward> for LedgerBlockReward` returning `(0, None)`; main branch, accessed 1 October 2026.] DUST cannot be paid to a contract either, so a contract can neither require nor observe a registration price. A membership therefore costs only the network fee of its registration transaction, about 0.25 DUST at genesis prices. A price set by the Bus Registry would need NIGHT or another token, and so would any bond. Such a bond would be a Bus Registry contract balance, unrelated to NIGHT staking, which locks nothing and carries no consensus weight [@midnight-mip-0016].

The decision register sets relays as unpaid by the protocol (`DEC-001`). Store Nodes, anchorers and monitors are funded by written off-protocol commitments. An anchorer's commitment has to cover about 280 DUST per day at genesis prices, generated from about 393 NIGHT that the anchorer holds (derived: 1,440 × 0.195 = 280.8 DUST per day; 280.8 / 0.714 DUST per NIGHT per day = 393), assuming generation runs at the full rate while the balance stays below the cap. Payment that rises with a relay's monitoring probe scores is rejected. Attacks on Nym's performance scoring cut the cost of dominating the paid active set by over 99% compared with a stake-only attack [@2026-cao-nymreputation]. A treasury that pays only for work a contract can check, such as Anchors and passed retention challenges, stays an optional later feature. Feed and repair service fall outside the mesh-egress rule and need a budget of their own (Chapter 10), and Q4 of Chapter 9 measures them apart from mesh traffic. If they load the busiest Bus Node, the open question is whether read-heavy Bus Operators will serve feeds without pay once grants end.

The third is who governs the Bus Registry, and what limits them. The default is a steward set of 7 seats, with 5 of 7 for normal actions and 3 of 7 for emergencies, and at most 2 seats per organization (`DEC-019`). Midnight transactions have no signer, so steward and anchorer authority is the knowledge of a secret whose hash the Bus Registry holds, proved in the circuit. An approved parameter change waits at least 7 days, and never less than the maximum MPE Envelope lifetime, so that Bus Operators and users can see it and act before it applies. The exploratory Bus Registry contract implements this time-lock itself. A proposal stores its activation time, and anyone can activate it once the block time has passed. A circuit cannot obtain the block time, however; it can only test the block time against a value that the transaction supplies. Every time-dependent write therefore carries a caller-supplied time T and succeeds only if the block time lies within a tolerance of T (900 s in the exploratory contract), and the contract then records T. That suffices for the time-lock. It does not suffice for the 3,600 s acceptance window for membership roots, the time for which a superseded root is still accepted, because the contract cannot record when a root was superseded. Bus Nodes therefore enforce that window.

An emergency action expires after 7 days unless it is ratified, which copies the bound on Midnight's own forced safe mode. Midnight's federated motions run for 5 days, which is too slow to eject a relay that is injecting malformed objects.^[midnightntwrk/midnight-node, runtime/src/lib.rs, constants `SafeModeForceDuration` (7 days) and `MOTION_DURATION` (5 days); main branch, accessed 1 October 2026.] Every governance act emits one `mip-xxxx:governance[v1]` `Misc` event (`MPE-OPS-018`). No operation can revoke a Publisher credential without misuse evidence checked by the Bus Registry circuit (`MPE-OPS-015`). In the compiled contract, that evidence is two shares under one admission window, class and credit that lie on the member's line with distinct MPE Envelope Identifiers, plus the member's Merkle path. Revocation makes the leaf index and identity commitment public, and no steward takes part.

One unknown can undo these limits. A contract maintenance update on Midnight can replace the contract's authority and insert or remove verifier keys.^[midnightntwrk/midnight-ledger, ledger/src/structure.rs, `SingleUpdate::ReplaceAuthority`, `SingleUpdate::VerifierKeyInsert`, `SingleUpdate::VerifierKeyRemove`; ledger-9 tag, accessed 1 October 2026.] If the ledger cannot enforce a delay on such an update, a maintenance transaction can restore the powers withheld from the steward set, such as revoking without misuse evidence or skipping the time-lock. The only guard left would then be the 14-day public notice of `MPE-OPS-016`. `MPE-OPS-017` requires this to be tested on a ledger-9 devnet before Stage 2, which makes it a Stage 1 exit gate. If the delay cannot be enforced, immutable contracts with no governance role become the stronger option, at the cost of losing the emergency path.

The fourth question is takedown and liability. Every Store Node holds the same ciphertext, so a court order could reach all Bus Operators at once, and none of them could show compliance. The default is an optional tombstone, an authenticated request to delete a named MPE Envelope, which binds only Bus Operators who signed the Bus Operator Agreement. It must be acted on within 24 hours, and every tombstone is published (`DEC-018`). A tombstone cannot remove copies that an adversary keeps, and it cannot remove ciphertext already carried on the ledger lane: encryption hides a lane payload's plaintext but neither hides its metadata nor retracts what was disclosed [@midnight-mip-0019].

Whether relaying ciphertext is protected in a given jurisdiction is unknown. `MPE-OPS-050` requires a counsel memo before a Bus Operator in that jurisdiction is admitted, but it cannot require the memo to say yes.

For monitoring, the default is canaries run by at least 3 independent monitor organizations, plus aggregated relay counters released with differential privacy at ε = 0.3, the setting used for privacy-preserving measurement of Tor [@2018-mani-tor-usage-privacy-preserving]. Differential privacy adds calibrated noise to each released count, so that the release changes little whether or not any one user's traffic is included; a smaller ε means more noise. Payouts are not tied to these probes until a framing analysis shows that one malicious monitor cannot get an honest relay ejected.

Midnight governance can put the chain into safe mode, which filters user transactions, so Bus Registry registrations and Anchors stop.^[midnightntwrk/midnight-node, runtime/src/check_call_filter.rs and runtime/src/lib.rs, `BaseCallFilter = InsideBoth<SafeMode, TxPause>`; main branch, accessed 1 October 2026.] The overlay keeps forwarding MPE Envelopes admitted under cached roots for up to 25 hours (Chapter 8; `MPE-NET-038`). That figure follows from the 3,600 s window, which starts only when a newer root replaces the current one. No root arrives during a pause, so the cached root lasts for its 24-hour membership period plus 3,600 s. The overlay cannot outlast a longer pause, and no requirement can remove this residual. The Bus Registry's own pause is separate: in the compiled contract it blocks registration and Anchor posts while revocation and pruning stay open.

The last question is where Midnight Express stands among Midnight's standards. Midnight Express is specified as MIP-xxxx, and the problem it answers is stated as MPS-xxxx. MPS-0043 leaves "choosing how the data travels" for confidential data to a pending problem statement, Confidential Contract Notifications (PR #271), whose text is not available [@midnight-mps-0043]. The Foundation's editors must decide whether MPS-xxxx is that statement, a replacement for it or a companion to it. The answer decides which document owns the transport requirements, so it is needed before the MIP leaves the Proposed state. Of the Foundation's event documents, only MIP-0002 is Accepted; MIP-0019, MPS-0005, MPS-0007 and MPS-0043 are Proposed [@midnight-mip-0002; @midnight-mip-0019; @midnight-mps-0005; @midnight-mps-0007; @midnight-mps-0043]. The Foundation's private event is a contract event with encrypted fields, planned for Phase 2 of MPS-0005, and the text of its Part 2 is not published. Midnight Express complements private events and, under the conditions that the MIP states for carried events, can transport them. It does not replace them or MPS-0005 Part 2.

## Main risks and what would change course

A reason to change course should be agreed before the evidence arrives, so that a threshold cannot be moved afterwards to fit a disappointing result. `MPE-OPS-053` therefore requires a course-change register in which every trigger has a measurable threshold and a named response. The thresholds come from the requirement set. The two load thresholds are assumed launch values that measurements in Stages 1 and 2 will replace. Table 11.3 gives the main entries of the register.

| Risk | Trigger | Response |
|---|---|---|
| The Admission Proof is too large or too slow | An encoded Admission Slot above 4,096 B (a proof above 3,992 B) or verification above 10 ms (`MPE-ECO-048`); an Admission Slot above 1,024 B | Drop the 256 B class from the table (`DEC-013`). If the proof also fails the size gate, fall back to an object-bound blind stamp. If no instrument fits, keep the count ceiling at 10/s per Shard |
| Admission does not hide the member | No proof system gives unlinkable admission within the budget | Withdraw the claim that an MPE Envelope is unlinkable to its membership. The stand-in's linkage becomes the stated limit |
| Scoring does not stop a determined Sybil | The fully poisoned eclipse or the 20% cold-start Sybil run at 200 Bus Nodes falls below 99% delivery or above 6 s p99; experiment 7 or 8 shows a peer that keeps its mesh place while sending invalid traffic or after exclusion; the Stage 3 red team pushes canary delivery below 99.9% | Keep the allow-list until scoring changes. Scoring alone neither limits identity creation nor guarantees Sybil resistance: the audit of GossipSub v1.1 found that scoring is not a complete Sybil defence [@2020-leastauthority-gossipsub-audit], and formal analysis found attackers that withhold messages while keeping positive scores [@2022-kumar-gossipsub-formal] |
| The anonymity set stays small | Organic load below 5 Messages/s six months into Stage 2; class 3 below 30 MPE Envelopes per minute | Fund cover traffic inside the bandwidth budget, or publish no broadcast anonymity claim. Strong anonymity needs either high bandwidth or high latency [@2017-das-trilemma], and an empty network has neither |
| Whole-Shard reception does not fit mobile | Measured whole-Shard reception above the 56 to 60 MB per day mobile budget; the arithmetic gives about 1.86 GB per day at the baseline mix (Chapter 6) | Mobile reception only as labelled opt-in profiles with their own claims (`DEC-021`). If no measured profile fits, mobile Subscribers are out of scope for the first release |
| Bus Operators face liability for ciphertext | Counsel memos find liability in core jurisdictions, or require capabilities that break content-blindness | Keep relays with stewards in safe jurisdictions, or stop at the ledger lane |
| The ledger lane cannot carry its classes | Experiment 14 shows class 2 below 1 Message/s inside the 10% share, or a 16-part proof too slow to build on client hardware | The lane carries classes 0 and 1 only, as emergency notification, and P-FMT-8 falls from 16 to 4 parts; MIP acceptance criterion 2 is restated for classes 0 and 1 before the criteria are frozen, and class 2 is refused on the lane with a stated non-claim |
| Contract consumption has no affordable authority check | Neither Ed25519 nor JubJub Schnorr proves within a practical client budget in experiment 16 | Withdraw in-contract consumption from the first release, and remove acceptance criterion 4 from the release profile, with a stated non-claim, before the criteria are frozen. An Anchor inclusion proof is never presented as authority, because nothing in it binds the consumption nullifier to the Message (`DEC-022`) |
| Ledger 9 is late on mainnet | No activation date before the planned Stage 2 start | Run Stages 1 and 2 against a devnet Bus Registry and make no mainnet claims |
| Midnight governance uses safe mode often | Repeated pauses longer than the 25-hour cached-root limit | Design a membership path that does not depend on the ledger |
| Bus Operator funding lapses | Commitments cover less than 12 months for any launch role | Do not leave the permissioned stage |
| A second GossipSub implementation cannot join the profile | A network-specific identifier such as `/mpe/<g>/1/gossipsub/1.2.0` would need a vendored `Version::V1_2` change; a default offer list negotiates a version other than `/meshsub/1.2.0`; the Go validator still rejects D_out = 4 at D = 8 | Negotiate the standard `/meshsub/1.2.0` under a strict version policy with a negotiation test; carry network scope in Shard names, admission binding of the full genesis hash and a subscription filter; propose the Go fix upstream, or fall back to the mesh tuple (D, D_low, D_high, D_out) = (6, 5, 12, 2), which rust-libp2p and go-libp2p-pubsub both accept |
| MPS-0005 Part 2 conflicts with carriage | Part 2 is published with a key type, topic model or commitment rule that a carried event cannot meet | Carry no private events. Midnight Express stays a complement that delivers Confidential Messages, and any recipient-key profile follows `encrypt_for` as specified |
| A claimed property breaks | A demonstrated break of content confidentiality or interest-hiding against an adversary that Chapter 4 names | Stop issuing that claim at once and treat the break as Sev1, the highest incident severity (`MPE-OPS-039`). A break of timing, relationship or global-observer privacy does not count, because those were never claimed |

Bus Nodes negotiate the GossipSub version through a protocol string, and a link that settles on v1.1 runs without IDONTWANT. The profile requires the standard `/meshsub/1.2.0`. The proof of concept will negotiate it too. A network-specific identifier such as `/mpe/<g>/1/gossipsub/1.2.0`, with `<g>` the first eight bytes of the genesis hash in lowercase hex, could get v1.2 only through a vendored `Version::V1_2` change, because the stock crate's `protocol_id` accepts only `V1_0` and `V1_1` (Chapter 8).^[libp2p/rust-libp2p, protocols/gossipsub/src/config.rs, enum `Version` and `ConfigBuilder::protocol_id`; libp2p-gossipsub 0.51.0, master branch, accessed 2 October 2026.] In the mesh tuple, D is a Bus Node's target number of mesh peers per Shard, D_low and D_high are the bounds at which it adds or prunes peers, and D_out is the minimum number of them that it dialled itself. The specification allows D_out = D/2, but the Go validator requires D_out < D_low and D_out < D/2, so it rejects (8, 6, 12, 4) and accepts (6, 5, 12, 2).^[libp2p/go-libp2p-pubsub, gossipsub.go, `GossipSubParams.validate`; master branch, accessed 1 October 2026.]

Two of these risks would change the architecture itself. If the poisoned-eclipse and red-team results show that a scored mesh cannot be opened safely, Midnight Express stays a federation of listed Bus Operators. That is closer to Option 5 of Chapter 6 than to an open overlay, and it should be described as one. If counsel or funding blocks every Bus Operator role, the ledger and Indexer path of Option 1 is what remains. That path is public and slow, bounded by the 256-byte `Misc` payload per part, the 1 KiB event limit and the per-block `block_usage` budget, or the 50,000 B `bytes_written` budget under MIP-0002's sizing, but it is available. Both outcomes turn on organizations and law more than on engineering, so the procurement exercise and the counsel memos can start while the proof of concept is being built.

Some risks have no trigger in the register, because no experiment can remove them; they are stated limits in every stage. The ingress Bus Node sees the submitting connection directly, and neighbours see forwarding paths and possession times. No GossipSub configuration removes these observations. Interest privacy holds only while subscriptions, requests, retries, errors, repair and diagnostics stay independent of recognition, so one selective request in a client can undo it. A Reject verdict charges P4 to the forwarding peer, which need not be the party that built an invalid admission, while an Ignore drops the MPE Envelope with no penalty; the boundary between Reject and Ignore must therefore stay conservative. P6, which counts peers sharing one IP address, can penalize honest peers behind a shared address. Overload can lose MPE Envelopes beyond a local Busy reply, through validation-cache deadlines, queue bounds and IDONTWANT races. Inventories and Anchors expose some omissions, but nothing proves that every MPE Envelope existed, reached a Store Node or remains retrievable. Expiry cannot delete ciphertext or metadata that an adversary has recorded, and a later key compromise exposes archived content under any profile without forward secrecy. Nor does a transport conformance result establish release security.
