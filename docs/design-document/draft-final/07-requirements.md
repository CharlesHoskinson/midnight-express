# Analysis of the requirements {#ch7}

Chapter 6 recommended Option 2: Bus Nodes running GossipSub as a sidecar beside the Midnight node, with the ledger used for membership, Anchors and a ledger lane. That recommendation does not yet say what a Bus Node must do with a malformed MPE Envelope, how long a Store Node keeps one, or what a contract may conclude from an Anchor. The requirement set answers those questions with one obligation per entry, each with a check that can fail, and its decision register records the 28 decisions that fix its main choices. Appendix A lists every requirement in full.

The object of the requirement set is the Message: the application message a Publisher seals into an MPE Envelope and delivers through Bus Nodes. A Message is not a contract event. In Midnight, an event is a contract event that a Compact circuit emits and a transaction records [@midnight-mip-0002]. A private event is a contract event some of whose fields are encrypted for chosen recipients and bound to the transaction by a commitment that the proof checks, as MPS-0005 plans for its unpublished Part 2 [@midnight-mps-0005]. Chapter 4 defines the Message, the Confidential Message and the carried event. Midnight Express redefines neither kind of event: it complements on-chain private events and, under the conditions its carried-event requirements state, transports them.

## How the requirements are organised

The requirements are written in EARS (Easy Approach to Requirements Syntax). Each requirement is one sentence in one of six patterns: ubiquitous ("The Bus Node shall ..."), state-driven ("While ..."), event-driven ("When ..."), optional feature ("Where ..."), unwanted behaviour ("If ..., then ...") and complex combinations of these. In EARS, "event-driven" names the sentence pattern, a trigger in time, and has nothing to do with contract events. The subject of a sentence comes from a fixed list: the client library, the Bus Node, the Store Node, the Bus Registry, the Ledger Adapter, the Indexer adapter, the prototype, or Midnight Express as a whole. The prototype is the requirement set's name for the proof of concept that Part III specifies and that has not yet been built: a single reference implementation with a deterministic simulator. The Indexer adapter is the client's interface to the Indexer, the service that reads chain data and serves it to wallets and DApps [@midnight-mps-0028]. Each record carries a scope (POC for the proof of concept, PROD for production), a priority (MUST, SHOULD or MAY), a status (settled, or open, with the register decision it waits on where one exists) and a verification method with a concrete check. A sentence that depends on a tunable number names a parameter such as `P-FMT-1` in place of a literal, so a value can change without rewriting the sentence.

`MPE-FMT-007` has all of these parts. It reads: "If a received MPE Envelope's length differs from 8 + P-FMT-2 + the P-FMT-1 body length of its `size_class`, then the Bus Node shall return Reject." It is an unwanted-behaviour sentence whose subject is the Bus Node, with POC scope, MUST priority and settled status. Its check is a test in which valid vectors shortened or lengthened by one byte must be Rejected. With the 8-byte header, the 512-byte Admission Slot (`P-FMT-2`) and a 1,024-byte class-1 body (`P-FMT-1`), a class-1 MPE Envelope must be exactly 8 + 512 + 1,024 = 1,544 B, and one of 1,543 B or 1,545 B fails. If a real admission proof forces the slot to grow, the value of `P-FMT-2` changes and the sentence stays as written.

Two structures organise the set. The first is the ten **decision areas**, the questions with several defensible answers that any system delivering Confidential Messages on Midnight has to settle once. Chapter 6 used the same list as its criteria: D1 Message format, D2 definition of private, D3 publish and subscribe model, D4 sustainable model, D5 performance, D6 storage, D7 infrastructure actors, D8 network tether, D9 threats, D10 build and verification plan. The second is the twelve **requirement areas**, each owning a prefix and the obligations that one component, or one discipline such as testing, has to meet; an open requirement names the register decision it waits on where one exists. Most requirement areas map to one decision area. Two decision areas are large enough to feed several. D1 feeds both the wire format (FMT) and part of cryptography (CRY), and D3 feeds the publish and subscribe semantics (PUB), the consumer interface (CON) and the rest of CRY.

## The shape of the set

The set has 625 entries. Of these, 510 are live requirements, 113 restate an obligation that another entry owns, and 2 are withdrawn. A restating entry keeps its identifier so that cross-references from other areas still resolve; its text points to the entry that owns the obligation, and its parameters take the owner's values. `MPE-CON-013`, for example, says only that `MPE-PUB-036` owns deduplication by MPE Envelope Identifier, and `MPE-PUB-036` states the obligation: the client library passes each MPE Envelope Identifier to the application at most once per subscription store. The two withdrawn entries, `MPE-CON-007` and `MPE-CON-008`, covered a Recognition Tag lookahead window that the salted Recognition Tag of `DEC-003` does not need.

The 625 entries have three sources. The twelve areas account for 572, of which 28 state obligations that span areas and that no single area owns. Another 44 state obligations imposed by the layers beneath the set: the GossipSub specifications and implementations, Midnight's improvement proposals and problem statements, and the compiled ledger interface. The last 8 keep the number of an area entry with a suffix, such as `MPE-CON-044b`. Of these 52, NET holds 17, and PUB, STO, SEC and VER hold none, because their obligations stay inside Midnight Express.

The areas differ most in how many of their entries are live and in how many live requirements wait on an open decision. The second column gives each area's share of the 572.

The table shows three things. First, the set leans towards the proof of concept: 341 of the 510 live requirements (67 percent) have POC scope. The prototype core of 137 requirements is a subset of these 342; the other 342 − 137 = 205 are outside the core. Appendix A gives each of them a verification method and records no execution status for any requirement. OPS and ECO are the exceptions, with 40 of 45 and 33 of 50 live requirements for production. Bus Operator rosters, governance, payment and bonds cannot be shown in a proof of concept with a mock ledger, so their requirements are stated as production gates with a check attached. The requirements on carried events, disclosure objects and measurements of the ledger interface are production requirements for the same reason: each needs a ledger-9 network, a published specification or both.

Second, the consumer and threat areas restate the most. CON has 66 entries but only 38 live requirements, and SEC has 42 entries but 20 live. PUB owns many consumer obligations (deduplication, gap reporting, cursors), and NET, PUB and OPS own many defences (rate limits, reconciliation, the relay allow-list).

Third, most of the set is MUST. Of the live requirements, 445 are MUST, 49 SHOULD and 16 MAY; of the 169 that wait on an open decision, 129 are MUST, 27 SHOULD and 15 MAY. An open MUST is written for the recommended default, so the proof of concept can be built and tested against it. If the decision goes the other way, the requirement changes.

The open requirements cluster in OPS (24), CRY (20), ECO and PRF (19 each) and STO (18). Most of those in CRY, ECO, PRF and STO wait on measurements that a proof of concept with a stand-in Admission Proof cannot make; those in OPS wait on written commitments, governance invariants and legal opinions.

Besides the requirements, the set defines 134 parameters. Of the 134, 53 have values reconciled across areas, each with a prototype value and a production value. The other 81 keep the default stated by the area that owns them.

The prototype core is 137 requirements in six milestones, listed here in dependency order: M0, the MPE Envelope, sealing and recognition (26); M1, the admission stand-in and the validator (32); M2, the Bus Node and overlay (20); M3, the client library and Store Node (23); M4, the ledger, Anchors, contract consumer and ledger lane (17); and M5, simulation and measurement (19). Each milestone checks, carries or measures what the ones before it produce. For each core requirement the set names the test or scenario whose failure would show it unmet, and whether that check depends on a mock. The other 204 POC-scope requirements are deferred mostly because their checks need a real admission prover, a 72-hour soak, adversarial runs with 200 or 1,000 Bus Nodes, or a ledger-9 devnet.

## The decision register

Each of the 28 decisions, `DEC-001` to `DEC-028`, gives the options considered, a prototype default, a production default, the reason, and the evidence that would settle it. Twenty-four settle choices within one area; four (`DEC-009`, `DEC-010`, `DEC-016`, `DEC-017`) settle choices that span areas.

### Architecture and the ledger tether

Three decisions set what the ledger does.

| Decision | Options | Choice | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| `DEC-008` Ledger role and ledger lane | Overlay from the start; ledger lane first; ledger only; degraded delivery by gateway, by `Misc` events, or by a protocol inside the Midnight node; Anchors only | Overlay from the start; ledger for membership and Anchors; degraded delivery first through whole-feed gateways, then through the ledger lane as a labelled last resort, never automatic | Mainnet runs ledger 8; the ledger lane shares 50,000 B of `bytes_written` and a block usage limit of 1,000,000 or 200,000 B (live value unknown) per 6 s block, in 256 B payloads |
| `DEC-009` Stale chain view | Ignore everything chain-dependent; keep forwarding under cached state | Keep forwarding MPE Envelopes valid under cached eligible roots; never Reject on a chain- or clock-dependent check | Otherwise any Ledger Adapter outage is a Midnight Express outage |
| `DEC-024` Anchor granularity and history | Per Shard per window; one per window over all Shards; 7- or 14-day history; history bounded by lifetime | One Anchor per 60 s window over all Shards; 49 h live history (2,940 records) in 2,940 slots indexed by window number modulo 2,940 | Per-Shard Anchors multiply records by the Shard count; contracts reject expired Messages |

`DEC-008` fixes the shape of the system. Option 1 of Chapter 6, a ledger-and-Indexer lane, has no public network to run on today. Contract events arrive with ledger 9, and mainnet ran ledger 8.0 as of 4 August 2026 [@midnight-mip-0018; @midnight-coip-0005]; MIP-0002 is live on Stagenet. The documentation describes the earlier ledger generation, while the Midnight node's code pins ledger 9 (Chapter 2). A ledger lane would also share each 6 s block with all other traffic, under two limits that measure different things. Block usage bounds the size of the block, every transaction included. `bytes_written` bounds the bytes the block newly writes to the ledger's storage, net of what it deletes.^[midnightntwrk/midnight-ledger, spec/cost-model.md, struct `SyntheticCost` and section "Churn vs Writes"; ledger-8 branch, accessed 2 October 2026.] The Midnight node's mainnet parameters set a block usage limit of 1,000,000 B,^[midnightntwrk/midnight-node, res/mainnet/ledger-parameters-config.json, `limits.block_limits.blockUsage` and `bytesWritten`; runtime/src/lib.rs, constant `SLOT_DURATION`; main branch, accessed 1 October 2026.] while the ledger's own initial limits set 200,000 B; which value is live is unknown until it is read from a ledger-9 network's `ledgerParameters`.^[midnightntwrk/midnight-ledger, ledger/src/structure.rs, constant `INITIAL_LIMITS`; main branch, accessed 1 October 2026.] Both sources set `bytes_written` to 50,000 B per block. Every contract event pays `Log` gas, and MIP-0002 sizes event capacity against the 50,000 B `bytes_written` budget at an event's serialized size, about 40 B above its struct data [@midnight-mip-0002]. The ledger-9 code charges a logged event as both written and deleted, and the block limit applies to net written bytes, so under the code an event adds nothing to `bytes_written`, its bytes count as churn, and block usage binds. Which reading governs a live ledger-9 network is unmeasured (`MPE-PRF-040`). The overlay therefore comes first.

The ledger still carries memberships, quota roots, Anchors and an emergency path. An Anchor is an authorized anchorer's commitment to the MPE Envelope Identifiers it accepted in one 60 s window, across all Shards. The Bus Registry checks the anchorer's authority, the window's timing bounds and the record's form. It does not check that the committed identifiers were admitted, nor their creation times or counts: the window, the root's contents and the counts are the anchorer's assertions, trusted as far as the allow-listed anchorer is. What the chain establishes is that the commitment existed at the finalized block that includes it. For each Shard the anchorer, a Bus Node whose Bus Registry entry carries the anchorer role, computes a batch root: a SHA-256 Merkle root over the MPE Envelope Identifiers it accepted in that window, in ascending byte order. It hashes the batch roots into one window root and posts the window number, the window root and the per-Shard counts to the Bus Registry (`MPE-NET-050` to `MPE-NET-053`). Anchor windows and admission windows are both 60 s long and numbered by Unix time divided by 60, so their boundaries coincide.

The order of the degraded paths matters for privacy. A gateway that streams the whole feed keeps the overlay's framing and reveals nothing new. The ledger lane puts sealed bodies into public `Misc` events, at block pace, and is allowed only as a labelled last resort, so that a client never drops to a weaker profile without being told (`MPE-PRV-013`). Bus Registry contract state never holds a body.

`DEC-009` resolves a conflict between two goals: a Bus Node with a stale chain view cannot both Ignore every MPE Envelope whose admission depends on chain state and keep delivering through a ledger interruption, and no test that freezes the mock adapter can pass both. GossipSub lets an application return one of three verdicts for each message it validates: Accept forwards it, Reject drops it and penalises the peer that delivered it, and Ignore drops it without a penalty. The register keeps delivery. While its view is stale, a Bus Node accepts MPE Envelopes that validate against a cached root, as long as that root is current or was superseded less than an hour ago. It Ignores what it cannot check, and it never Rejects on a check that depends on the chain or the clock. A Reject applies GossipSub's invalid-message penalty (P4, one component of the score each Bus Node keeps for every peer) to the forwarding peer, which need not be the Publisher, and would punish honest forwarders for the Bus Node's own lag. Ignore avoids that penalty, although it earns the forwarder no delivery credit [@2020-gossipsub-v11-spec].

Suppose a Bus Node's Ledger Adapter falls two minutes behind a registration that changes the membership root. A Reject of an MPE Envelope admitted under the new root would charge the P4 penalty to a mesh peer that broke no rule. An Ignore only declines to forward, and the MPE Envelope keeps spreading through Bus Nodes whose view is current. `MPE-ECO-051` bounds how long cached roots stay usable: a Bus Node Ignores an MPE Envelope whose root serves a membership period that ended more than an hour earlier. Bus Nodes enforce the one-hour window itself (`P-ECO-5`). The Bus Registry does not, because its historic Merkle tree for each membership period accepts every root it has ever had, with no time bound (`MPE-ECO-015`).

`DEC-024` rests on one observation about expiry. A contract rejects an expired Message (`MPE-CON-046`), so no contract needs a root older than the 48-hour lifetime, and live Anchor history of 49 hours leaves a one-hour margin. The Bus Registry stores live Anchors in 2,940 slots indexed by window number modulo 2,940, each new record replacing the one 2,940 windows older, so the bound on live records holds by construction and pruning needs no privileged caller (`MPE-STO-028`, `MPE-STO-029`). The `Misc` copy of an Anchor is a notification, not consensus state. A client that needs an older window root reads the `mip-xxxx:anchor[v1]` event through the Indexer and checks it against the historic roots of the Bus Registry's Anchor tree, which the contract keeps accepting after the slot record is replaced. That set of roots is not bounded by the slots. The live Anchor records stay at 2,940, but the Anchor tree's set of accepted roots grows by about 1,440 roots (about 46 KB) a day until a reset rule, still open, bounds it, so the 49-hour bound covers the slot records only.

### The MPE Envelope and its identifiers

Four decisions fix what a Bus Node sees on the wire and how it names each MPE Envelope.

| Decision | Options | Choice | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| `DEC-013` Size classes and Admission Slot | One 4,096 B cell; one 2,048 B record; two cells; bodies 256 B to 16 KiB; 1 to 64 KiB; others | Bodies 256, 1,024, 4,096, 16,384 B with a 512 B Admission Slot; drop the 256 B class if the measured slot exceeds 1,024 B | One table serves the overlay and the ledger lane (1, 4 and 16 `Misc` parts; class 3 would need 64) |
| `DEC-010` Message identifier | MPE Envelope Identifier (excludes the slot); hash of every MPE Envelope byte | GossipSub message identifier over the complete MPE Envelope; MPE Envelope Identifier kept for admission binding, deduplication and Anchors | An identifier that excludes the slot lets one peer suppress an honest MPE Envelope |
| `DEC-015` Unknown version | Reject; Ignore; relay; accept two versions in one mesh | Version bound to the GossipSub topic; a mismatch on a supported topic is Reject; a protocol context the Bus Node does not support is Ignore; two GossipSub topics run side by side during an upgrade | Ignore of a mismatch would let a neighbour inject junk at no cost |
| `DEC-003` Recognition | Trial decryption; sequence Recognition Tags with lookahead; hourly Recognition Tags; salted per-Envelope Recognition Tag; encrypted-header trial; visible clue | Salted 16 B Recognition Tag in the sealed body's clear prefix; kept only if it measurably beats trial decryption | Unlinkable without the key; one HMAC per key per MPE Envelope |

`DEC-013` balances three costs. Every MPE Envelope pays the 8-byte visible header and the 512-byte Admission Slot, so a small class wastes a large share of its bytes. Every Subscriber downloads the whole Shard, so a large class raises everyone's cost. And each class is its own anonymity set: an observer who sees a 1,544-byte MPE Envelope learns only that it is one of the Shard's class-1 MPE Envelopes, so more classes split the traffic. The four classes chosen are each four times the last. Their sealed bodies map onto 1, 4, 16 and 64 parts of the 256-byte `Misc` payload, so one table serves both carriers, although the ledger lane stops at 16 parts (`P-FMT-8`) and never carries class 3 (`MPE-FMT-044`).

The production rule includes its own reversal: if a real Midnight-native proof runs to kilobytes, the 256-byte class becomes mostly overhead and is dropped. The compiled ledger interface contracts suggest the reversal may be needed; the arithmetic is given under `DEC-007` among the open decisions.

`DEC-010` follows from the order of work in rust-libp2p. A GossipSub router keeps the message identifiers it has seen recently in the GossipSub duplicate cache, so that a message arriving from several mesh peers is processed once. rust-libp2p inserts a message identifier into its duplicate cache before the application validates the message. A Reject then removes the message from the message cache, which serves gossip requests, but not from the duplicate cache.^[libp2p/rust-libp2p, protocols/gossipsub/src/behaviour.rs, functions `handle_received_message` and `report_message_validation_result`; master branch, accessed 1 October 2026.] Suppose the message identifier were the MPE Envelope Identifier, which leaves out the Admission Slot. A peer could forward an honest sealed body with a corrupted slot ahead of the honest copy. Every receiver that saw the corrupted copy first would drop the honest one as a duplicate, and the attacker would pay one invalid-message penalty per receiver. The set therefore defines the GossipSub message identifier as SHA-256 over the domain string `mpe/v1/msgid` and the complete MPE Envelope carried as `Message.data`, including the Admission Slot and excluding protobuf, RPC and transport framing (`MPE-NET-010`). Every Bus Node implementation must hash identical bytes. The corrupted copy then gets its own identifier. Given a collision-resistant hash, this closes that particular collision path; it is not a general delivery guarantee. The identifier leaves out the GossipSub topic, so a Bus Node must discard a message on a GossipSub topic it does not subscribe to before it records the message's identifier. The stock rust-libp2p router records the identifier first, so an unchanged valid MPE Envelope sent first on an unsubscribed topic would suppress the honest copy even with one Shard; the rule needs a router change and a recorded conformance test, and Q6 includes an unchanged copy sent first on an unsubscribed topic and on a wrong subscribed topic. An entry stays in the GossipSub duplicate cache for the configured lifetime, 60 s in the Rust configuration and 120 s by default in Go.^[libp2p/go-libp2p-pubsub, pubsub.go, variable `TimeCacheDuration`; master branch, accessed 1 October 2026.] The application seen-set, keyed by MPE Envelope Identifier and filled only after Accept (`MPE-SEC-041`), still collapses different valid admissions of one body into one delivery. It is kept until expiry plus 60 s, independently of the GossipSub duplicate cache (`MPE-STO-013`).

`DEC-015` separates two cases that differ in who is at fault. An MPE Envelope whose header version disagrees with the supported GossipSub topic it arrived on is malformed, and Reject is correct (`MPE-FMT-023`). An MPE Envelope whose validity a Bus Node cannot check, because it lacks support for that protocol context, is Ignored (`MPE-SEC-008`). Bus Nodes do not subscribe to versions they do not support, so version rejection never penalises valid traffic from a later profile.

A sequence-numbered Recognition Tag would lose a Publisher once more than a window's worth of numbers is skipped; the salted Recognition Tag of `DEC-003` has no such window. A salted Recognition Tag, an HMAC-SHA-256 over a fresh 16-byte salt, is unlinkable without the key. A Subscriber that follows three streams reads the salt from each MPE Envelope's clear prefix, computes the HMAC under each of its three recognition keys, truncates each result to 16 bytes and compares it with the Recognition Tag. A match names the key to try, and opening the body confirms it (`MPE-CRY-014`); no match means the MPE Envelope belongs to none of its streams. At the 256-key cap and 10 MPE Envelopes per second the cost is 2,560 evaluations per second. Recognition-hiding fields never sit in the visible header, because a visible clue would give every relay a per-Envelope string to correlate. The Recognition Tag does for Messages what MPS-0005's planned topic values do for on-chain private events, sparing the recipient a trial decryption of everything, but it is per-Envelope, unlinkable and evaluated only by the Subscriber [@midnight-mps-0005].

### Admission and payment

Five decisions settle who may publish, how much, and who pays.

| Decision | Options | Choice | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| `DEC-007` Admission instrument | Membership with per-Envelope admission nullifier proof (RLN-style, after the rate-limiting nullifier); single-use tickets; visible member key; issuer stamps; per-Message ledger fee; NIGHT bond | RLN-style membership, registered by a transaction whose DUST network fee is the only price, with revocation; bond off at launch; issuer stamps if the proof fails its size or time gate | Tickets cap at a few per second; visible keys link MPE Envelopes; issuers see customers; per-Message fees cap the chain; a contract cannot receive DUST |
| `DEC-016` Quota accounting | MPE Envelope count; byte weights with credit indices; per-class limits in the membership leaf | Per-class limits (64, 16, 4, 1) per 60 s admission window, one admission nullifier per MPE Envelope | Byte weights need up to 64 admission nullifiers per MPE Envelope, which one fixed slot cannot hold |
| `DEC-014` Equivocation | Reject the second MPE Envelope; Ignore it with evidence; deduplicate only | Ignore, record evidence, raise `AdmissionConflict`, revoke the membership | The forwarding peer may be honest, having seen the other copy first in another partition |
| `DEC-017` Replay-state durability | Durable commit before forwarding; accept a restart replay window; wait out the window | In-memory state; after every start, refuse live admission for 140 s | Every allowance is eligible for at most 100 s, which the 140 s barrier exceeds |
| `DEC-001` Paying Bus Operators | No payment; off-protocol contracts; treasury for contract-checkable work; pool paid on probe results | No protocol payment to relays; launch Bus Operators funded by 12-month written commitments; treasury option later | DUST is non-transferable, cannot be paid to a contract, and the runtime credits fees to no one |

The admission instrument has to hide which member published an MPE Envelope while stopping one member from flooding. A Waku-style rate-limiting nullifier (RLN) does both [@2021-vac-waku2-rln-relay-spec]: each MPE Envelope carries a zero-knowledge proof of membership and an admission nullifier that repeats only if the member exceeds its quota in an admission window.

The admission nullifier is computed from the member's secret, the network's genesis hash, the Bus Registry's contract address and the allowance in use: the admission window, the class and the credit index within the class. Binding the network and the Bus Registry keeps one secret used on two networks or two Bus Registries from yielding two points on one line. The proof shows that some member computed it correctly without showing which. Two MPE Envelopes under one allowance carry the same admission nullifier, so a Bus Node that sees it twice knows the allowance is spent. Each MPE Envelope also carries a share, a point on a straight line fixed by the member's secret and the allowance, whose value at x = 0 is the secret. One point reveals nothing about that value; two distinct points fix the line and reveal it, and the Bus Registry's `revoke` circuit checks that both lie on the member's line. A member within quota therefore stays anonymous, while one who spends an allowance twice hands every Bus Node that sees both MPE Envelopes the evidence for revocation. A Publisher must therefore durably reserve a credit (admission window, class and credit index) before any share computed under it leaves the client, and devices that share one membership must use disjoint credit ranges. After a restore whose credit state is uncertain, the Publisher publishes nothing under that membership until every admission window it might have used has ended plus the 20 s tolerance, or it registers a fresh membership.

Each alternative fails one test. Single-use on-chain tickets are unlinkable only at one ticket per transaction, and a visible member key links every MPE Envelope a member sends. An issuer of blind stamps learns a customer-to-Message map; a per-Message ledger fee caps the whole chain at a few tens of calls per second. A NIGHT bond with slashing needs contract-held NIGHT, which is not known to exist on mainnet, and the deployed Waku relay registers members for a transaction fee without the deposit of the original RLN proposal [@2024-revuelta-waku-latency]. A bond, if enabled, is a Bus Registry contract balance and has no relation to NIGHT staking, in which nothing is locked and stake carries no consensus weight [@midnight-mip-0016]. The proof of concept will use a stand-in with the same slot layout and a synthetic 4.5 ms verification cost. Its secrets are visible to every Bus Node, so it supports no claim about admission privacy (Chapter 8).

The requirements fix how membership works on the ledger. Registration inserts a leaf computed from the Bus Registry's current per-class quota, so a registrant cannot choose its own limits. Each membership period has its own tree, which anyone may remove one root window after the period ends (`MPE-ECO-026`, `MPE-ECO-055`). Because a contract can neither receive DUST nor observe a price, the cost of a membership is the network fee of the registration transaction (`MPE-ECO-004`). A price beyond that would need NIGHT or another token with a named receive path, shielded NIGHT by default (`MPE-ECO-056`). That default assumes a contract can receive shielded NIGHT, which, like the contract-held NIGHT a bond would need, is not known to exist on mainnet, so `MPE-ECO-056` remains an open production MAY. Revocation takes two shares under one admission window, class and credit index that lie on one member's line with distinct MPE Envelope Identifiers, plus that member's Merkle path, and it makes the leaf index and the identity commitment public (`MPE-ECO-028`). The admission relation must use a hash that Midnight documents as stable across upgrades; Compact's `transientHash` carries no such guarantee (`MPE-ECO-057`). The compiled Bus Registry hashes membership leaves with SHA-256 but the tree's inner nodes with the ledger's transient (Poseidon) hash, which Midnight does not commit to keeping stable. Before an admission proof profile is selected, either Midnight documents that hash as stable or the Bus Registry keeps membership in a tree whose inner nodes use `persistentHash`; the choice is open. The admission secret may come from the operating-system generator or from the wallet through the `deriveSecret` method of MIP-0015, which is Proposed and optional per wallet, with the genesis hash, the Bus Registry's contract address and the membership period as context. Either way it stays independent of every spending, encryption and signing key (`MPE-ECO-006`, `MPE-CRY-027`) [@midnight-mip-0015]. It is never sent to a prover outside the client's trust boundary unless that prover is attested, because a remote prover receives the private witness (`MPE-ECO-054`) [@midnight-mps-0004; @midnight-mps-0035; @midnight-mip-0017].

`DEC-016` avoids an arithmetic impossibility in weighting MPE Envelopes by size, at one credit per 256 B of body. A class-3 MPE Envelope would then consume 64 credits and need 64 admission-nullifier-and-share pairs, at least 4 KiB of public outputs against a 512 B slot. The lowest tier's quota of 10 credits would also have made class 3 impossible for that tier. Per-class limits fix both problems. Each class gets 16,384 B of body per admission window (64 × 256, 16 × 1,024, 4 × 4,096, 1 × 16,384), and one admission nullifier per MPE Envelope, bound to its class. The cost is looser byte accounting. A byte-weighted rule would keep a member's bytes within a factor of 2 across class mixes, but a member who uses all four classes can send 4 × 16 KiB per admission window, four times what one class allows and twice that target. That excess is accepted for the proof of concept.

The quota is a local rule: each Bus Node accepts at most one MPE Envelope Identifier per live allowance under its own acceptance state (`MPE-ECO-022`). Independent Bus Nodes can accept conflicting first uses before they exchange them, and no network-wide count follows from mesh connectivity alone.

`DEC-014` and `DEC-017` follow from one principle: a Bus Node must not punish a peer for something the peer could not know. A second MPE Envelope under a spent allowance may have been accepted first in another partition. If a member spends one allowance twice, sending MPE Envelope a to Bus Node X and b to Bus Node Y at the same moment, each accepts its copy correctly under its own state. When b later reaches X, the mesh peer that forwarded it may have seen b first and broke no rule, so X Ignores b, keeps the pair as evidence and raises `AdmissionConflict`. The two shares, with the member's Merkle path, are what revocation needs.

A restarted Bus Node has lost its admission-nullifier cache, so for a while it cannot tell a fresh MPE Envelope from a second use of an allowance spent before the restart. `MPE-ECO-014` bounds that while. A Bus Node Ignores an MPE Envelope whose admission window is not its current one unless its clock lies within the 20 s tolerance of the boundary between the two, so an allowance is eligible for at most 20 + 60 + 20 = 100 s. The restart barrier `P-ECO-7` is longer, two admission windows plus the tolerance, 2 × 60 + 20 = 140 s, so a Bus Node that waits it out after every start has nothing left that it could have forgotten. Ignore with evidence handles the first case, and the fixed waiting period the second. A Bus Node inside the waiting period answers each publication with an explicit Busy reply, and Q8 of Chapter 9 measures the benefit of that reply with silent Ignore as a control arm.

`DEC-001` is constrained by Midnight itself (Chapter 2): DUST cannot be transferred or paid to a contract, and the current runtime credits transaction fees to no account, so no in-protocol currency can pay a relay. A contract cannot check relay quality either. Payment on measured performance invites attacks on the measurement; in Nym's reputation system such attacks cut the cost of dominating the paid active set by over 99 percent [@2026-cao-nymreputation]. Launch Bus Operators are therefore funded off-protocol. A treasury paying only for work a contract can check (posting Anchors, passing retention challenges) remains an optional later feature. Posting Anchors costs the anchoring Bus Operator about 280 DUST a day at genesis prices (see the chain budget).

### Overlay and capacity

The load on a Shard and the shape of the mesh that carries it follow from five decisions.

| Decision | Options | Choice | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| `DEC-005` Operating rate | 10/s with a 64 KiB/s byte cap; 50/s per Shard; network-wide 50 to 500/s; ledger-lane rates | 10 MPE Envelopes/s and 64 KiB/s per Shard, whichever binds first; 50/s as a comparison run | Rates mean nothing without sizes; the binding cost is per-Envelope CPU |
| `DEC-011` Shard count | One feed; up to 8; 16 | One Shard at launch, up to 8 by Bus Registry change after sustained overload | Each split divides the anonymity set; sharding does not reach mobile budgets |
| `DEC-012` Mesh profile | (6, 5, 12, 2); (8, 6, 12, 4) | (8, 6, 12, 4) with (6, 5, 12, 2) as a required comparison | The profile evaluated under Sybil cold start; four dialled slots to eclipse; satisfies D_out < D_low and D_out ≤ D/2 |
| `DEC-027` Bandwidth direction | Aggregate; per direction | Per direction: 6 Mbit/s for an edge Bus Node, 24 Mbit/s for a full Bus Node | An aggregate reading halves the planning figure |
| `DEC-023` Publish ingress | One-hop stem; two-hop stem; Dandelion++; mix ingress later; submission over an onion-routing network; no stem claim | One Bus Node chosen at random from two dialled; no anonymity claim | The Dandelion++ bound does not transfer to GossipSub meshes; the ingress sees the submitting connection |

`DEC-005` counts both rates in one unit. With the `P-PRF-28` class weights of 70, 25, 5 and 0.1 the mean wire size is 1,175.7 B (derived: (0.70 × 776 + 0.25 × 1,544 + 0.05 × 4,616 + 0.001 × 16,904) / 1.001), so 50 per second is 58,785 B/s, or 57.4 KiB/s, inside a 64 KiB/s byte cap. The binding unknowns are proof verification and recognition CPU per MPE Envelope, so the count cap is the conservative one. It rises only if a measured verifier keeps CPU, latency and delivery within budget at 50 per second.

For every GossipSub topic it joins, each Bus Node keeps a mesh: a small set of peers to which it forwards every full message, while other peers only hear message identifiers through gossip. D is the mesh size it aims for. At each heartbeat a Bus Node with fewer than D_low mesh peers adds some, and one with more than D_high prunes back to D. D_out is the number of mesh peers that must be connections the Bus Node dialled itself [@2020-gossipsub-v11-spec]. `DEC-012` takes the mesh parameters that the GossipSub v1.1 evaluation used: D = 8, D_low = 6, D_high = 12, with an outbound quota D_out kept below D_low and at most D/2 [@2020-vyzovitis-gossipsub]. That evaluation is the one run under the cold-boot Sybil attack that the proof of concept's gates include.

An eclipse attack surrounds a victim with attacker peers so that everything it sends or receives passes through the attacker. With an outbound quota of 4, an attacker who wants to eclipse a Bus Node must hold four of the connections the victim dialled itself; flooding it with inbound connections is not enough. Dialled connections matter because Heilman and colleagues showed that an off-path attacker who fills a Bitcoin node's address tables through unsolicited incoming connections can make all eight of its outgoing connections lead to attacker addresses after a restart [@2015-heilman-eclipse]. The quota resists inbound-only capture when honest dial sources exist; it does nothing against poisoned discovery or colluding outbound peers. The tuple satisfies the specification's constraints, `4 < 6` and `4 ≤ 8/2` (`MPE-NET-011`), but go-libp2p-pubsub validates D_out < D/2 strictly and rejects it,^[libp2p/go-libp2p-pubsub, gossipsub.go, method `GossipSubParams.validate`; master branch, accessed 1 October 2026.] so interoperation with Go under this profile is unestablished until that check is resolved and tested. The mesh bounds are maintenance targets, not a hard cap on the neighbours who see a message. Gossip widens that set, and so do explicit peers (configured peers outside the mesh), publication replenishment (publishing to extra peers when the mesh has fewer than D) and opportunistic grafting (adding well-scored peers when the mesh's median score falls). The cost of (8, 6, 12, 4) is a higher planning egress, derived under Bandwidth below: 5.24 Mbit/s at the byte cap, against 3.93 Mbit/s at D = 6.

`DEC-023` makes no anonymity claim for the publish path. Dandelion++ first passes a message along a short random path, the stem, and bounds the first-spy estimator, which names as the source the first honest peer to deliver the message to the adversary. Its proof assumes a random, roughly 4-regular anonymity graph that the adversary does not know [@2018-fanti-dandelionpp]. A GossipSub mesh is neither, so a one-hop stem is kept only if a first-spy measurement shows it lowers the precision of locating the Publisher compared with publishing directly. Submission over an onion-routing network would hide the Publisher's address from the ingress Bus Node at interactive latency but not from an adversary who observes both ends [@2015-sun-raptor]. Whatever the stem, the ingress Bus Node observes the submitting connection, its libp2p identity and its network address. That identity authenticates an endpoint, not the Publisher sealed inside the MPE Envelope.

No decision in the register covers the transport profile; the requirement set fixes it directly. A Bus Node runs GossipSub with v1.1 peer scores and extended application validation plus the v1.2 IDONTWANT capability (`MPE-NET-008`). With IDONTWANT, a Bus Node that has just received a large message tells its mesh peers not to send it another copy. The v1.2 specification is still a working specification, and a link to a v1.1 peer carries no IDONTWANT. Shard GossipSub topics and request-response identifiers use the prefix `/mpe/<g>/1`, where `g` is the first eight bytes of the genesis hash in lowercase hexadecimal (`MPE-NET-006`). Those names are routing labels; admission binds the full genesis hash. The profile requires negotiating `/meshsub/1.2.0`. The default offer lists of the Rust and Go libraries also offer v1.3 and older versions, so a Bus Node records the negotiated identifier and internal peer kind of every connection and refuses a mesh slot to any peer whose negotiated identifier is not the one the release profile selects (`MPE-NET-057`, `MPE-NET-058`). Stock rust-libp2p 0.50 negotiates `/meshsub/1.2.0` with its default identifiers but cannot pin v1.2 on a custom identifier: it accepts only `V1_0` or `V1_1` in `protocol_id`, and a `V1_1` selection with a name ending in `1.2.0` leaves IDONTWANT disabled.^[libp2p/rust-libp2p, protocols/gossipsub/src/config.rs, enum `Version` and method `ConfigBuilder::protocol_id`; master branch, accessed 1 October 2026.] The transport profile, and the proof of concept, negotiate the standard identifier `/meshsub/1.2.0` and apply `MPE-NET-058` to what each connection negotiates. A custom identifier such as `/mpe/<g>/1/gossipsub/1.2.0` would need a source change that adds `Version::V1_2` and would not interoperate with standard-only peers.

Four further rules complete the profile. Bus Nodes publish under StrictNoSign and drop any message that carries a `from`, `seqno`, `signature` or `key` field, even an empty one (`MPE-NET-009`). The rust-libp2p 0.50.0 decoder omits the `key` check, which 0.51.0 adds, so a Bus Node built on 0.50.0 is not fully StrictNoSign until it carries that check. StrictNoSign removes the pubsub author stamp. It authenticates no Publisher and gives no replay protection; both stay inside the sealed MPE Envelope.

Bus Nodes send and honour IDONTWANT on every v1.2 link and emit it only for serialized pubsub messages longer than 1,000 B (`P-NET-18`). They never penalise a peer that sends despite a notice, and they apply any excess-notice penalty only above a per-heartbeat limit stated in the release profile (`MPE-NET-062` to `MPE-NET-065`).

A Bus Node refuses subscriptions outside the supported major versions, the genesis prefix and the Bus Registry's current or transitional Shards, and negotiates no extension that changes message behaviour, partial messages included, unless the release profile specifies and reviews it (`MPE-NET-066`, `MPE-NET-067`). Finally, every encoded RPC, batched messages and control fields included, must fit 65,536 B, a limit Go must configure explicitly because its default is 1 MiB; the MPE Envelope length check is separate (`MPE-NET-014`, `MPE-FMT-010`).

The validation path is also fixed. Local submissions pass the same application checks before `publish` is called (`MPE-NET-059`). The ingress sends exactly one response per publication request, after it has committed application acceptance and attempted the GossipSub `publish` call: `Accepted{eid}` when the MPE Envelope was accepted and queued for sending; `PublishFailed{eid}` when it was accepted but the `publish` call failed (no mesh or fanout peers, full publication queue), in which case the acceptance state stays committed and the client retries the identical bytes through another Bus Node; `Busy` for transient local unavailability; `Refused` for other refusals (`MPE-NET-061`). If reporting a validation result fails because the message has left the GossipSub cache, the Bus Node records the MPE Envelope as not propagated (`MPE-NET-060`). `Accepted` means that the ingress completed application acceptance and committed its acceptance state (`MPE-NET-049`, `MPE-PUB-022`); it does not prove that any mesh peer received the MPE Envelope. Busy and Refused are Midnight Express service replies, not GossipSub validation outcomes.

### Delivery, retention and consumption

Five decisions govern how long a Message lasts, how a client learns that nothing was withheld, and what a contract may do with it.

| Decision | Options | Choice | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| `DEC-004` Lifetime and retention | 5 to 60 minutes; 10-minute cache plus stores; 48 h; 7 days; TTL classes; 24 h; 14 days | 48 h lifetime equal to ordinary retention; optional paid archive to 7 days; 14-day hard ceiling | Covers a wallet offline overnight; storage per replica stays near 11 GB per Shard |
| `DEC-025` Second-source reconciliation | Mandatory; optional with sequence-gap detection | Mandatory from two independent Bus Operators | Sequence gaps cannot reveal suppression of a whole Publisher or of first contact |
| `DEC-026` Delivery labels | One label, gossip or final; a stored label; a degraded-path label | Three independent attributes: finality (gossip or final, with a deferred in-block state), carrier (overlay, gateway or ledger lane), storage | The properties vary independently |
| `DEC-022` Contract consumption | In-circuit publisher signature with consumption nullifier; Anchor inclusion check; commitment note; none; Ed25519 or JubJub Schnorr | In-circuit publisher signature with a secret-derived consumption nullifier, Ed25519 by default with an optional JubJub Schnorr profile chosen by measurement; Anchor inclusion optional and never authority on its own | Transactions have no signer; inclusion proves existence, not who may act |
| `DEC-021` Light and mobile reception | Whole Shard; server-side filter; Recognition Tag index with decoys; fuzzy message detection (FMD); bucket prefixes | Whole Shard by default; others only as labelled opt-in profiles | No selective mode keeps selection privacy at a demonstrated cost |

`DEC-004` sets the window in which a Subscriber can be offline and lose nothing. A 48-hour lifetime covers a device switched off overnight, with a day's margin. A Subscriber offline for longer gets a typed retention-gap item in place of silent loss (`MPE-PUB-045`). The hard ceiling of 14 days equals the ledger's own `global_ttl` of 1,209,600 s,^[midnightntwrk/midnight-node, res/mainnet/ledger-parameters-config.json, field `global_ttl`; main branch, accessed 1 October 2026.] the furthest ahead a transaction's time-to-live may be set,^[midnightntwrk/midnight-ledger, spec/intents-transactions.md, validity of an intent's `ttl` against `global_ttl`; ledger-8 branch, accessed 2 October 2026.] so no Message is kept longer than the longest validity horizon the ledger grants a transaction.

Expiry is an application rule that GossipSub does not read. In its gossip path, IHAVE advertises message identifiers to peers outside the mesh and IWANT asks for one of them, and the stock handlers can serve a validated cached message without checking expiry. The set therefore requires expiry-aware sending, IWANT responses and queued sends included, with a recorded conformance test (`MPE-FMT-032`). Expiry deletes nothing that an adversarial store chose to keep.

`DEC-025` makes the client check its feed against a second source and repair every difference. For each 60 s window of the Shard the client compares the MPE Envelope Identifiers it received with the inventory held by a Store Node of a Bus Operator other than its feed's (`MPE-PUB-014`). It retrieves every differing window in full from the source that has the missing identifiers, independently of recognition (`MPE-PUB-015`), because repairing only recognised MPE Envelopes would reveal interest (`MPE-SEC-023`). One honest source is enough to expose an omission by the other. The cost is one inventory of 32-byte identifiers per window, which the bandwidth derivation puts at 1.6 percent of feed bytes.

`DEC-026` separates three properties that one label cannot carry. Each Message carries three independent attributes: finality (gossip or final), carrier (overlay, gateway or ledger lane) and storage. A Message is stored once three Bus Operators have issued receipts for it, each signed with the Bus Operator's key and stating that its Store Node has committed the MPE Envelope to restart-safe storage until a stated deadline (`MPE-STO-011`, `MPE-STO-042`). A client requests receipts per Shard window, and each Store Node returns one for every MPE Envelope Identifier it retains in that window, so a request never singles out the Messages the client recognised. `final` means that the MPE Envelope Identifier verifies under an Anchor in Bus Registry state at a finalized block, or that ledger-lane parts match finalized on-chain data confirmed by `P-NET-14` independent sources or by block replay (`MPE-CON-016`).

The attributes of one Message change over its life. A client receives an MPE Envelope from the overlay within the 3 s propagation target: `gossip`, carrier overlay, not yet stored. Receipts from three Bus Operators make it stored. Its Anchor window lasts 60 s, and the Anchor is posted after a 20 s close delay, included in a block within about 6 s and finalized about 18 s later. An MPE Envelope accepted at the start of its window therefore turns `final` about 60 + 20 + 6 + 18 = 104 s later, plus the Anchor's proving time, which is unmeasured. If no Anchor covers it after 300 s, the client reports it as unanchored. The Ledger Adapter takes no value from a block that is not finalized (`MPE-NET-068`), so no rollback is ever needed at launch. If MPS-0028 produces a best-chain Indexer view, a deferred `in-block` state applies, withdrawn with a `rolled-back` notice when a reorganization removes its block (`MPE-CON-062`, `MPE-CON-063`) [@midnight-mps-0028; @midnight-mip-0018].

`DEC-022` decides how a contract acts on a Message, given that a Midnight transaction has no signer and a contract cannot read the overlay. A consumption nullifier is a value the contract stores when it acts on a Message, derived from a secret carried in the Message, so a second reaction to the same Message derives the same value and fails. The consuming circuit takes the Message statement, the payload bytes it uses and the publisher signature as private witness data, verifies the signature against a publisher set committed in the contract's state (`MPE-CON-043`), recomputes SHA-256 of those payload bytes and requires it to equal the signed digest, derives every effect input from those bytes under the signed schema, derives a consumption nullifier from a secret in the Message, and inserts it in the same transition as the effect (`MPE-CON-044`). This signed-instruction consumption proves who authorized the instruction, not that the overlay admitted or anchored it.

Ed25519 is the default signature because the sealed authentication block already uses it. MIP-0013 rejects Ed25519 inside circuits, because emulated field arithmetic needs hundreds of constraints per operation [@midnight-mip-0013]. An optional JubJub Schnorr profile, which Compact's `jubjubSchnorrVerify` accepts, therefore sits beside the default (`MPE-CRY-043`), and measurement decides between them. Anchor inclusion is optional because it cannot stand alone. A circuit that proves an anchored MPE Envelope Identifier exists and derives a consumption nullifier from a private secret binds nothing between the two, so one holder of an inclusion proof could react several times with different secrets. Where inclusion is used, it proves only that some anchored MPE Envelope exists. Binding the signed statement to that Envelope, by proving in circuit that the anchored MPE Envelope Identifier commits to a Sealed Body that opens to the statement, is the anchored-consumption profile, which version 1 does not specify (`MPE-CON-060`); until it is specified, no contract can prove that a Message was admitted or anchored by a deadline. The expiry check has its own leak. Every block-time comparison puts its operand in the public transcript, the record of ledger operations that the transaction publishes, so the circuit discloses only a coarse bound chosen by the prover and checks the signed expiry privately (`MPE-CON-061`).

`DEC-021` keeps the privacy definition of Chapter 4: a Subscriber that asks for less than the whole Shard tells the server something, so selective profiles may come later only as opt-in profiles with their own claims. Fuzzy message detection is one example. In Penumbra's variant, consensus sets the false-positive rate so that the filtered stream stays near a target size, and a consensus rule checks that clues use it; the recipient does not choose its own precision, and the specification sets no numeric floor on the rate [@2022-penumbra-fmd-spec]. MPS-0005's planned topic filtering at the Indexer and MIP-0002's prefix filters, which let an application trade disclosure against returned volume, belong in the same family: applied to Messages they are reduced-privacy selective profiles, never the default [@midnight-mps-0005; @midnight-mip-0002]. The requirements that forbid infrastructure-side relevance mapping (`MPE-PUB-009`, `MPE-PUB-010`, `MPE-PRV-007`, `MPE-PRV-008`) exclude MPS-0005's per-wallet relevance table and session decryption by design.

### Cryptographic profile and first contact

Two decisions limit what the cryptography claims at launch and decide how two parties first meet.

| Decision | Options | Choice | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| `DEC-002` Forward secrecy | No claim; per-admission-window hash progression; Double Ratchet sessions | No forward-secrecy claim for broadcast Shards; ratchet as an optional pairwise profile behind an independent security review | Stream keys give no forward secrecy; hash progression does not revoke; ratchets need erasure |
| `DEC-020` First contact | Invitations only; inbox to a published key; inbox with FMD clue; intro inbox with a stricter tier | Authenticated invitations only; no fuzzy detection clues; a profile that encrypts to Midnight shielded keys deferred until MPS-0005 Part 2 defines `encrypt_for` | An open inbox invites harassment; detection keys expose candidates to the detector |

Forward secrecy means that a key stolen today does not open messages sent before the theft. Static or stream-key encryption gives no forward secrecy against a compromised recipient key [@2022-barnes-rfc9180, sec. 9.1]. A hash chain per period would add some, but it does not remove a revoked holder, and keys kept for the 48-hour retention window cancel most of the benefit. Double Ratchet sessions give forward secrecy only if old keys are actually erased [@2016-signal-double-ratchet-spec]. For a broadcast feed retained for two days, the set therefore claims none at launch and records that in the capability record (`MPE-CRY-026`).

Each MPE Envelope version binds exactly one cryptographic profile. The one-byte suite identifier names that profile in invitations and in the label table, as a suite byte marks MIP-0012's encrypted inbox entries [@midnight-mip-0012]; it is not a wire field and is not in the associated data, and a new suite takes a new version (`MPE-FMT-053`). The launch profile is suite `0x01`: invitation-distributed symmetric stream secrets with HKDF-SHA-256, HMAC-SHA-256 recognition, ChaCha20-Poly1305 sealing and plain Ed25519 publisher authentication (`MPE-CRY-001`). A first-contact profile that encrypts to a recipient's Midnight shielded encryption public key, resolved through MIP-0007, is written as a deferred option (`MPE-CRY-045`) [@midnight-mip-0007]. No suite identifier for it is frozen until the `encrypt_for` construct of MPS-0005 Part 2 is published, so that Midnight Express cannot standardise a profile that Midnight's own private events later contradict (`MPE-CRY-044`).

### Governance and operation

Four entries decide who relays, who governs the Bus Registry and how the rollout stages are numbered.

| Decision | Options | Choice | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| `DEC-006` Relay admission | Bus Registry allow-list until a failed capture test; contracted Bus Operators plus open relays; open relays policed by peer scores | Allow-list at launch, at least 8 organisations, at most 20 percent each; removal only after a red-team test fails to push delivery below 99.9 percent | Peer scores alone do not limit identity creation |
| `DEC-019` Bus Registry governance | Steward set with time-locks; Council and Technical Committee; Foundation multisig; immutable contracts | Steward set with time-locks and 7-day emergency expiry, handed over or removed before open admission ends | Federated motions take 5 days; immutable contracts have no ejection path |
| `DEC-018` Tombstones | 48 h authenticated deletion; none; cooperative deletion | Off in the proof of concept; optional in production, binding only Bus Operators, deletion within 24 h, every tombstone published | A 48 h deadline never acts before 48 h expiry; deletion authority is a censorship tool |
| `DEC-028` Rollout stage numbering | Verification stages; operations stages; ledger-first stages | Operations numbering: everything in the proof of concept is Stage 0 | The operations scheme has the open-admission and handover stages |

`DEC-006` is the most consequential operational decision. Least Authority's security review of GossipSub and the formal analysis of its score function both describe configurations in which Sybil peers, many identities run by one attacker, raise their scores, take over a victim's mesh, or withhold messages while keeping a positive score [@2020-leastauthority-gossipsub-audit; @2022-kumar-gossipsub-formal]. GossipSub v1.1 combines peer scores, outbound quotas, adaptive gossip and secure discovery to improve attack resistance, but scores alone neither limit identity creation nor guarantee Sybil resistance [@2020-gossipsub-v11-spec]. Paying for publication does not restrict who may relay. A permissioned roster at launch is the only defence against relay Sybils that the set can state and test. Exclusion is enforced in its own right: a Bus Node refuses GRAFT from unlisted peers and prunes an evicted peer directly (`MPE-NET-034`). An application score of −20 alone would not prune a relay whose capped topic contribution of up to +32 outweighs it, so the application score is −33 at weight 1 under the stated profile, enough to make the total negative, for an unlisted peer while the allow-list applies and for an evicted peer throughout its backoff period, the interval after a prune (one minute by the specification's recommendation) in which the pruned peer may not rejoin the mesh.

`DEC-019` follows from the same need for an ejection path. Midnight's federated governance motions run for five days,^[midnightntwrk/midnight-node, runtime/src/lib.rs, constant `MOTION_DURATION`; main branch, accessed 1 October 2026.] too slow to remove a relay injecting malformed traffic. In the Bus Registry the time-lock is a stored delay between proposing and activating parameters, and the steward's authority is knowledge of a secret whose hash the contract holds, proved in circuit. Every governance action emits one `mip-xxxx:governance[v1]` event (`MPE-OPS-018`).

`DEC-028` numbers the rollout in stages because "Phase 2" already names MPS-0005's private events. Stage 0 is the proof of concept; the later stages carry the open-admission and handover steps.

## Contract events, carried events and the ledger interface

Midnight's event standard sets the terms the requirement set must meet wherever Midnight Express touches the ledger. Only MIP-0002, Public Contract Log Emission, is Accepted; MIP-0019, MPS-0005, MPS-0007 and MPS-0043 are Proposed. A contract emits an event with Compact's `emit`, the ledger records it as a versioned log item in the transaction, and the Indexer serves it from finalized blocks [@midnight-mip-0002; @midnight-coip-0003]. Custom data travels in the standard `Misc` event, a 32-byte name and a 256-byte payload, with a 1 KB cap on any one serialized event. Where MPS-0005 differs from MIP-0002 and CoIP-0003 on Phase 1 events, the Accepted and implemented texts govern. Private events, MPS-0005's Phase 2, are planned, and their specification text is unpublished. The set therefore treats Midnight's events as given, adds requirements in three places and draws one boundary.

Every `Misc` event that Midnight Express emits follows MIP-0018's convention that the name carries the version: `mip-xxxx:anchor[v1]`, `mip-xxxx:envelope[v1]` and `mip-xxxx:governance[v1]`, ASCII and NUL-padded to 32 bytes, where `xxxx` becomes the number the proposal is assigned and a new layout takes a new name (`P-FMT-10`, `MPE-FMT-040`, `MPE-FMT-047`, `MPE-NET-040`) [@midnight-mip-0018]. The Bus Registry emits only `Misc` events. On the ledger lane an MPE Envelope travels as its sealed body alone, cut into the 4^c parts of its class, all under the envelope name (`MPE-FMT-041`, `MPE-NET-044`). The visible header and the Admission Slot are not posted: the ledger-lane contract checks no Admission Proof, and each publication pays the DUST fee of its transaction instead. Each part is one `Misc` event with 288 bytes of struct data; 288 B is the struct, not the serialized size, which must be measured (`MPE-FMT-043`, `MPE-PRF-040`). The parts of one MPE Envelope are emitted in one execution phase of one intent, a group of contract calls inside a transaction, as MIP-0019's multipart rule requires (`MPE-FMT-042`) [@midnight-mip-0019]. On the ledger lane, a Publisher is also a MIP-0019 publisher and a Subscriber reads as a MIP-0019 reader. The lane cursor is the Indexer's monotonic event `id`, which the Ledger Adapter translates into the Midnight Express cursor, so the cursor never depends on one source's row numbers (`MPE-CON-053`, `MPE-PUB-043`).

Messages on the overlay are not events in MPS-0005's fee sense, so its base, data and planned encryption fees do not apply to them. The ledger budget of `MPE-ECO-011` counts registrations, Anchors and ledger-lane parts together, against both the block usage and the `bytes_written` limits. Version 1 uses the slash labels of the proposal's label table (`mpe/v1/msgid`, `mpe/v1/shard`, `mpe/v1/receipt`, `midnight-pe/id/v1` and the others) for every hash, MAC, KDF and signature domain, and the form `mip-xxxx:<name>[v1]` only for `Misc` names. MPS-0027 records that Midnight has no adopted label convention, and the proposal publishes the full table of labels and `Misc` names for registration once the registry that MPS-0027 recommends exists (`MPE-CRY-006`, `MPE-CRY-042`) [@midnight-mps-0027].

Midnight Express transports a contract event only as a carried event: a Message whose payload is the unchanged bytes of a contract event, public or private, with the hash of the recording transaction and the event's position in it. The set reserves one schema identifier for carried events (`MPE-FMT-054`). It requires the `VersionedLogItem` bytes, the ledger's own encoding of the event, exactly as the transaction records them (`MPE-FMT-055`), with the transaction hash, the event's logical and physical segments and position, the emitting contract address and the entry-point name, in the layout of schema `636576656e743031` (`MPE-FMT-056`). It decodes them only with Midnight's decoder for their event type and schema version, never with an application decoder (`MPE-FMT-057`). A carried event stays `gossip` until its transaction is finalized and its transcript holds the carried bytes at the stated position (`MPE-CON-058`), and an event from a fallible segment that did not succeed is never `final` (`MPE-CON-059`). Carriage adds no meaning. The event remains the contract author's claim [@midnight-mip-0002], the Publisher's signature adds only who forwarded it, and the fee is paid once, on chain. The layout spends 73 + `e` bytes before the logged item, `e` being the entry-point name length. Class 1 (854 B of payload) holds a logged item of up to 781 − `e` B; class 2 holds every event, since even with `e` = 255 it leaves 3,926 − 73 − 255 = 3,598 B; no carried event fits class 0 (86 B). A `Misc` event of about 328 B serialized fits class 1 for any entry-point name, and an event at the 1 KiB limit needs class 2. A private event must meet two further conditions. Its `@topic` values, if any, travel only inside the sealed body (`MPE-FMT-058`). And carriage requires that the client SDK holds the ciphertext at transaction-building time and that the chain keeps the binding, either the ciphertext itself or a `proven` commitment to it. Whether Part 2 allows either is unknown until it is published, and that requirement stays open until then.

MPS-0043 asks that a party can reveal chosen data to a chosen reader [@midnight-mps-0043]. A per-Message disclosure hands the reader the MPE Envelope, its per-Envelope encryption key `K_enc` (which opens that MPE Envelope and no other), the Publisher's verifying key, the Anchor inclusion path and the discloser's signature over the bundle; the reader opens the MPE Envelope, checks the Publisher's signature, recomputes the MPE Envelope Identifier and checks it against the Anchor (`MPE-PRV-039`). Version 1 has no per-period construction short of the stream secret, which opens every retained Message of the stream and cannot be revoked, so per-period disclosure (`MPE-PRV-040`) is unmet. The proposal specifies neither object; a scoped-disclosure MIP that maps each MPS-0043 goal is a follow-up. A reader accepts a disclosed Message only under an Anchor in Bus Registry state at a finalized block (`MPE-PRV-041`), and every object expires (`MPE-PRV-042`). The proposal maps each of MPS-0043's five goals to a requirement or to an explicit non-claim (`MPE-PRV-043`). Handing a stream secret to an auditor is a coarse, irrevocable viewing capability, the same limit MIP-0012 records for its custody viewing secret [@midnight-mip-0012].

The Foundation's proposals ask for an off-chain channel in several places. MIP-0011 tells callers to deliver coin information out of band, and MPS-0043 assigns the choice of how confidential data travels to a pending problem statement on Confidential Contract Notifications, whose text is not published [@midnight-mip-0011; @midnight-mps-0043]. MIP-0012 is the counter-case: custody discovery and recovery must work from chain data alone, so Midnight Express is not a substitute there [@midnight-mip-0012].

### Compiled contracts and their costs

The Bus Registry, the ledger-lane contract and a contract consumer exist as exploratory Compact code, compiled with toolchain 0.35.0 (language 0.27.0, ledger 9.1 release candidate, compact-runtime 0.20.0) to cost the ledger interface. The design does not depend on these contracts being final. Nothing was run on a Midnight network: no transaction was built, proved or submitted, and no proof was generated or verified. Circuit sizes are those the proving toolchain reports at compile time. A circuit is proved over a table of 2^k rows; `postAnchor`'s 152,138 rows exceed 2^17 = 131,072 and so need k = 18, room for 262,144. Proving-key size and key-generation time grow with k, so k sets the prover's cost. Fees are derived from the ledger's cost rules at genesis mainnet prices with estimated block bytes, not measured. The generated JavaScript was exercised in the Compact simulator, which shows the contract logic but not that the circuits accept the same executions. Chapter 8 describes the contracts. The circuits that bear on the requirements have these sizes and derived fees.

The compiler's cost model gives proofs of 2,800 B or 4,368 B and verifying keys of 1,351 B or 2,119 B: 2,800 B for `activateParams`, `anchorRootValid`, `prunePeriod` and the four ledger-lane circuits, 4,368 B for the other Bus Registry circuits and the consumer's `react`. These sizes are the toolchain's estimate, not a measurement. Transaction sizes and fees are derived with 4,368 B for every contract proof, an upper bound for the ledger lane; with the modelled 2,800 B the sixteen-call shape is about 55,170 B and 0.62 DUST instead of 80,260 B and 0.866 DUST (derived: 80,260 − 16 × 1,568; 0.552 + 0.045 + 0.018), and the one-call shapes keep their compute-bound fees. The verifier's cost does not grow with the circuit. The prover's does: the k = 18 proving keys are 67 to 76 MB, and key generation for one k = 18 circuit took 264 s and 1.8 GB of memory on a 6-core host. Proving times were not measured.

Six consequences for the requirement set follow from the compiler's behaviour and the ledger's rules.

**Contract events need ledger 9.** Toolchain 0.31.1, the ledger-8 toolchain, rejects `emit`. Without its two `emit` statements the Bus Registry compiles with 0.31.1, so events are its only ledger-9 dependency; the consumer needs the later toolchain for its witness-free cross-contract read. `MPE-OPS-044` covers this case: no production claim for a feature that needs ledger-9 events or cross-contract calls while the target network runs ledger 8. The proof of concept must use toolchain 0.33.0 or later (`MPE-NET-070`), the first release with `emit` and the witness-free cross-contract read. Each version of the proposal names the toolchain, language, runtime and ledger versions, as MIP-0018 does (`MPE-OPS-056`).

**Bytes cost more than hashes.** A circuit computes over field elements, large integers taken modulo a prime, not over bytes. Compact packs byte values into field elements, and every operation that needs individual bytes adds about 670 rows. Emitting a constant 256-byte payload costs 23 rows; emitting a 256-byte circuit argument costs 166,241 rows. Three chained SHA-256 Merkle-tree hashes cost 133,922 rows when the preimage is built by byte concatenation and 12,674 rows when it is a struct of byte fields, with identical digests. The consumer's 19 SHA-256 evaluations take 703,378 rows (k = 20) with byte concatenation and 77,905 rows (k = 17) with struct preimages. Two rules follow. Every protocol hash that a circuit computes takes a struct preimage of fixed-width fields (`MPE-CRY-046`), and the Bus Registry emits at most 72 non-constant payload bytes per Anchor event and 35 per governance event (`MPE-FMT-059`). The 16-part limit of `P-FMT-8` stays, set by fees, but one 16-part call is a k = 22 circuit against k = 20 for four parts, and a lower in-contract limit of four parts is the alternative unless a cheaper `Misc` path appears. The proposal states k and rows for every circuit (`MPE-PRF-042`), and an Anchor whose proving takes longer than one 60 s window fails acceptance (`MPE-PRF-043`).

**No signer and no readable clock.** With no transaction signer to check (`DEC-022`), the steward and the anchorer prove knowledge of a secret whose hash is stored in the relay entry (`MPE-NET-053`). The relay list is keyed by SHA-256 of the serialized libp2p PeerId, because an Ed25519 PeerId is a 38-byte multihash, not the 32-byte raw key (`MPE-NET-054`). A circuit can compare block time but not read it, and cannot read its own Merkle root. Every time-dependent write therefore takes a caller clock checked against the block time within 900 s (`MPE-NET-069`), and a transaction that waits longer in the pool fails. Root publication and supersession times come from the timestamps of the finalized blocks that include root-changing transactions, read by the Ledger Adapter (`MPE-ECO-027`, `MPE-NET-055`).

**Anchors have a fixed shape.** The payload is an 8-byte big-endian window number, the 32-byte window root and eight 4-byte big-endian per-Shard MPE Envelope counts, 72 B, zero-filled to 256 B whatever the Shard count; the counts of unused Shards are zero (`MPE-NET-050`). Each Anchor also writes a leaf, SHA-256 of a domain string, the window and the window root, at slot `window mod 2940` of a depth-12 historic Merkle tree, which a witness-free Bus Registry circuit checks for other contracts (`MPE-CON-047`). A reaction that uses Anchor inclusion is therefore two contract calls with two proofs.

**Large transcripts leave the guaranteed segment.** A Midnight transaction is applied in two stages. The guaranteed segment runs first, includes the fee payment and fails the whole transaction if any of it fails. Fallible segments run afterwards, and a failing one is rolled back alone while the fee stays paid. Midnight nodes must run the guaranteed segment before they can collect a fee, so its execution time is capped to keep invalid transactions cheap to dismiss.^[midnightntwrk/midnight-ledger, spec/intents-transactions.md, section "Transaction application", and spec/cost-model.md, section "Guaranteed Segment limits"; ledger-8 branch, accessed 2 October 2026.] A transcript without checkpoints goes into the guaranteed segment only if its cost fits the time-to-dismiss budget, max(2 µs per transaction byte, 15 ms) less a reserve; otherwise all of it, events included, goes into the fallible segment.^[midnightntwrk/midnight-ledger, ledger/src/construct.rs, guaranteed and fallible segment partition; ledger 9.1 release candidate, accessed 1 October 2026.] Derived from the simulator's gas, the 16-part call (27.6 ms against 25.8 ms), `register` and `postAnchor` all exceed it. The parts still share one phase, which MIP-0019 accepts, so `MPE-FMT-042` requires one phase of one intent, not the guaranteed phase, and the fee is paid even if such a call fails. This placement is derived from the partitioning code, not observed.

**Cost assumptions become derived inputs.** The registration gate (`MPE-ECO-049`), the ledger-cost report (`MPE-OPS-046`) and the ledger-operation measurement (`MPE-VER-030`) use the compiled inputs (estimated 4,368-byte proofs, generated 2,119-byte verifying keys and the simulator's gas) until a ledger-9 devnet measures them. Two capability requests would make the interface cheaper and belong in the proposal's rationale: a cheaper `Misc` emission path for public byte payloads, which dominates the prover's cost, and a way to read the block time or a contract's own Merkle root inside a circuit.

### MPE Envelope sizes

An MPE Envelope is an 8-byte fixed visible header, a 512-byte Admission Slot and a sealed body of one of four lengths, so its wire length is 8 + 512 + body: 776, 1,544, 4,616 and 16,904 B. The slot holds the RLN-style fields: an 8-byte admission window number, a 32-byte membership root, a 32-byte admission nullifier, a 32-byte share and a 256-byte proof. That is 360 B, and the remaining 152 B are zero-filled (`MPE-FMT-050`). These sizes match the Waku rate-limit proof, which carries a 256-byte uncompressed proof and 32-byte root, share and nullifier fields [@2021-vac-waku2-rln-relay-spec]. The spare bytes let a different proof encoding fit without changing the class table. Chapter 8 gives the byte layout. The wire lengths are those of the MPE Envelope carried as `Message.data`; GossipSub's protobuf and RPC framing come on top and must fit the 65,536 B frame limit, batches included.

The sealed body begins with a 44-byte clear prefix (16-byte salt, 12-byte nonce, 16-byte Recognition Tag), and the authenticated encryption adds a 16-byte tag. Inside the plaintext sit a 40-byte sealed prefix and a 70-byte authentication block (2-byte key index, 4-byte creation time, 64-byte Ed25519 signature). Payload capacity is therefore body − 44 − 16 − 40 − 70 = body − 170 B: 86, 854, 3,926 and 16,214 B. The smallest class spends 520 of its 776 wire bytes, 67 percent, on header and slot. If the slot had to grow to the 4,096-byte ceiling allowed for a real proof, class 0 would be 4,360 B with 94 percent overhead, and overhead of that size is the trigger that removes class 0.

### Load and CPU

The Shard cap is 10 MPE Envelopes per second or 65,536 B/s, whichever binds first. The byte cap binds once the mean wire size exceeds 65,536 / 10 = 6,553.6 B; a Shard carrying only class 3 reaches it at 65,536 / 16,904 = 3.88 MPE Envelopes per second. At 10 MPE Envelopes per second and the 4.5 ms verification cost, validation takes 10 × 4.5 ms = 45 ms of CPU per second, or 0.045 of a core per Shard. At the 10 ms gate set for a real proof it takes 0.10 core, inside the 0.30-core edge budget. The 4.5 ms figure is the published RLN verification time on a 4-CPU cloud machine [@2024-revuelta-waku-latency]; the proof of concept will simulate it, not measure it. A full verification queue of 128 jobs holds 128 × 16,904 B = 2.2 MB and drains in 128 × 4.5 ms = 0.58 s, inside the 3 s propagation target. Recognition at the 256-key cap costs 256 × 10 = 2,560 HMAC evaluations per second. The CPU cost of one evaluation is not derived here; Q1 of Chapter 9 measures it at 1, 32 and 256 keys against a budget of 0.5 core (`P-PRF-19`).

The per-class quota sets how many members can saturate a Shard. Counted in body bytes alone, each class allows 16,384 B per 60 s admission window, which is 273 B/s, so a Shard's 65,536 B/s would be filled by 65,536 / 273 = 240 members using one class each, or by 60 members using all four. At the registration fee of about 0.25 DUST, 240 memberships would cost about 59 DUST.

That count leaves out the cap of 10 MPE Envelopes per second, which binds first for every class but the largest. A member using only class 0 sends 64 MPE Envelopes per admission window, 64 / 60 = 1.07 per second, so 9.4 such members reach the count cap, at only 10 × 776 = 7,760 B/s. A member using all four classes sends 64 + 16 + 4 + 1 = 85 per window, 1.42 per second, so about 7 members reach it; at 64 × 776 + 16 × 1,544 + 4 × 4,616 + 16,904 = 109,736 wire bytes per member per window, they send 7 × 109,736 / 60 ≈ 12,800 B/s. Only class 3 is byte-bound: at 16,904 / 60 = 282 B/s of wire bytes per member, about 233 members fill the byte cap, before the 600 the count cap would need. A single member cannot fill a Shard, but seven to ten can, so per-member quotas only bound spam once membership costs something; ten memberships cost about 2.5 DUST.

### Bandwidth

The planning rule in the set estimates mesh egress as 1.25 × D × unique bytes per second. It is a planning assumption, not a bound that GossipSub guarantees. At the byte cap and D = 8, that is 1.25 × 8 × 65,536 B/s × 8 bit = 5.24 Mbit/s; at D = 6 it is 3.93 Mbit/s. The edge budget of 6 Mbit/s per direction was set from the D = 8 figure, and a full Bus Node with four Shards gets 4 × 5.24 = 20.97 Mbit/s plus headroom, or 24 Mbit/s. One whole-Shard stream to a Subscriber at the byte cap is 65,536 × 8 = 0.524 Mbit/s, and a Bus Node serves at most four such streams (`P-PUB-10`).

The rule is a hypothesis that Q4 of Chapter 9 tests. If D = 8 exceeds 6 Mbit/s in either direction, `P-PRF-12` rises to the measured value plus 20 percent or production adopts D = 6; if measured amplification falls below 1.25 × D, the rule overstates and the byte cap can be re-derived upward. A comparison with the rule must count the same traffic categories at the same endpoints over the same interval, because a Bus Node that also serves Subscriber feeds and repair carries traffic the rule does not model. Chapter 10 records what exploratory local runs suggested about mesh egress and about feed and repair traffic, as unreplicated observations that support none of these figures.

The Subscriber's cost is the whole Shard. At the byte cap that is 65,536 × 86,400 = 5.66 GB per day. At 10 class-1 MPE Envelopes per second it is 10 × 1,544 × 86,400 = 1.33 GB per day, and at a planning load of 10 × 2 KiB per second it is 1.77 GB per day. Reconciliation adds one inventory from the second source, 600 identifiers × 32 B per minute, or 27.6 MB per day, which is 1.6 percent of a 1.77 GB feed. Splitting into eight Shards brings one Shard to about 221 MB per day, still several times the mobile budget of 56 to 60 MB per day that the requirement set assumes for `DEC-021`. `DEC-021` rests on this arithmetic: whole-Shard reception is a desktop or always-connected profile, and sharding alone does not make it mobile.

### Storage and memory

A Store Node keeping one Shard at the byte cap for 48 hours holds 65,536 × 172,800 = 11,324,620,800 B, or 10.55 GiB. At an assumed database multiplier of two that is 21.1 GiB, inside the 32 GiB allocation. Seven days would be 65,536 × 604,800 = 39.6 GB per replica, held by each of three Bus Operators, so the longer period is an optional paid archive. A Bus Node's application seen-set holds every accepted identifier until expiry plus 60 s: 10 × (172,800 + 60) = 1,728,600 identifiers per Shard, about 124 MB at 72 B each, or about 0.5 GB for four Shards. When that state cannot be kept, the Bus Node refuses new admission and keeps every unexpired record. The admission-nullifier cache is much smaller, 10 × 140 = 1,400 entries per Shard. The GossipSub duplicate cache holds identifiers for only 60 s in the Rust configuration (`DEC-010`) and is no substitute for either. A 2 GiB resident ceiling covers the seen-set, GossipSub's caches and queues, and the process.

### The chain budget

At 1,000,000 B per 6-second block the ledger has 14,400 blocks and 14.4 GB of block usage per day. The production design takes at most 10 percent of that, 1.44 GB per day, and enforces the limit by client back-off. If the live limit is the ledger's initial 200,000 B, every figure in this paragraph that depends on block usage falls by a factor of five. Anchors at one per minute come to 1,440 a day. At the estimated 10,340 block bytes of one `postAnchor` transaction, that is 14.9 MB a day, 0.10 percent of the daily block usage and about 1 percent of the 10 percent share. If no ledger-lane traffic uses the share, the rest of the block-usage share bounds registrations: 1.44 GB − 14.9 MB = 1.425 GB, which at the 16 KiB registration gate is 1.425 × 10^9 / 16,384, about 86,980 registrations a day, and at the estimated 8,670 B of the compiled `register` call about 164,370. The `bytes_written` share binds first. The 10 percent share of the 50,000 B per-block budget is 72 MB a day, and at the simulator's 560 B of net writes per registration it holds 72 × 10^6 / 560 = 128,571 registrations, so about 128,570 a day is the ceiling at 1,000,000 B of block usage before other writers. At 200,000 B the block-usage share is 288 MB a day, and after the 14.9 MB of Anchors it holds (288 − 14.9) × 10^6 / 8,670, about 31,500 registrations a day. The Anchor payload (8-byte window, 32-byte root and eight 4-byte counts) is 72 B and fits one 256-byte `Misc` payload.

The second limit is `bytes_written`, 50,000 B per block, or 14,400 × 50,000 = 720 MB a day, of which the 10 percent share is 72 MB. MIP-0002 sizes event capacity against it at an event's serialized size, about 40 B above its struct data, so one part counts about 288 + 40 = 328 B and a block holds about 152 class-0, 38 class-1 or 9 class-2 ledger-lane MPE Envelopes: about 6.3 class-1 and 1.5 class-2 Messages per second for the whole chain with no other writers [@midnight-mip-0002]. The ledger-9 code charges a logged event as both written and deleted, so under the code an event adds nothing to `bytes_written`, and block usage binds instead: about 120, 108 and 77 lane transactions per block at 1,000,000 B, or 24, 21 and 15 at 200,000 B. Planning uses the lowest figure for each class: about 4.0 class-0, 3.5 class-1 and 1.5 class-2 Messages per second for the whole chain with no other writers, and 0.40, 0.35 and 0.15 inside the `P-ECO-8` share. The Compact simulator records 693 B written and 693 B deleted for each further `Misc` inside one call (414 B each for a one-part call): gross bytes with no net growth, which the fee model books as about 832 B of churn per part. Only a gross reading of `bytes_written` would count the 693 B, and it would roughly halve the `bytes_written` figures. All of these are derived. Measuring the serialized size, the budget charged and the fee of one part and of 1-, 4- and 16-part groups on a ledger-9 network (`MPE-PRF-040`) decides which reading governs.

Fees set the operating cost. One Anchor post costs about 0.195 DUST at genesis prices (0.244 with the wallet's margin), so one Anchor per minute costs 1,440 × 0.195 = 280.8 DUST a day for the anchoring Bus Operator. Sustaining that takes about 393 NIGHT (derived: 1,440 × 0.195 = 280.8 DUST per day; 280.8 / 0.714 DUST per NIGHT per day = 393), assuming generation runs at the full rate while the balance stays below the cap; Chapter 2 gives the generation rate. The 0.195 DUST per Anchor assumes no net state growth; each retained Anchor-tree root adds about 32 B, about 0.006 DUST per Anchor or about 9 DUST per day (derived: 32 × 0.0002; 1,440 × 0.0064), until a reset rule bounds the tree. One registration costs about 0.246 DUST (0.308), inside the 0.5 DUST gate of `P-ECO-13`. On the ledger lane, one 16-part call costs 0.188 DUST and sixteen separate part calls 0.866 DUST, 4.6 times as much; one call per MPE Envelope is the cheaper shape for fees and the more expensive one to prove. Live prices move each block by up to about 4.6 percent per dimension,^[midnightntwrk/midnight-ledger, base-crypto/src/cost_model.rs, `price_adjustment_function`; ledger 9 source, version 9.1.0.0-rc.4, commit 9a8777c, accessed 1 October 2026.] and mainnet still runs the ledger-8 parameter schema, so these figures can change before ledger 9 is deployed.

### Timing chains

Several parameters only make sense in relation to each other, and the set requires each of the following inequalities to hold.

| Chain | Arithmetic | Holds because |
|---|---|---|
| Publish retries inside admission-window tolerance | 3 Bus Nodes × 5 s = 15 s < 20 s | A retransmission started in an admission window's last second stays admissible |
| Restart barrier | 2 × 60 s admission window + 20 s tolerance = 140 s | An allowance is ineligible 20 s after its admission window ends, at most 100 s after use, so the barrier exceeds it |
| Barrier below warm-up | 140 s < 600 s | A restarted Bus Node already waits 600 s before injecting local publications |
| Gap wait above repair | 120 s > 60 s reconciliation + 10 s delivery = 70 s | A gap item is not raised for an MPE Envelope the next reconciliation fills |
| Unanchored wait | 300 s > 60 s window + 20 s close delay + 6 s inclusion + 18 s finality = 104 s | A client waits long enough for proving, indexing and margin before reporting "unanchored" |
| Anchor proving | one k = 18 proof < 60 s window | Anchors keep pace with windows (`MPE-PRF-043`); unmeasured |
| Anchor post horizon | 20 s ≤ window age < 2,940 × 60 s = 176,400 s | A post neither precedes its window's close nor overwrites a live record |
| Stale-root bound | 86,400 s period + 3,600 s window = 90,000 s (25 h) | Forwarding under cached state cannot outlive the membership period by more than an hour |
| Staleness threshold | 60 s = 10 slots, over three times the 18 s finality | A stale view is declared early, and declaring it costs little after `DEC-009` |
| Anchor history | 48 h lifetime + 1 h = 49 h × 60 = 2,940 records | No contract consumes a Message older than its lifetime |
| Clock tolerance | 900 s | A Bus Registry write waiting longer in the pool fails and must be rebuilt |
| Version overlap | 2 × 172,800 s + 30 days = 34 days | Every old-version MPE Envelope expires before its GossipSub topic closes |
| Soak duration | 48 h fill + 24 h steady state = 72 h | A Store Node only reaches steady state after one retention window |

The barrier chain's arithmetic holds, but it says nothing about what a publishing client sees while the barrier lasts. A Publisher whose Bus Node stayed silent for 140 s after a restart could not tell a refusal from a slow acceptance, so a Bus Node inside the barrier answers with an explicit Busy reply. An identical copy Ignored at that Bus Node stays suppressed only while its identifier remains in the GossipSub duplicate cache, 60 s in the Rust configuration and 120 s by Go's default. Recovery then comes from the retrieval path, another application path or a later retransmission that is still eligible. Q8 of Chapter 9 measures the benefit of that reply, running churn with a Busy reply and with silent Ignore as a control arm, and the barrier stands as specified only if delivery reaches at least 0.999 after the drain with repair carrying less than 2 percent of pairs.

## Rules that hold across areas

Twenty-six rules hold across areas. Each is stated once, in the entry that owns it and under the identifier that other areas cite, so that no two requirements give one test opposite verdicts. They fall into four kinds; the cases below are examples of each kind, not all 26.

Some rules give one test one verdict. A Bus Node with a stale chain view keeps delivering MPE Envelopes valid under cached roots and never Rejects on a chain-dependent check, so one frozen-adapter test passes `MPE-NET-018`, `MPE-FMT-031`, `MPE-SEC-035` and `MPE-OPS-021` together (`DEC-009`). A second MPE Envelope under a spent allowance is Ignored with evidence (`MPE-ECO-020`, `MPE-SEC-018`, `DEC-014`). Durable replay state is optional, and the restart barrier with the recovery condition of `MPE-STO-015` covers its absence (`MPE-SEC-015`, `DEC-017`). Contract state never holds a body, and the ledger lane's labelled `Misc` logs are permitted (`MPE-STO-001`).

Some rules give a shared quantity one value. The expired-on-arrival tolerance is one parameter, 60 s (`P-FMT-4`). Chain-view staleness has one threshold, 60 s (`P-NET-13`). Seen-set retention is expiry plus 60 s, because a Bus Node Ignores anything already expired by more than that, and longer retention protects nothing. The Shard range is 1 to 8, and the recognition key cap is 256.

Some rules define a quantity by what the party applying it can see. Retention runs to the visible expiry, because the creation time is sealed inside the body and invisible to a Store Node (`MPE-STO-003`). The membership-root acceptance window counts from the root's supersession, taken from the timestamp of the including block, so a Bus Registry with no new registrations for an hour does not make every MPE Envelope ineligible (`MPE-ECO-015`). The per-peer rate limit is 20 MPE Envelopes per second per peer per Shard, because a mesh peer on four Shards at capacity forwards up to 40 per second (`MPE-NET-023`). One Anchor covers all Shards, so the record bound holds at eight Shards (`MPE-NET-041`, `DEC-024`), and bandwidth budgets are per direction, which an aggregate reading would halve (`MPE-PRF-013`, `MPE-PRF-014`, `DEC-027`).

Some rules give an obligation one priority and one scope. Second-source reconciliation is MUST in every area (`DEC-025`), and so is the version overlap. The open-admission delivery threshold is 99.9 percent, because removing a safety restriction should meet the production service level. Tombstone handling has production scope, because tombstones are disabled in the prototype.

Twenty-eight entries state obligations that one area relies on and no other area owns. Examples: the Admission Slot field layout and zero fill, the clear prefix of the sealed body, a check that the visible Shard byte matches the GossipSub topic, the inventory and receipt request services that reconciliation needs, the Anchor payload, batch-root construction and inclusion-path service that the "final" label needs, anchorer authorization, the Bus Node memory ceiling, a closed set of outcome names, a carrier attribute, and one end-to-end prototype scenario that ties the stages together. Twenty-six of the 28 are needed for the proof of concept to run end to end, and 27 are in the core. The one outside the core is anchorer authorization (`MPE-NET-053`), which has production scope.

Further rules follow from the layers beneath the set: the GossipSub specifications and implementations, Midnight's event proposals and the compiled ledger interface. The GossipSub message identifier is the full-byte identifier and differs from the MPE Envelope Identifier (`MPE-PUB-026`, `MPE-FMT-019`, `MPE-NET-010`, `DEC-010`). A version mismatch on a supported GossipSub topic is Rejected, and an unsupported protocol context is Ignored (`MPE-FMT-023`, `MPE-SEC-008`, `DEC-015`). The application seen-set runs to expiry plus 60 s, independently of the GossipSub duplicate cache (`MPE-STO-013`). The quota is per Bus Node (`MPE-ECO-022`). A ledger-lane MPE Envelope is 4^c `Misc` parts (`MPE-NET-044`, `MPE-FMT-041`). Anchors are verified against Bus Registry state, and ledger-lane bodies only against finalized chain data from independent sources, because events are not consensus state (`MPE-CON-016`, `MPE-SEC-022`) [@midnight-mip-0002]. Governance emits `mip-xxxx:governance[v1]`, because the Bus Registry emits only `Misc` events (`MPE-OPS-018`, `MPE-NET-040`). Where a contract cannot act, three ledger requirements state what the Bus Registry, the transaction and the Bus Nodes provide: the price of a membership is the network fee of its registration transaction, because a contract cannot receive DUST (`MPE-ECO-004`); ledger-lane parts share one phase of one intent, not the guaranteed phase (`MPE-FMT-042`); and Bus Nodes, not the contract, enforce the root window (`MPE-ECO-015`).

## Threats and defences

The threat area (D9, `SEC`) names the attacks that matter. Other areas own most of its defences, which is why only 20 of its 42 entries are live. The privacy claims being defended are the limited ones of Chapter 4: content confidentiality and conditional interest privacy against infrastructure. Nothing here defends timing or relationship privacy, or anything against a global observer. Each attack meets a defence in the requirement set and leaves a residual risk.

The set names several attacks that it does not stop. The design makes no claim against a global passive observer, who watches every network link at once and can correlate publish and receive times at the network edge. It makes no claim about relationship privacy: a Subscriber's reaction, if it is on-chain, is public. It does not defend against the governance body or an admission issuer acting maliciously. Those parties can censor or grant capacity, and the set's controls (time-locks, emergency expiry, published tombstones) make such acts visible without making them impossible. It does not prove that delivery is complete. Matching inventories and Anchors show that two sources agree, not that every Message was included, and expiry removes nothing that an adversarial store recorded. Interest privacy holds only if the whole client behaves uniformly: a selective request, an error path, a repair or a diagnostic export that depends on recognition reveals it even when the main feed is uniform. Admission privacy is unknown, because the stand-in exposes membership secrets to relays and no production proof profile has been selected.

The transport adds its own open risks. The peer-score cap of 32 applies after positive contributions and invalid-message penalties are summed, so a peer rewarded on several Shards can absorb invalid deliveries on one. Each GossipSub topic contributes at most 30 (10 for time in the mesh, 20 for first deliveries), and eight maximally rewarded topics sum to 240 before the cap. Score safety therefore needs tests across every supported Shard configuration. The IP-colocation penalty (P6), which lowers the score of peers that share an IP address, can hit honest peers behind shared addresses, and development exemptions do not define a production policy.

Interoperation between rust-libp2p and go-libp2p-pubsub is unestablished: default version lists, Go's outbound-quota check, IDONTWANT predicates and Peer Exchange support all differ. Rust emits IDONTWANT for a serialized message strictly longer than the threshold, while Go compares payload length, sends at equality and defaults to 1,024 B. In Peer Exchange a pruning peer suggests other peers to try, and rust-libp2p's Peer Exchange supplies peer identifiers but no signed addresses. The savings of a lower IDONTWANT threshold, and of IDONTWANT on wide-area links, are unknown.

Finally, the score vulnerability CVE-2022-47547, found by formal analysis of GossipSub's score function [@2023-kumar-gossipsub-acl2s], has no documented fix in rust-libp2p 0.50, and its closure for the complete score profile is unknown. `MPE-SEC-040` blocks a public-overlay release until a fix or a reviewed non-applicability argument exists for the chosen score parameters.

## Decisions still open

All 28 decisions have a default that the proof of concept will implement, so none blocks it. A decision counts as open when its production answer depends on evidence not yet gathered. Six are settled by argument: `DEC-010` (the wire-byte identifier, from the order in which rust-libp2p fills its duplicate cache), `DEC-014` (Ignore with evidence, because the forwarding peer may be honest), `DEC-015` (version bound to the GossipSub topic), `DEC-025` (mandatory reconciliation, because sequence gaps cannot reveal suppression of a whole Publisher), `DEC-026` (three attributes that vary independently) and `DEC-027` (per-direction budgets). Q6 and Q9 of Chapter 9 still test `DEC-010` and `DEC-014`, and a failure there would change them. `DEC-016` holds in the per-Bus-Node form given above, because independent Bus Nodes can accept conflicting first uses before they exchange them (`MPE-ECO-022`). `DEC-017` has a Bus Node inside the restart barrier answer a publication with an explicit Busy reply, a service reply and not a GossipSub validation outcome; Q8 measures its benefit against silent Ignore. What a contract cannot do (check a signer, read the block time, read its own Merkle root, receive DUST) fixes the shape of the Bus Registry, the Anchor store and the consumer's expiry check, and narrows `DEC-022`. Each decision that remains open names the evidence that would settle it.

The exploratory contracts bear on two of these. For `DEC-007`, the compiler's cost model gives the compiled circuits proofs of 2,800 B or 4,368 B; the 4,368 B of `revoke`, which checks the rate-limiting relation and a depth-20 Merkle path on chain, is a contract proof, not the off-chain Admission Proof that the slot carries. A production slot is `W = roundUp64(104 + proof length)`, capped at 4,096 B (`P-ECO-11`), so the largest admissible proof is 3,992 B. A 2,800 B proof gives `W` = 2,944 B, inside the cap but above 1,024 B, so class 0 would leave the production table (`DEC-013`); a 4,368 B proof gives `W` = 4,480 B, above the cap. No admission circuit is written, so which applies is open. The verifier side looks affordable: the ledger's cost parameters charge 3.27 ms plus 4.56 µs per public input to verify a proof, and 1.53 ms to load a verifying key,^[midnightntwrk/midnight-node, res/mainnet/ledger-parameters-config.json, proof verification cost parameters; main branch, accessed 1 October 2026.] although these are charging rules, not measurements.

For `DEC-022`, the inclusion-only consumer compiles at k = 17 but gives no authority, so the signature profile is the production default. In the simulator, `persistentHash` over a struct of byte fields equals plain SHA-256 of the concatenated bytes, so a circuit can verify the RFC 6962 batch roots, Merkle roots built with the separate leaf and node prefixes of Certificate Transparency, without a new identifier scheme. Whether the circuits accept the same executions as the simulator remains to be shown by proving them.

Two parameters stay open until the proof of concept runs. The reconciliation interval `P-PUB-3` defaults to 60 s, and when a Bus Node drops a copy that interval bounds how long a Subscriber waits for repair. Experiment 5 of Chapter 11 runs churn with reconciliation at 5, 15 and 60 s, with and without a Busy reply, and passes an interval only if at least 99.9 percent of pairs arrive within 10 s while gateway bytes stay near baseline. Chapter 10 records what the exploratory runs suggested about the interval, as an unreplicated observation. The bandwidth planning rule is the other: Q4 of Chapter 9 measures mesh egress and amplification by traffic category, which decides both the rule and whether feed and repair service needs a budget of its own.
