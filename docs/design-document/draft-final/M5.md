## Security Considerations

### Threat model and privacy boundary

A Message passes through several parties, and the threat model separates what each observes from what it can change. A Publisher seals the Message into an MPE Envelope (an 8-byte visible header, an Admission Slot carrying the proof of the right to publish, and a sealed body) and submits it to a Bus Node, a relay that forwards MPE Envelopes over GossipSub. Traffic is partitioned into Shards, each with its own GossipSub topic; there is one Shard at launch. Store Nodes retain MPE Envelopes for 48 hours. The Bus Registry contract holds memberships, the relay roster and parameters, and it records Anchors, each of which commits to the identifiers of the MPE Envelopes accepted in one 60-second window. The ledger lane publishes a sealed body as contract events, and only when an application selects it.

A single Bus Node observes ingress connections, MPE Envelopes and forwarding times. The ingress, the Bus Node to which a Publisher submits an MPE Envelope, also learns the submitting endpoint's identity and network address from the authenticated libp2p connection; with Identify enabled, it further learns the endpoint's public key, listen addresses, supported protocols and implementation metadata. A Store Node observes retrieval windows, cursors and download volume. An Indexer, a service that reads chain data and serves it to wallets and DApps [@midnight-mps-0028], observes queries and public ledger activity. A chain observer sees Bus Registry changes, Anchors, ledger-lane publications and public contract effects. Colluding infrastructure combines these observations.

Infrastructure can delay, omit or refuse what passes through it, but a changed sealed body fails authenticated opening at the Subscriber. An active adversary can inject, replay, delay, withhold, partition or eclipse traffic, acquire publication allowances and create relay identities, and a compromised endpoint or authorized insider can disclose its keys and plaintext. A relay identity is the libp2p peer identity under which the Bus Registry relay roster lists a forwarding Bus Node with its Bus Operator. The Bus Registry does not limit how many relay identities one Bus Operator holds. Bus Operator share, relay-identity share, bandwidth and purchased allowances are therefore separate adversarial resources (`MPE-PRV-016` to `MPE-PRV-023`, `MPE-SEC-039`).

Midnight Express claims four properties for a Confidential Message. Content confidentiality means that only holders of the decryption secrets can read the payload; it assumes uncompromised decryption secrets and authenticated key establishment. Sealed labels means that no stream name, recipient address or session identifier appears outside the sealed body. Each MPE Envelope has one of four size classes, 0 to 3, with sealed bodies of 256, 1,024, 4,096 and 16,384 B, and equal lengths within a class also conceal payload length, although the size class itself remains visible. Subscriber-interest privacy is conditional. It holds only for a client that receives every MPE Envelope of its Shards and recognizes its Messages locally. No silent change means that no retrieval path, overload path, ledger lane or application reaction changes what infrastructure learns without an application opt-in.

Recognition works per stream, an application channel between a Publisher and its Subscribers. A stream's recognition key is derived from its 32-byte stream secret, which is shared by authenticated invitation. Each MPE Envelope carries a 16-byte Recognition Tag computed from a fresh salt and that key, so a relay without the key sees different bytes in every MPE Envelope, while a client recomputes the Recognition Tag under each key it holds. Interest privacy compares executions with different recognition keys while the received Shard set, incoming traffic, online schedules and public application actions are held fixed. The client MUST keep its recognition secrets (recognition keys, stream secrets, decryption secrets and wallet viewing secrets) local. Recognition MUST NOT change subscriptions, infrastructure requests, acknowledgements, retries, errors, repair, receipts or diagnostics (`MPE-PRV-002`, `MPE-PRV-003`, `MPE-PRV-005` to `MPE-PRV-008`, `MPE-PUB-012`).

Run one client twice over the same day, once with the recognition key of one stream and once with that of another. The property holds only if its Bus Nodes and Store Nodes see the same subscriptions, requests and retries in both runs. A client that fetched receipts only for the Messages it recognized would reveal its stream to its Store Node, with every MPE Envelope still sealed.

GossipSub does not prove this property. GossipSub topic names disclose the network prefix, the protocol major version and Shard membership. A Bus Node advertises the message identifiers it holds in IHAVE, a neighbour requests missing ones with IWANT, and on first receipt a Bus Node sends IDONTWANT so that mesh peers skip the message. These control messages therefore expose which identifiers a Bus Node holds and when it obtained them. With one launch Shard no Shard is distinguished from another, yet participation and traffic are still visible. Keeping these properties distinct avoids treating encryption as metadata anonymity [@2019-kuhn-privacynotions].

At launch Midnight Express provides no publisher anonymity, no timing, volume or relationship privacy, and no anonymity against a global observer, and it gives no numerical bound on what a colluding minority of Bus Operators can learn. Shard membership, size class, expiry, participation and public reactions remain observable. StrictNoSign removes only the pubsub author stamp. Separate sidecar keys (`MPE-NET-005`) avoid key reuse but do not stop correlation with a co-located Midnight node through addresses or traffic. Recipient-key hiding, forward secrecy, post-compromise security, deniability and post-quantum security are not launch claims, and retention expiry cannot erase adversarial archives (`MPE-PRV-004`, `MPE-PRV-021`, `MPE-PRV-024` to `MPE-PRV-029`, `MPE-CRY-026`, `MPE-CRY-031`). Any anonymity property beyond these requires a separately reviewed profile. The anonymity trilemma states necessary tradeoffs within its model and gives no security guarantee for these parameters [@2017-das-trilemma].

Giving a stream secret to an auditor or any other third party grants a coarse, irrevocable viewing capability over every retained or archived Message of that stream. That capability is comparable to the custody viewing capability of MIP-0012 [@midnight-mip-0012], and the client releases it only on explicit application authorization (`MPE-PRV-015`).

Midnight Express does not define private events and does not replace them. In the Foundation's documents, a private event is a contract event emitted by a Compact circuit, some of whose fields are encrypted for chosen recipients and bound to the transaction by a commitment that the proof checks. Private events are planned for Part 2 of MPS-0005, whose text is not published [@midnight-mps-0005]. Such an event is authenticated to the contract and entry point that emitted it [@midnight-mps-0043], and it remains the contract author's claim [@midnight-mip-0002]. A Confidential Message is authenticated differently, by the Publisher's signature inside the seal, and an Anchor over identifiers commits it. No contract executed it, so nothing in Midnight Express can stand in for "the contract emitted this field". A carried event, the unchanged bytes of a contract event sealed with its transaction hash and position, is labelled `gossip` until those bytes match the finalized transcript; it thereby keeps the binding to its own transaction transcript. Midnight Express adds early, private delivery and interest hiding, but no authority.

The default retrieval profile is strict. Under it a client fetches whole Shard windows and matches Recognition Tags locally, so its Store Node or Indexer learns which windows it fetched but not which items concern it. The indexer-side topic filtering and per-wallet relevance planned in MPS-0005, and the prefix filters of MIP-0002, correspond to reduced-privacy selective profiles, because the filtering party learns which items concern which client. The client enables such a profile only after an explicit application opt-in (`MPE-PUB-016`, `MPE-PRV-013`). What an MPS-0005 topic filter would learn is unknown until Part 2 is published. Messages cannot be recovered from chain data alone, so Midnight Express MUST NOT be offered as a substitute where a standard requires discovery and recovery from chain data, as MIP-0012 R5 requires for custody [@midnight-mip-0012].

### Admission limitations

A publication allowance is one credit of a Bus Registry membership: the right to publish one MPE Envelope of one size class in one admission window. Every use of a credit carries the same admission nullifier, a domain-separated hash of the member's admission secret, the window, the class and the credit index (`MPE-ECO-017`). Independent validators cannot enforce a network-wide admission bound without serializing the consumption of allowances, and mesh connectivity alone yields no such bound. Each Bus Node checks an allowance against its own local acceptance state. If one class-3 allowance is presented at k ingress Bus Nodes at once, each finds it unused and accepts its copy, so up to k conflicting MPE Envelopes are accepted and forwarded before any Bus Node holds two of them. Evidence of the conflict arrives after forwarding, and Q9 under Testing measures how far such conflicts spread.

This MIP therefore states `MPE-ECO-022` as a per-Bus-Node bound, not a network-wide one. The MPE Envelope identifier (EID) excludes the Admission Slot, the fixed-width field (512 B at the prototype parameter value) that carries the Admission Proof and the rate-limit share, so it is proof-independent. Each Bus Node MUST accept at most one proof-independent EID per live publication allowance under its local acceptance state. It MUST enforce the membership's class limits of `(64, 16, 4, 1)` per 60-second admission window, suppress locally observed conflicting use, retain verified evidence and emit `AdmissionConflict`. Bus Nodes MUST disseminate equivocation evidence, and clients MUST retain at most one MPE Envelope per visible admission nullifier (`MPE-ECO-016`, `MPE-ECO-020` to `MPE-ECO-022`, `MPE-SEC-018`, `MPE-SEC-019`). Evidence supports revocation through the Bus Registry (`MPE-ECO-028`). It neither establishes a globally agreed winner nor retracts delivered copies.

The evidence follows the rate-limit relation of RLN-v2 [@2024-vac-rln-v2-spec]. For one admission window, class and credit index, the admission secret fixes a line, and each MPE Envelope carries one point on it, at a position derived from its EID. One share does not determine the line. Two shares with distinct EIDs under one admission nullifier do, and they recover the secret, which identifies the member.

The exploratory Bus Registry contract compiles a revocation circuit (k = 17, as defined under Ledger interface evidence; 77,971 rows in the circuit model). It accepts two shares under one admission window, class and credit index that lie on the member's line with distinct EIDs, together with the member's Merkle path, and it discloses the membership period, the root, the leaf index, the identity commitment and a reason code. No steward action is involved (`MPE-OPS-015`). The circuit expresses the rate-limit relation in the native field with `transientHash`, the form an in-circuit admission proof on Midnight would use. The stand-in Admission Proof specified for devnet and testnet uses SHA-256 and curve25519 scalars instead, so its shares do not satisfy this circuit's relation, and end-to-end revocation from evidence it produces has not been demonstrated.

A production Admission Proof must show a Bus Node that the Publisher holds a current membership and an unused allowance without revealing which member it is. The stand-in does not. Bus Nodes receive members' admission secrets, can link publications to members and can forge admission authorizations. The stand-in therefore provides no admission privacy, no admission unlinkability and no forgery resistance against Bus Nodes, and the 4.5 ms verification cost assumed for it is synthetic. The stand-in Admission Proof MUST be restricted to devnet and testnet. Production requires a separately selected, measured and independently reviewed proof profile (`MPE-PRV-018`, `MPE-PRV-030`, `MPE-ECO-048`, `MPE-CRY-040`, `MPE-VER-037`), and three constraints govern that selection.

The first is size. Proofs from Midnight's own proof system are 2,800 B (one lookup) to 4,368 B (three lookups) in the circuit model of the exploratory Bus Registry contracts, so none fits the 512 B prototype Admission Slot. The production slot width is the measured proof fields rounded up to a multiple of 64 B, at most 4,096 B (the slot ceiling, P-ECO-11), and a three-lookup proof of 4,368 B already exceeds that ceiling. A slot wider than 1,024 B removes class 0 from the size-class table (P-FMT-1, `DEC-013`). Even at 1,024 B, the class-0 wire grows from 8 + 512 + 256 = 776 B to 8 + 1,024 + 256 = 1,288 B (header, slot and body), of which header and slot take 1,032 B, about 80%. Whether a Midnight-native admission proof fits that ceiling is unknown until its circuit is written and measured.

The second is hash stability. Midnight does not guarantee that `transientHash` stays stable across upgrades, so the selected profile MUST use a hash that Midnight commits to keeping stable. The revocation circuit above uses `transientHash` and therefore shows only that the relation compiles.

The third is key separation. The admission secret MUST remain independent of every wallet, encryption, signing and session key (`MPE-ECO-006`, `MPE-SEC-029`). It MUST NOT be sent to a prover outside the client's trust boundary unless that prover is attested, because a remote prover receives the private witness [@midnight-mps-0004].

### Bus Registry and ledger-lane limitations

The Bus Registry, ledger-lane and consumer contracts exist as exploratory Compact code. They were compiled with toolchain 0.35.0 (language 0.27.0, ledger 9.1 release candidate) and costed against the ledger cost model, and the design does not depend on them being final. Each ledger-lane contract event carries one 256-byte part of a sealed body, without header or Admission Slot. Classes 0 to 2 take 1, 4 and 16 parts; a class-3 body of 16,384 B would need 64, above the 16-part limit (P-FMT-8). The consumer contract lets a contract act once on an anchored Message. None of the contracts ran on a Midnight network, and every fee below is derived, not measured. Compiling them exposed seven limits that bear on security.

Authority is a hash preimage. A Midnight transaction has no signer, so the steward and the anchorer prove in circuit that they know a secret whose SHA-256 is stored in Bus Registry state (`MPE-NET-053`). Whoever holds the steward secret controls the relay roster, parameter proposals and the pause flag; whoever holds an anchorer secret can post Anchors for that relay. Parameter changes take effect after a time-lock fixed at deployment (`MPE-OPS-012`), and the exploratory contracts set it to zero. Pause blocks registration and Anchor posting, but revocation and pruning stay open.

Time can be compared but not read, and every comparison is public. Each time-dependent write takes a caller-supplied clock value that the contract checks against block time within a tolerance (900 s in the exploratory contracts), so a transaction that waits longer than that fails. A consumer that compared the signed expiry with block time would publish the expiry. The consumer circuit therefore discloses only a coarse bound chosen by the prover and checks the real bound privately (`MPE-CON-046`).

The historic-root check has no time bound. The contract accepts every past membership root, so Bus Nodes, not the contract, enforce the 3,600 s root window (P-ECO-5, `MPE-ECO-015`). Publication and supersession times come from the timestamps of the including blocks, which the Ledger Adapter reads (`MPE-NET-055`).

DUST cannot be paid to a contract. The price of a membership is the network fee of the registration transaction: about 0.246 DUST (0.308 with wallet margin) at genesis prices, derived. A price above that fee needs NIGHT or another token (`MPE-ECO-036` covers unshielded deposits). Bonds, where used, are Bus Registry contract balances and have no relation to NIGHT staking.

Inclusion is not authority. A consumer circuit that proves only Anchor inclusion, and derives a consumption nullifier from a private secret, binds neither the inclusion nor the consumption nullifier to the Message, and a holder of the inclusion path could then react several times with different secrets. Authority requires the Publisher signature of `MPE-CON-043` (`DEC-022` option (a)). That circuit has not been compiled. In-circuit Ed25519 needs ZKIR v3, the third version of Compact's circuit intermediate representation, and MIP-0013 rejects it for cost [@midnight-mip-0013]. A JubJub Schnorr profile is the alternative to measure.

Large transcripts leave the guaranteed segment. A Midnight transaction applies its guaranteed phase first, and a fallible phase can then fail while the guaranteed effects stay applied [@midnight-mip-0019]. The placement is derived from the cost model's gas figures for the compiled circuits and from the ledger's partitioning code, not observed.^[midnightntwrk/midnight-ledger, ledger/src/construct.rs, partition_transcripts; ledger 9.1.0.0-rc.4, accessed 2 October 2026.] A 16-part ledger-lane call, a registration and an Anchor post each exceed the guaranteed-segment budget and land in the fallible segment. All parts of one MPE Envelope still lie in one phase of one intent (`MPE-FMT-042`), which MIP-0019 accepts [@midnight-mip-0019]. Because a failed fallible phase discards its events, readers MUST count parts only from a phase that succeeded.

`emit` needs ledger 9. It produces a `Misc` contract event, which carries a 32-byte name and a 256-byte payload. With its two `emit` statements removed, the Bus Registry compiles with the ledger-8 toolchain 0.31.1, but the `Misc` copies of Anchors and the ledger lane are made of `emit` statements and cannot be compiled for ledger 8. While the target network runs ledger 8, Midnight Express makes no production claim for a feature that needs ledger-9 contract events or cross-contract calls (`MPE-OPS-044`).

### Attacks, defences and residual risks

Several defences below act through GossipSub v1.1 peer scoring [@2020-gossipsub-v11-spec]. Its per-topic components include P3, a penalty for a mesh peer that delivers fewer messages than expected; P3b, which keeps that deficit after the peer is pruned; and P4, a negatively weighted count of invalid messages. Their weighted sum across topics is capped by the topic score cap before the global components are added: P5, an application-specific score, and P6, a penalty for many peers behind one IP address. The profile sets P3 and P3b to zero weight and replaces them with load-aware withholding eviction, under which a Bus Node prunes a mesh peer that delivered nothing, or only Rejected MPE Envelopes, for 90 consecutive heartbeats in which the Shard carried accepted traffic (`MPE-NET-022`).

Application validation ends in one of three outcomes. Accept permits forwarding and makes the forwarding peer eligible for accepted-delivery rewards. Reject suppresses delivery and applies P4 to the forwarding peer and to tracked duplicate forwarders, none of whom need be the Publisher. Ignore suppresses delivery and applies no P4; it earns no reward either, and it does not rule out delivery-deficit effects.

Each MPE Envelope has two identifiers because of one attack. If the transport identifier excluded the Admission Slot, an adversary could alter an honest MPE Envelope's slot and forward that copy first. A neighbour would cache the shared identifier, reject the altered copy and then drop the honest copy as a duplicate.

The GossipSub message identifier MUST be the 32-byte value `SHA-256(UTF8("mpe/v1/msgid") || envelope_bytes)`. Here `envelope_bytes` is the complete, immutable MPE Envelope carried as `Message.data`, including the Admission Slot; it excludes protobuf framing, the transport topic, RPC batching, and Noise and Yamux framing. Every implementation MUST hash the same bytes and use no transform that changes them (`MPE-NET-010`). This identifier differs from the EID, which excludes the Admission Slot and serves admission, inventories, application deduplication and Anchors. The duplicate cache holds the GossipSub message identifiers already received. The message cache holds recent and pending messages and answers IWANT. The application seen-set holds accepted EIDs and MUST record an EID only after Accept (`MPE-SEC-041`). Because the slot is hashed, a copy with a corrupted Admission Slot cannot suppress the honest copy through the duplicate cache, assuming collision resistance. The full-byte hash closes that one attack and guarantees nothing else about delivery.

Implementations MUST apply the controls below within their stated scope. Every required defence leaves a residual risk, and the identifiers name the requirements that carry each defence.

In gossipsub 0.50.0, Peer Exchange exchanges peer identifiers only. It does not process signed peer-record addresses, and its default `prune_peers = 0` supplies no candidates. The zero-degree bootstrapper profile (`MPE-NET-024`), in which a bootstrapper serves discovery without relaying, is untested. A P5 application score of −20 does not guarantee pruning, because positive topic contributions can offset it. P6 can penalize honest peers behind a shared address.

The library does some work before application validation: it computes the identifier, inserts it into its duplicate cache, sends IDONTWANT and updates delivery bookkeeping. Only payload forwarding waits for Accept.^[libp2p/rust-libp2p, protocols/gossipsub/src/behaviour.rs, Behaviour::handle_received_message and Behaviour::report_message_validation_result; accessed 2 October 2026.] Implementations MUST complete validation before the pending message leaves the message cache and MUST check the result of `report_message_validation_result`. A cache miss, absent recipients or a failed publication queue is not successful propagation (`MPE-NET-015`). Local publication requests MUST pass the same application checks before `publish` is called.

Reject penalizes forwarders, so honest relays can be penalized for invalid traffic that someone else made. Reject is therefore reserved for failures that do not depend on chain state, under a supported topic version (`MPE-NET-016`). An MPE Envelope whose validity cannot be determined, because the Bus Node does not support its protocol context, produces Ignore, and Bus Nodes MUST NOT subscribe to Midnight Express GossipSub topics of an unsupported version. Membership-root checks depend on chain state and end in Ignore. A root becomes ineligible 3,600 s after it is superseded (`MPE-ECO-015`) or after its membership period ends; with the 24-hour period, cached-root eligibility therefore ends after 25 hours (`MPE-ECO-051`). A check against the local clock, such as an expiry too far in the future, yields Reject only while the chain view is fresh. A view whose latest finalized block lies more than 60 s from the local clock is stale and cannot justify Reject, and every failure that depends on chain state or the clock then produces Ignore (`MPE-NET-018`, `MPE-NET-019`).

Local overload, the restart barrier and stale authorization state MUST produce Ignore without P4, and publication ingress MUST return the applicable `Busy` or `Refused` response (`MPE-NET-018`, `MPE-SEC-009`, `MPE-STO-015`, `MPE-NET-049`). Busy and Refused are Midnight Express service replies, not GossipSub validator outcomes. `Accepted{eid}` means only that the named ingress completed application acceptance and committed its acceptance state. It proves no remote receipt, delivery, storage durability or finality, and enqueue failures are reported separately (`MPE-PUB-022`).

An identical gossip copy that was ignored during overload stays suppressed at that Bus Node while its identifier remains in the duplicate cache, since neither Reject nor Ignore removes the duplicate-cache entry. The rust-libp2p default lifetime is 60 s; the go-libp2p-pubsub default is 120 s.^[libp2p/rust-libp2p, protocols/gossipsub/src/config.rs, Config::default (duplicate_cache_time); libp2p/go-libp2p-pubsub, pubsub.go, TimeCacheDuration; accessed 2 October 2026.] Recovery comes through the Midnight Express retrieval path, or through a retransmission that is still eligible after the duplicate-cache entry expires. Clients therefore MUST reconcile from a second Bus Operator from the start of reception (`MPE-PUB-014`).

### Open transport risks

Nine risks remain open after every control above, and each is an acceptance obligation:

- Scoring safety is unknown. Replacing P3 and P3b with load-aware withholding eviction adds a mechanism that needs its own evidence.
- The pinned dependency is not fully StrictNoSign. The vendored gossipsub 0.50.0 decoder omits the `key` check that 0.51.0 adds.^[libp2p/rust-libp2p, protocols/gossipsub/CHANGELOG.md, entry 0.51.0 (rejection of `key` under `ValidationMode::Anonymous`); accessed 2 October 2026.] Citations of 0.51 source do not establish how 0.50 behaves.
- Compatibility across GossipSub implementations is unestablished. The Rust and Go libraries differ in standard versus custom identifiers, default version lists, Go's outbound-quota check, IDONTWANT predicates and Peer Exchange capabilities.
- Prevalidation state can suppress later traffic. Hashing the complete bytes addresses corrupted-slot collisions, but cache poisoning through other collisions, cross-topic handling, implementation defects or configured transforms remains a risk.
- First-hop and timing exposure remain, because no compliant GossipSub configuration removes the ingress's view of the submitter or the neighbours' view of possession times.
- Whole-Shard privacy depends on the whole client: selective receipt requests, error handling, repair, automatic external actions or diagnostic exports can reveal recognition even when the main feed is uniform.
- Retention and completeness depend on honest application services, since GossipSub cannot prove that every MPE Envelope existed, reached a store or can still be retrieved, and expiry cannot remove recorded ciphertext or metadata.
- IDONTWANT savings at any threshold are unknown. A threshold below class 0 could reduce data amplification or raise control cost, and wide-area links, workload mix and peer versions change the result (Q5).
- No transport compliance result establishes release security; regression closure, complete privacy-game evidence, sustained resource behaviour, wide-area performance and production-scale resilience are separate acceptance obligations.

## Testing

### Conformance and interoperability

Before multi-node acceptance, implementations MUST reproduce byte-exact vectors and their expected outcomes (`MPE-FMT-049`, `MPE-VER-004`). The vectors and fixtures MUST be published in the proposal's resource directory (`mips/mip-xxxx/`); they are not yet published. The named conformance cases include:

- `wire::vectors_roundtrip` and `wire::eid_ignores_slot`: four wire lengths of 776, 1,544, 4,616 and 16,904 B. Changing only the admission serialization preserves the EID and changes the GossipSub message identifier. A message-identifier vector fixes the exact hash input.
- `seal::class_edges` and `seal::too_large`: payload boundaries at 86/87, 854/855 and 3,926/3,927 B. A 16,215 B payload fails without publication.
- `seal::aad_tamper`, `seal::nonzero_pad_discarded`, `tag::vectors` and `seal::statement_field_substitution`: authentication rejects altered context, padding and statement fields, and Recognition Tag vectors are reproduced exactly.
- `admission::slot_vectors`, `admission::epoch_boundaries`, `validator::class2_limit`, `validator::equivocation_ignore_and_evidence` and `validator::outcome_table`: exact field offsets, admission-window boundaries, quota enforcement, and forwarding and penalty outcomes, including Ignore for an unsupported protocol context.
- `node::corrupted_slot_front_run`, `anchor::rfc6962_vectors`, `fallback::part_vectors` and `fallback::interleaved_intents`: delivery of the honest copy, independently reproduced roots, ledger-lane opening compatible with the carrier, and separate multipart groups.
- Transport decoder cases: a received message carrying any of `from`, `seqno`, `signature` or `key`, including an explicitly empty field, is dropped (`MPE-NET-009`). The pinned 0.50.0 decoder fails the `key` case until the 0.51.0 behaviour is applied.
- Expiry: no expired MPE Envelope is sent, including in IWANT responses and queued sends (`MPE-FMT-032`). The transport needs cache and send handling that is aware of expiry.
- Subscription filtering: Bus Nodes accept subscription names only for supported major versions, the selected genesis prefix and current or transitional Shards authorized by the Bus Registry, under count limits. The default count-limited allow-all filter fails this case. Partial-message and other extensions that change behaviour stay disabled (`MPE-NET-006`, `MPE-NET-031`).
- Ledger lane: the reader reproduces every normative vector of MIP-0019, so that it can count as one of the two independent readers that MIP-0019's acceptance requires. Lane parts can land in the fallible segment, so the publisher MUST also test fallible success, fallible failure and the mixed-phase counterexample, as MIP-0019 requires of any publisher that supports fallible publication [@midnight-mip-0019].
- Carried events: the reserved schema identifier, verbatim contract-event bytes with transaction hash and position, decoding only with the Foundation's decoder, the `gossip` label until the transcript matches, the fallible-phase success check, and sealed `@topic` values.

Primitive checks use the RFC 5869, RFC 4231, RFC 8439 and RFC 8032 vectors, for HKDF, HMAC-SHA-256, ChaCha20-Poly1305 and Ed25519 (`MPE-CRY-001`). Wrong-key, nonce-reuse, restored-state and unauthorized-signature cases accompany the positive vectors.

Interoperability needs a second implementation, developed separately from the proof of concept. Before Active, the two MUST exchange all supported classes in both directions and MUST reproduce the same decoded Messages, identifiers, validation outcomes, inventory windows and Anchor roots (`MPE-FMT-049`, `MPE-VER-004`, `MPE-VER-011`). The mixed network also exercises back-fill, failover and ledger-lane deduplication. Implementations that each succeed alone do not establish interoperability.

The transport profile requires GossipSub v1.2 behaviour: v1.1 scoring and mesh rules plus IDONTWANT, the one control message that v1.2 adds. The v1.2 specification is a Working Draft, revision r2.^[libp2p/specs, pubsub/gossipsub/gossipsub-v1.2.md, lifecycle table (Working Draft, r2, 30 August 2026); accessed 2 October 2026.] A connection to a v1.1 peer is compatible but provides no IDONTWANT. The MIP profile requires negotiating `/meshsub/1.2.0`, and a default offer list does not establish this, because the defaults of rust-libp2p gossipsub 0.50.0 and of go-libp2p-pubsub also offer v1.3 and earlier versions.^[libp2p/rust-libp2p, protocols/gossipsub/src/protocol.rs, ProtocolConfig::default; libp2p/go-libp2p-pubsub, gossipsub_feat.go, GossipSubDefaultProtocols; accessed 2 October 2026.] Negotiation tests MUST record the negotiated identifier and the internal peer kind and MUST show IDONTWANT working.

Stock rust-libp2p gossipsub 0.50 cannot reach v1.2 behaviour behind a custom protocol identifier. Its `protocol_id` accepts only `V1_0` or `V1_1`, and the string does not select the internal version, so selecting `V1_1` with a name ending in `1.2.0` leaves IDONTWANT disabled.^[libp2p/rust-libp2p, protocols/gossipsub/src/config.rs, ConfigBuilder::protocol_id and Version; accessed 2 October 2026.] Stock 0.50 supports the standard `/meshsub/1.2.0` with IDONTWANT, and a conforming implementation pins that identifier.

Go interoperability under the selected mesh tuple has not been established. The tuple gives the target mesh degree D, the bounds D_low and D_high at which the heartbeat (periodic mesh maintenance, every 1 s here) grafts or prunes peers, and the minimum outbound count D_out. The specification permits `(D, D_low, D_high, D_out) = (8, 6, 12, 4)`, since `D_out < D_low` and `D_out ≤ D/2`. The go-libp2p-pubsub parameter validator requires `D_out < D/2` and rejects the tuple.^[libp2p/go-libp2p-pubsub, gossipsub.go, GossipSubParams.validate; accessed 2 October 2026.] Interoperability reports MUST also record each implementation's IDONTWANT predicate. Rust compares the serialized pubsub-message length with a strict greater-than; Go compares the payload length, sends at equality and defaults to 1,024 B.

Every encoded RPC, including batched messages and control fields, MUST fit the 65,536 B frame cap. Go MUST configure that cap explicitly instead of using its 1 MiB default (`MPE-NET-014`).

### Standard scenarios

Each scenario ends with a drain, a fixed period after publication stops in which delivery, repair and anchoring continue. The pass criteria are the requirement set's acceptance thresholds for the prototype. Ratios use the declared drain, with omissions kept in the denominator, and passing a scenario discharges only the checks it lists.

| Scenario | Fixture and pass criteria | Requirements |
|---|---|---|
| `baseline` | Nominal load: delivery ≥0.999; each Subscriber ≥0.99; completed p99 ≤10,000 ms; zero duplicates, detected plaintext leaks and panics; at most four sizes. | `MPE-VER-013`, `MPE-VER-014`, `MPE-VER-029`, `MPE-PRV-005` |
| `spam` | One membership offers 100 class-0 MPE Envelopes/s through one ingress: zero acceptance above its local quota; zero panics. Concurrent conflicting publications are a separate experiment. | `MPE-ECO-016`, `MPE-VER-019` |
| `malformed` | Five hostile peers each offer 20 injections/s during honest traffic: zero panics; honest delivery ≥0.999. | `MPE-VER-020`, `MPE-VER-021` |
| `replay` | Identical wires beyond the duplicate-cache lifetime, copies with fresh slots, and expired input: suppression ratio 1.0; zero panics. Fixtures use the recorded duplicate-cache lifetime and the measured first-receipt time with margin; an injection at +62 s tests past the 60 s Rust duplicate-cache lifetime only when that timing holds. | `MPE-PUB-028`, `MPE-FMT-030`, `MPE-SEC-041` |
| `eclipse` | Withholding attackers at 20% around a victim: victim delivery ≥0.99; zero panics. Gossip delivery is reported separately from delivery that repair recovers. | `MPE-VER-018`, `MPE-NET-011` |

| `fallback` | Explicitly selected ledger lane on a mock ledger: eligible delivery ratio 1.0; zero Messages dropped by overflow of a ledger limit. Class 3, whose body would need 64 parts, fails before submission. | `MPE-PUB-035`, `MPE-FMT-044` |

The proof of concept will run these scenarios first in its deterministic simulator, where one seed fixes every schedule and a failure replays exactly, and then in a single-implementation harness of libp2p swarms in one process, starting at 50 Bus Nodes with the stand-in Admission Proof and a mock ledger. The harness will execute real library code but will not reproduce deployment latency, bandwidth, IP diversity or failure behaviour. A gated run must outlast eviction after 90 loaded heartbeats (90 s at the 1 s heartbeat), and 48-hour retention needs a separate long run. Acceptance MUST repeat gated runs in isolation with at least five seeds, keep failures, and extend runs beyond the applicable eviction, pruning and restart deadlines (`MPE-VER-001`, `MPE-VER-016`, `MPE-VER-041`). Reports MUST separate gossip delivery from feed, repair and back-fill delivery (`MPE-PRF-003`, `MPE-PRF-005`, `MPE-SEC-039`).

### Ledger interface evidence

The ledger interface contracts (Bus Registry, ledger lane and consumer) compile with Compact toolchain 0.35.0 (language 0.27.0, ledger 9.1 release candidate, compact-runtime 0.20.0). They are exploratory code, compiled and costed but not run on a network: no transaction was constructed, proved, balanced or submitted. Their generated JavaScript was also exercised in the Compact runtime simulator, covering registration, relay management and Anchor posting with their refusal cases; the historic Anchor-root check across a later Anchor; revocation with its refusals, and pause and unpause; the 16-part ledger-lane call, its emission order and refusal of a foreign `Misc` name; and hash encodings.

That exercise runs the generated JavaScript, not the circuits. It does not show that the circuit constraints accept the same executions, and it did not run the consumer's cross-contract call. The consumer contract was compiled and costed but not executed.

A circuit's k is the smallest integer whose 2^k^ rows hold it. The 77,971 rows of `revoke` exceed 2^16^ = 65,536 but fit in 2^17^ = 131,072, so `revoke` has k = 17. In the circuit model the main circuits run from k = 6 for the witness-free root check to about k = 22 for sixteen ledger-lane parts in one call.

|---|---|---|---|
| `register` | 13 | 6,305 | Two SHA-256 evaluations; membership tree per period |
| `revoke` | 17 | 77,971 | Rate-limit evidence check and Merkle path |
| `addRelay`, `removeRelay`, `setSteward` | 17 | 67,234 to 67,862 | Dominated by the byte work of the governance `Misc` payload |
| `pause`, `unpause`, `proposeParams` | 15 | 26,902 to 27,842 | |
| `postAnchor` | 18 | 152,138 | Dominated by the payload bytes of its single `emit` |
| `anchorRootValid` | 6 | 46 | Witness-free; called by consumers |
| One ledger-lane part (`publishClass0`, `publishPart`) | 18 | 166,241; 186,418 | Emitting a 256-byte circuit argument |
| Four parts in one call | 20 | 663,950 | |

| `react` (consumer) | 17 | 77,905 | 19 SHA-256 evaluations with struct preimages, plus a second call to `anchorRootValid` |

Every proof in the model is at most 4,368 B, and every verifying key at most 2,119 B. The k = 18 proving keys are 67 to 76 MB. Key generation for one part took 264 s and 1.8 GB of memory; proving time and prover memory were not measured. Turning circuit values into bytes dominates proving cost, and `emit` needs bytes: emitting a constant 256-byte payload costs 23 rows, while emitting a 256-byte argument costs 166,241. In the runtime simulator, `persistentHash` over a `Bytes` value, or over a struct of `Bytes` fields, equalled plain SHA-256 of the concatenated bytes, so the RFC 6962 batch roots should be verifiable in a contract; agreement inside the circuit is still to be shown. The same 19 hashes cost 703,378 rows (k = 20) when written with byte spreads and 77,905 rows (k = 17) with struct preimages. Whether a circuit can recompute the EID from the sealed body has not been compiled.

Fees for the main transactions are derived from the ledger-9 cost model at genesis mainnet prices, with estimated block bytes; none was measured.

|---|---|---|
| Registration | about 8,670 | 0.246 / 0.308 |

Live prices move each block, and mainnet still runs the ledger-8 parameter schema.

### Adversarial tests, measurements and release gates

Adversarial suites MUST cover targeted discovery poisoning, cold-start Sybils, withholding, partitions, old roots, revocation, rollback, crash boundaries and 1,000 admission identities (`MPE-VER-018`, `MPE-VER-019`, `MPE-VER-023`). Tests include hostile Indexer data, forged receipts and Anchors, malicious payloads, replacement of compromised keys, and 100 concurrent contract reactions with exactly one committed effect (`MPE-SEC-020`, `MPE-SEC-022`, `MPE-SEC-032`, `MPE-VER-032`, `MPE-VER-033`). A consumption attempt that uses Anchor inclusion without a Publisher signature MUST fail to produce authority. Executions with swapped interests MUST produce identical infrastructure traces from the library, including failures, repair, errors and active probes (`MPE-VER-028`).

Decoder fuzzing MUST complete 1,000,000,000 iterations that reach the decoder without a crash, keeping the corpus, coverage and sanitizer results (`MPE-VER-005`). Short property tests do not satisfy that gate. Bounded exploration of admission, delivery and consumption MUST preserve counterexamples and state bounds (`MPE-VER-007` to `MPE-VER-009`). The pinned GossipSub profile also requires the scoring-vulnerability regression, which replays the CVE-2022-47547 attack against the complete scoring profile and every supported topic configuration (`MPE-VER-026`).

Reports MUST include p50, p95 and p99 latency; expected, delivered, late and missing pairs, and admission outcomes; CPU, resident memory and cache occupancy; transport amplification and Subscriber bytes; and attribution of recovered delivery (`MPE-VER-016`).

Comparisons of ingress and egress require consistent endpoints, traffic categories and observation intervals. Nominal acceptance requires at least 99.9% timely delivery within 10 s, counted over all expected deliveries, including missing ones (`MPE-VER-014`). The resource gates are 0.30 core per edge Bus Node, 2 GiB resident memory, 6 Mbit/s per direction and 32 GiB per retained Shard. Desktop recognition may use 0.5 core at 100 MPE Envelopes/s with 32 keys (`MPE-VER-015`, `MPE-PRF-019`, `MPE-PRF-041`). These values are acceptance targets for Midnight Express; GossipSub does not guarantee them. Admission verification has a 10 ms gate on one core of a 4-vCPU machine, and the proof must fit the selected slot (`MPE-ECO-048`, `MPE-FMT-048`).

Exploratory runs guided the design but supply no reference values for these gates; the proof of concept, the next step, will make the first measurements against them. Savings at deployment scale and at lower IDONTWANT thresholds are unknown. Published GossipSub latency figures describe their own experiments: the 141 ms p99 result concerns a warm network under one network-wide eclipse attack [@2020-vyzovitis-gossipsub].

Before Active, acceptance MUST include the 72-hour nominal soak; scale evaluations at 50, 200 and 1,000 Bus Nodes; integration with a real ledger-9 network, with measured transaction costs and timing replacing the derived fees above; and zero unresolved critical or high-severity findings from an independent security assessment (`MPE-VER-012`, `MPE-VER-017`, `MPE-VER-030`, `MPE-VER-034`, `MPE-VER-037`).

Mainnet ran ledger 8.0 as of 4 August 2026, and contract events arrive with ledger 9. While the target network runs ledger 8, no production claim is made for Anchors, the ledger lane or contract consumption (`MPE-OPS-044`). The production pilot requires at least 99.9% canary delivery within 5 s for 30 consecutive days (`MPE-VER-038`). Canary delivery counts MPE Envelopes that monitor organizations publish on a schedule in every Shard through the ordinary size classes and admission path. Removing the launch roster also requires the separate red-team gate with Sybil identities at a 1:1 ratio, at no less than 99.9% delivery (`MPE-OPS-008`).
