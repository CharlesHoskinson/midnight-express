# What the exploration suggested and what comes first {#ch10}

Midnight Express has no proof of concept yet. Throwaway exploratory runs in Rust on rust-libp2p (libp2p 0.57, gossipsub 0.50) were used to understand the design and to decide what the proof of concept should be. The exploratory code contained the sealed, fixed-size MPE Envelope (in this chapter, the Envelope), the stand-in Admission Proof, Bus Nodes and Store Nodes, a mock ledger with a Bus Registry, Anchors and a contract Consumer, and the ledger lane. Its figures appear in this chapter and nowhere else in the document, and each one is an unreplicated exploratory observation. They informed the design. No requirement, threshold, choice or recommendation rests on them.

The ledger interface of Midnight Express was also written in Compact: the Bus Registry, the ledger-lane contract and a consumer contract. The contracts were compiled with Midnight's ledger-9 toolchain, their circuit sizes taken from the circuit model and their fees derived from the ledger's cost model. The compile shows that these contracts can be written in Compact today for ledger 9 without changing the Midnight node. Nothing ran on a network: no transaction was constructed, proved, balanced or submitted. The contracts are exploratory code, and the design does not depend on them being final.

The proof of concept, a single reference implementation with a deterministic simulator, is the next step. Chapter 8 specifies the system it will implement and Chapter 9 the experiments it will run.

Everything the exploratory runs carried was a Message, the application message that a Publisher seals into an Envelope and that is meant to hold the properties of a Confidential Message (Chapter 4). None of the runs carried a contract event. Anchors, ledger-lane parts and governance notices are ordinary public `Misc` events under MIP-0002 [@midnight-mip-0002], some with ciphertext payloads. They are not private events in the Midnight Foundation's sense, which are contract events whose fields are encrypted for chosen recipients and bound to the transaction by a commitment the proof checks. MPS-0005 plans private events for its Part 2 and has not published that text [@midnight-mps-0005]. Carried events (Messages whose payload is the unchanged bytes of a contract event) were not exercised either.

## What the exploratory runs suggested

Every figure in this section is an unreplicated exploratory observation. Each scenario of Chapter 9 ran once, at seed 1, for a 60 s publish window, with 50 Bus Node swarms plus Publisher, Subscriber and Store Node swarms in one process on a 6-core host. Networking was in memory or over loopback, with no link delay, loss, bandwidth limit or IP diversity. Proof verification was a 4.5 ms busy loop, and the ledger, Indexer, Bus Registry and fees were mocks without the `bytes_written` limit or fallible phases. Transport, codec and reconciliation settings were not held fixed from run to run, so no figure separates the effect of a design choice from the effect of the code around it. Delivery is counted in eligible Message-Subscriber pairs, a pair being eligible when the Subscriber follows the Message's stream, and p99 is the time from the publish call to authentication within which 99 percent of delivered pairs arrived.

The Envelope format behaved as specified. Every honest run showed exactly the four wire lengths of the specification, 776, 1,544, 4,616 and 16,904 B. A scan of the 38,938 GossipSub payloads and client frames captured after transport decryption in one leakage run found none of the planted payload canaries, stream or admission secrets, keys or identity commitments; a canary is a known byte string placed inside a Message payload, and its appearance in a frame would mean the sealing failed. The scan matched contiguous byte strings, so a key in a field encoded as a CBOR integer array would have escaped it. At baseline, 1,108 of 1,108 pairs arrived with p99 2.39 s. Under spam, one membership offering 6,000 class-0 Envelopes at 100 per second through one ingress Bus Node had exactly 128 accepted, its quota of 64 per admission window over the two windows the attack spanned. Honest delivery stayed at 1.0, but honest p99 rose to 14.3 s because second-source reconciliation had to repair 103 Subscriber observations that the overloaded ingress path had dropped. Of 6,002 malformed injections, 107 reached the application validator; graylisting (a score below −80) and rate limits dropped the rest before any cryptographic work.

IDONTWANT mattered more than expected. The expectation was a saving under 10 percent in one process, where no slow link gives a notice time to beat a duplicate. In paired runs at the nominal mix, median ingress amplification (bytes received per Bus Node over unique accepted Envelope bytes) was 2.26× with IDONTWANT and 5.12× without. Class-3 egress fell by 70 to 76 percent across the class-3 and nominal-mix pairs (at the byte cap, 1.72 against 5.85 MB per Bus Node in 60 s), while class 0 gained nothing (1.17 against 1.20 MB). A plausible mechanism is that each hop spent 4.5 ms in proof verification while rust-libp2p sends IDONTWANT before validation, so the notice overtook most duplicates even in one process [@2025-farooq-staggering, p. 6]. Mesh egress sat far below the planning rule. A class-3 run published 194 Envelopes in 60 s, 83 percent of the byte cap (194 × 16,904 B / 60 s = 54,656 B/s; 54,656 / 65,536 = 0.834), and dividing by 0.834 puts the median Bus Node at about 0.61 Mbit/s of egress and the busiest at 3.3 Mbit/s, against the rule's 5.24 Mbit/s. One Bus Node that also served Subscriber feeds with a 5 s reconciliation interval sent 43.0 MB in 60 s (5.7 Mbit/s). With IDONTWANT on, the (8, 6, 12, 4) mesh used 7 to 8 percent more median traffic than (6, 5, 12, 2). A victim surrounded by inbound withholders (10 attackers against 27 honest peers) received every Envelope, which on links with no delay says little.

Three restart behaviours were tried under churn, in which 10 percent of the Bus Nodes were replaced every 10 s, and they bracket the design. Where Bus Nodes inside the 140 s restart barrier Ignored gossip and clients could not learn the addresses of replacement Store Nodes, 904 of 1,108 pairs arrived (0.816, worst Subscriber 0.454). Where barrier Bus Nodes stayed silent and Subscribers repaired every 60 s, every pair arrived but p99 was 42.7 s. Where barrier Bus Nodes answered Busy, the Publisher retried elsewhere and each Subscriber repaired every 5 s, every pair arrived with p99 1.10 s, at 4.2 times the gateway bytes per client (7.59 against 1.81 GB/day). The behaviours differed in more than one setting, so these runs do not separate the reply from the repair interval. In back-fill, a late Subscriber resumed across Store Nodes of different Bus Operators with the portable (expiry, Envelope Identifier) cursor; without a reconciler, it missed the one Message in flight at the instant it joined (60 of 61).

Two failures that follow from the specification by argument appeared as expected. One membership that spent its single class-3 credit at several ingress Bus Nodes at once had every conflicting Envelope accepted (2 of 2 in one run, 5 of 5 in another). With the Envelope Identifier as GossipSub message identifier, one corrupted-slot copy sent ahead of an honest Envelope cost one pair of 1,108, which the 60 s reconciliation did not repair before the drain ended; with the full-byte identifier the same run delivered 1,108 of 1,108. No run lasted the 90 loaded heartbeats that application eviction needs, so no withholder was evicted inside a scenario.

Subscriber costs tracked the arithmetic, and the feed protocol around the payload did not. Recognition by salted Recognition Tag cost 0.18 to 0.50 µs per key, so 256 keys at 100 Envelopes per second came to 0.5 to 1.3 percent of a core. Trial decryption was not written; an estimate from authenticated-open timings gives about 3.5, 7.5 and 18.7 µs per key and Envelope at classes 0, 2 and 3, which at class 2 and 100 per second is about 2.4 percent of a core at 32 keys (7.5 µs × 32 × 100) and about 19 percent at 256. Whole-Shard download was 1.67 to 1.69 GB/day, within 2 percent of the arithmetic for the observed rate of 532 Envelopes per minute at a mean wire length of about 2,183.5 B (1,161,632 B per minute × 1,440 minutes = 1.67 GB). One feed protocol that overlapped its cursor by 2 s on every 1 s poll drew 3.9 times its raw feed through the gateway (8.27 against 2.09 GB/day), while another drew 1.08 times. Size-class counts were 325, 119, 62 and 26 per minute for classes 0 to 3, so class 3 sat below the floor of 30. At 50 Envelopes per second the shared host collapsed: 2,527 of 6,004 pairs (42.1 percent) arrived, with p99 57.2 s.

On the mock ledger, with the overlay off, the ledger lane delivered every Message of classes 0 to 2, including a burst of 200 at 20 s, at 9.0 to 10.3 kB of ledger bytes per Message and p99 of 25.0 to 36.2 s. The latency came from the modelled 6 s blocks, finality after 3 blocks and up to 1 s of Indexer polling, not from Midnight. All-or-nothing reassembly let a single malformed part group block every valid group in the same read, and a reader that reassembled before draining every log entry of a block lost Messages in a burst.

Host contention moved results as much as any design choice did. The spam scenario delivered 1,198 of 1,198 honest pairs when run alone and 795 (0.66) when other work shared the host.

## Choices that rest on derivation and source code

The choices below do not rest on the exploratory runs. Each follows from the specification, the Foundation's published limits, the libp2p source or a cited paper, and the derivation is given with it. Where the exploration prompted a choice that no derivation supports, the choice is a hypothesis with a test and a threshold in Table 10.5.

### Envelope overhead and the associated data

The fixed cost of the Envelope follows from the specification. Class 0's 776 wire bytes are the 8-byte visible header, the 512-byte admission slot and a 256-byte Sealed Body (8 + 512 + 256 = 776). Inside the body, 170 B are fixed fields (salt, nonce, Recognition Tag, authenticator, and the sealed header with its signature), which leaves 256 − 170 = 86 B for the payload. The larger classes add body bytes and nothing else, so every class carries the same 690 B of fixed overhead (8 + 512 + 170 B), and payload capacity is the wire length minus 690 B: 86, 854, 3,926 and 16,214 B. In class 0 the overhead is 690 of 776 B (89 percent), and the header and slot alone are 520 B (67 percent); in class 3 it is 4.1 percent. Figure 10.3 shows the four classes side by side.


Writing the format exposed a conflict in the requirement set that follows from the specification alone. MPE-CRY-004 places header bytes 0 to 7 in the AEAD associated data. Associated data is the part of an authenticated encryption that is checked but not encrypted, and the reader must supply exactly the same bytes or decryption fails. Header bytes 4 to 7 hold the expiry. The ledger lane carries only the Sealed Body, in 256 B `Misc` parts, so a reader of a body-only copy cannot rebuild the visible expiry and, under MPE-CRY-004 as written, could not open the Envelope at all. The specified profile puts header bytes 0 to 3 (version, class, Shard and a zero reserved byte), which either carrier can rebuild, into the associated data; it fixes expiry at creation plus 172,800 s and authenticates it through the Envelope Identifier, the signature and admission (`MPE-CRY-004`, `MPE-FMT-037`, `MPE-FMT-051`). A variable lifetime would need a sealed expiry field.

The pubsub origin fields (`from`, `seqno`, `signature`, `key`) would link an Envelope to its Publisher's libp2p identity, so Midnight Express uses the StrictNoSign policy, under which a message carries none of them, and rust-libp2p enforces it on receipt in its anonymous validation mode. The `key` field alone, the publisher's public key, still reveals that identity. Under anonymous validation, the pinned gossipsub 0.50.0 decoder rejects the first three fields but not `key` on its own; the check that StrictNoSign requires arrived in 0.51.0.^[libp2p/rust-libp2p, protocols/gossipsub/src/protocol.rs (decoder branch for `ValidationMode::Anonymous`) and protocols/gossipsub/CHANGELOG.md, entry for 0.51.0 ("Reject messages carrying a `key` field in `ValidationMode::Anonymous`"); release 0.50.0 and master branch, accessed 1 October 2026.] The proof of concept must carry that check into its pinned dependency and test it (`MPE-NET-009`).

### Transport profile and IDONTWANT

GossipSub v1.2 adds IDONTWANT: a peer that has received a message above a size threshold tells its mesh peers not to send it again [@2025-farooq-staggering, p. 2]. The v1.2 specification makes both sending and honouring the notice optional, forbids penalising a peer that sends a message after a notice, and is not yet final (version r2 of 30 August 2026). A link to a v1.1 peer has no IDONTWANT, whatever the local Bus Node supports. The case for requiring it is arithmetic. A Bus Node with eight mesh peers that receives a class-3 Envelope of 16,904 B can receive further copies from every peer that got the Envelope elsewhere and forwards it into its own mesh. A notice of a few dozen bytes naming the 32-byte message identifier replaces each 16,904 B duplicate that has not yet been sent, but only when the notice arrives in time, and that timing on real links is what Q5 tests.

The library constrains how v1.2 is pinned. In stock rust-libp2p gossipsub, the version argument of a custom protocol identifier selects the router's internal peer kind, and that argument accepts only v1.0 or v1.1, while the supplied string is what goes on the wire. A network-scoped name ending in `1.2.0` therefore negotiates that name, but the router treats the peer as v1.1, and IDONTWANT stays off.^[libp2p/rust-libp2p, protocols/gossipsub/src/config.rs, enum `Version` (variants `V1_0` and `V1_1` only) and `ConfigBuilder::protocol_id`; release 0.50.0 and master branch (0.51.0), accessed 1 October 2026. The default `idontwant_message_size_threshold` in the same file is 1,000 B.] Pinning v1.2 on a custom identifier takes a 3-line `V1_2` arm in a vendored copy of the crate. The library's default offer list begins with `/meshsub/1.3.0`, then 1.2.0, 1.1.0 and 1.0.0, so a default offer does not by itself establish v1.2. The transport profile that follows has four parts:

- Shard GossipSub topics and request-response identifiers use `/mpe/<g>/1`, where `g` is the first eight bytes of the genesis hash in lowercase hexadecimal.
- The transport requires negotiating `/meshsub/1.2.0`; the specified system negotiates `/mpe/<g>/1/gossipsub/1.2.0` through the vendored change and does not interoperate with peers that offer only the standard identifiers unless it is configured to.
- Each Bus Node records the identifier and internal peer kind it negotiated on every link.
- A default offer list does not count as evidence of v1.2.

The libraries test the threshold differently. rust-libp2p compares the serialized pubsub message length with a strict greater-than, while go-libp2p-pubsub compares the payload length, sends at equality, and defaults to 1,024 B. Class 0's 776 B Envelope, plus pubsub framing, stays below the configured 1,000 B, so class 0 cannot gain from IDONTWANT at that setting, and every report must record the predicate in force. Per-class egress is not observable through the public API and needs a hook inside the connection handler.

### Mesh egress

The requirement set budgets mesh egress as 1.25 × D × the Shard byte cap: 1.25 × 8 × 65,536 B/s × 8 bit/B = 5.24 Mbit/s. That rule is a planning assumption, not a bound in the GossipSub specification. It assumes that a Bus Node forwards each Envelope to its D mesh peers. At the byte cap the Shard's unique traffic is 65,536 B/s × 8 = 0.52 Mbit/s, and D times that is 4.19 Mbit/s before the 1.25 margin.

The rule overstates by construction. When every endpoint, every traffic category and the same interval are counted, every byte sent over the mesh is a byte received, so mean egress equals mean ingress, which is unique bytes times amplification, not D times unique bytes. A Bus Node never forwards an Envelope back to the peer it came from and skips peers that sent IDONTWANT, so amplification stays below D, and how far below is what Q4 measures. Client feeds, repair and traffic crossing the boundary of the measured set break the equality, so feed and repair service needs a budget of its own. `MPE-PRF-013` and `MPE-PRF-014` replace the 1.25 × D rule with unique bytes × measured amplification, labelled a planning assumption, and budget feed and repair service separately; Q4 tests whether every Bus Node stays within 6 Mbit/s.

The chosen tuple (8, 6, 12, 4) meets the specification's constraints (`D_out < D_low` and `D_out ≤ D/2`). The inspected go-libp2p-pubsub validator requires `D_out < D/2` and rejects it.^[libp2p/go-libp2p-pubsub, gossipsub.go, `GossipSubParams.validate`; master branch, accessed 1 October 2026.] Interoperability with Go under this profile is unestablished, and no claim is made until the Go validator accepts the tuple and an interoperability test succeeds (`MPE-NET-011`). The choice between (8, 6, 12, 4) and (6, 5, 12, 2) waits for the cold-start Sybil result that `DEC-012` names.

### Restart barrier, Busy reply and repair

A Bus Node holds its admission nullifier state in memory. An admission nullifier is derived from the member's secret, the admission window, the class and the credit index, so it is the same for every Envelope that spends that credit. A Bus Node that has just started cannot tell whether a credit was already spent, and without a barrier it would accept a second Envelope under that credit. Waiting two 60 s admission windows plus the 20 s tolerance (2 × 60 + 20 = 140 s, P-ECO-7) lets every admission nullifier it might have missed expire.

What a Bus Node inside the barrier does with a publish request follows from the rust-libp2p source. The router records a message identifier in its duplicate cache when the message arrives, before the application validator runs, and keeps it after Reject or Ignore. A copy that a Bus Node Ignores because it is busy therefore stays suppressed at that Bus Node while the entry lives, which is 60 s in the Rust configuration and 120 s under go-libp2p-pubsub's default.^[libp2p/rust-libp2p, protocols/gossipsub/src/behaviour.rs, function `handle_received_message` (insertion into `duplicate_cache` before validation) and protocols/gossipsub/src/config.rs, default `duplicate_cache_time` of 60 s and default history windows of 5, of which 3 are gossiped; master branch, accessed 1 October 2026.] Gossip gives up on such a copy within seconds. A Bus Node advertises the identifiers of recent messages to peers outside its mesh (IHAVE), and a peer that lacks one asks for it (IWANT); with a 1 s heartbeat, IHAVE covers only the last three history windows, about 3 s (3 × 1 s). Inside the gossip path, the copy is lost at that Bus Node unless a still-eligible retransmission arrives after the cache entry expires.

Two requirements follow. A Bus Node answers a publish request with Busy or Refused for local, non-content reasons (full queue, rate limit, restart barrier, stale view), so that the Publisher can send the same bytes to another Bus Node at once; silence stays for Reject and content Ignore (`MPE-NET-049`, `MPE-STO-015`). Busy and Refused are service replies to a publishing client, not GossipSub validator outcomes; a gossip copy that a Bus Node cannot take receives the validator outcome Ignore, which drops it without penalising anyone, while Reject lowers the forwarding peer's score through P4. And recovery must come from a second source outside the gossip path. A Subscriber compares the Envelope Identifiers it holds for each 60 s window with the inventory of a Store Node run by a different Bus Operator and fetches any window with a gap, from its first second after joining, because a Message in flight at the instant of joining is in neither the store snapshot nor the live feed (`MPE-SEC-009`, `MPE-PUB-014`). Clients obtain Bus Node and Store Node addresses from the finalized Bus Registry relay list and refresh them on change, because a client that learns addresses only at start has nowhere to repair from once its Store Nodes are replaced (`MPE-NET-054`, `MPE-PUB-013`). How short the reconciliation interval must be is open (P-PUB-3); Table 10.5 states the hypothesis.

The replay fixture depends on the same cache. Identical bytes re-injected 62 s after the originals fall past the 60 s duplicate cache configured in rust-libp2p, so only the application seen-set can catch them. go-libp2p-pubsub keeps seen identifiers for 120 s by default.^[libp2p/go-libp2p-pubsub, pubsub.go, variable `TimeCacheDuration` (120 s); master branch, accessed 1 October 2026.] A fixture for another library must use that library's recorded cache lifetime and the first-receipt time, with a margin.

### Quotas, message identifiers and eviction

Each Bus Node can bound only its own acceptances. Every Envelope that spends a given credit carries the same admission nullifier. Suppose a member creates two Envelopes on its one class-3 credit and sends one to Bus Node X and the other to Bus Node Y at the same moment. Neither has seen that admission nullifier, so each accepts the Envelope it received and forwards it. When Y's Envelope later reaches X through the mesh, X finds the admission nullifier already recorded with a different Envelope Identifier and Ignores it, but by then both Envelopes are in the mesh. Deployed relays that use rate-limiting nullifiers behave the same way: each routing peer keeps a local nullifier map and punishes double signalling after the fact [@2022-taheri-waku-rln-relay, p. 4]. The requirement that admitted "at most one Envelope per allowance network-wide" (`MPE-ECO-022`) is therefore false for independent validators, and it becomes a per-Bus-Node bound with no network-wide count claimed.

Of the three options for `DEC-014`, only option (b) spares honest peers and still leads to revocation. Option (a), rejecting the second copy, would apply the P4 penalty to honest forwarders that saw it first; option (c), deduplicating with no rule for conflicting acceptances, yields no evidence. Option (b) ignores the second copy, keeps the evidence and gossips it, and revokes the membership (`MPE-ECO-020`, `MPE-SEC-018`). Subscribers keep at most one Envelope per visible admission nullifier. The evidence is what makes revocation possible. Each Envelope carries one point on a line whose intercept is the member's secret, at a position fixed by its Envelope Identifier. One point reveals nothing; two points with distinct Envelope Identifiers fix the line and with it the secret. The Bus Registry's revocation circuit checks two such shares together with the member's Merkle path, and it compiles at k = 17 (Table 10.1).

The GossipSub message identifier is the hash of the complete Envelope bytes, because the router records an identifier before the application validator runs, so the first copy to arrive takes the identifier even if that copy is then Rejected. The Envelope Identifier excludes the admission slot. Were it the GossipSub identifier, an attacker who altered one byte of an honest Envelope's slot and delivered the copy first would have it Rejected and the honest Envelope dropped as already seen. The bytes hashed are the immutable `Message.data`, admission slot included, and exclude protobuf, RPC, Noise and Yamux framing (`DEC-010` option (b), `MPE-SEC-042`). The full-byte identifier closes this one collision path, assuming the hash resists collisions. It is not a general guarantee of delivery: other ways of filling the transport's seen state before validation remain possible.

Application eviction needs enforcement that does not rest on one score. A heartbeat counts as loaded when the Bus Node accepted at least one Envelope during it, and eviction targets a mesh peer that has not been first to deliver any of those Envelopes for 90 such heartbeats. An application score (P5) of −20 does not guarantee pruning. A peer's capped per-GossipSub-topic score contribution can reach +32, and +32 − 20 leaves a positive total, while the heartbeat prunes a mesh peer for its score only when the total is negative. A replacement score of −33 makes the total negative when P6 and P7 are not positive (`MPE-NET-022`).

Eclipse resistance is not established by anything run so far. Outbound quotas resist capture by inbound connections only while honest dial sources exist, and a victim that keeps even a few honest outbound peers is not eclipsed. The published evaluation of GossipSub v1.1 used 50 ms of latency per edge, 1,000 honest peers against 4,000 Sybils, and a Sybil-to-honest connection ratio of 20 [@2020-vyzovitis-gossipsub-v11-evaluation, pp. 8-9]. The fully eclipsed victim, whose every connection belongs to the attacker [@2015-heilman-eclipse, p. 1], and poisoned discovery covering every dial source remain to be tested.

### Bus Node and Subscriber sizing

A Bus Node forwards only what it has validated itself, so every Bus Node verifies the proof of every Envelope. At the 4.5 ms stand-in cost, 10 Envelopes per second take 10 × 4.5 ms = 45 ms of CPU per second, 0.045 of a core. Fifty Bus Nodes on one host verifying 50 proofs per second need 11.25 cores (50 × 50 × 4.5 ms per second), against 6 available, so a single host cannot test the 50-per-second ceiling. One Bus Node at 50 per second would need 0.225 of a core for verification, inside the 0.30-core edge budget. The count ceiling stays at 10 per second until one Bus Node per host is measured (Q2). At baseline, verification alone takes 50 × 10 × 4.5 ms = 2.25 core-seconds per second, over a third of the 6-core host, which is why every gated run must have the host to itself.

The application seen-set is independent of GossipSub's transport seen cache. It must hold every accepted Envelope Identifier until expiry plus P-FMT-4 (60 s): 10 × 172,860 s = 1.73 million identifiers per Shard. If an entry is dropped before its Envelope expires, that Envelope can be accepted a second time, which is the replay the seen-set exists to stop. A seen-set capped at 65,536 identifiers that answered Busy once full would go silent after 6,554 s at 10 per second (65,536 / 10), about 1.8 hours, and stay silent for two days, which no 60 s scenario can show. The requirement is therefore one of sizing, never met by a cap; where the protection cannot be maintained, the Bus Node refuses new admissions explicitly (`MPE-PUB-027`, `MPE-STO-013`, `MPE-PRF-041`). By the same arithmetic, an admission nullifier cache of 8,192 entries would refuse Accepts above about 58 per second per Bus Node (8,192 / 140 s).

Recognition happens at the Subscriber, so every Subscriber downloads every Envelope on its Shard, whatever streams it follows. At 600 Envelopes per minute and the baseline mean of 2,158.4 B, that is 10 × 2,158.4 B × 86,400 s = 1.86 GB/day, 31 to 33 times a mobile budget of 56 to 60 MB/day. Whole-Shard reception is therefore a desktop or always-connected profile, and mobile needs one of the opt-in profiles of Chapter 6. One Shard of eight would still be 1.86 / 8 = 233 MB/day.

An Envelope's size class is visible, so a Message hides only among the Envelopes of its own class in the same period. At 10 per second the baseline mix gives 600 Envelopes per minute, and class 3, at 5 percent, gives 30 per minute, exactly the floor set in Chapter 7. At the 50-per-second weights class 3 would receive 3,000 × 0.001 = 3 per minute. The decision belongs with `DEC-013`.

The ledger lane's bytes per Message follow from the transaction shape the mock assumed, a base of 8,192 B plus 288 B per part. Classes 0 to 2 use 1, 4 and 16 parts (Sealed Bodies of 256, 1,024 and 4,096 B divided by the 256 B payload of one `Misc` event), and a class-3 body of 16,384 B would need 64, so class 3 is refused before a transaction is constructed. Transactions are then 8,480, 9,344 and 12,800 B. Weighted 60, 25 and 10 and renormalised to 95 percent, because class 3 cannot use the lane, they give (0.60 × 8,480 + 0.25 × 9,344 + 0.10 × 12,800) / 0.95 = 9,162 B per Message. A reader groups parts by transaction and intent in emission order, as MIP-0019 specifies for multipart events [@midnight-mip-0019]. A Midnight transaction applies in two phases (segments in the ledger code): the guaranteed phase runs before the fee is taken and dismisses the transaction if it fails, and the fallible phase runs after it, discarding its state changes and events on failure while the fee stays paid. A reader therefore counts parts from a fallible phase only if that phase succeeded.

`final` means that the Message is covered by a Bus Registry Anchor in a finalized block. An Anchor commits to every Envelope accepted in one 60 s window: the anchorer computes a Merkle tree over the window's Envelope Identifiers and posts its root. The authority for `final` is Bus Registry state. The `Misc` copy of the Anchor is a notification, because events are not consensus state [@midnight-mip-0002]. A Subscriber decides `final` per window from the anchorer's published leaf list; the request is per window, not per Message, so it adds no interest leak (`MPE-CON-016`, `MPE-STO-042`, `MPE-NET-052`).

## Ledger interface contracts, compiled and costed

The figures in this section are compile results from the Compact toolchain, circuit-model figures from the prover's cost model, and fees derived from the ledger's cost model. None was measured on a Midnight network, devnet or local node.

Each entry point of a Compact contract compiles to a circuit, the computation that the caller proves in zero knowledge to have carried out correctly. To prove it, the prover fills in a table of rows that encodes every step, and the size of that table sets the prover's time and memory.

### What the contracts contain

The Bus Registry holds one membership tree per membership period, a set of revoked identity commitments, the parameters (with a time-lock on changes), the relay list, a pause flag and a steward, and the Anchors. The Anchors form a ring of 2,940 slots indexed by `window mod 2940`; each slot holds the window, its root and eight Shard counts. A `HistoricMerkleTree` of depth 12 over the Anchor leaves lets other contracts check an Anchor root through `anchorRootValid`. That circuit is witness-free. A witness is the private input that a caller's own code supplies to a circuit; `anchorRootValid` takes none, so it depends only on its arguments and public state, which is what lets another contract call it.

The ledger-lane contract emits one 256-byte `Misc` part per call. It can also emit a whole class-0, class-1 or class-2 Sealed Body in one call, and it has no circuit for class 3. The consumer contract has one circuit, `react`. It takes a private RFC 6962 inclusion path from an Envelope Identifier to an Anchor-tree root, checks that root against the Bus Registry through a cross-contract call, checks expiry, and records a consumption nullifier together with the action in the same state transition. A consumption nullifier is derived from the Message's secret and the contract, so every reaction to the same Message carries the same value, and once the contract has recorded it the contract refuses the rest. `react` discloses the Anchor-tree root, a coarse expiry bound, the consumption nullifier and the action. The Envelope Identifier, window, Shard, paths and Message secret stay private.

The contracts were compiled with Compact toolchain 0.35.0 (language 0.27.0, ledger 9.1.0.0-rc.3, compact-runtime 0.20.0) on the ZKIR v2 back end, on a host with 6 cores and 47 GB of memory. Proving and verifying keys were generated for every circuit except the largest. A simulator test against the generated JavaScript covered registration and relay management, Anchor posting with five refusal cases, the historic Anchor-root check after a later Anchor, revocation with two refusal cases, pause, the 16-part ledger-lane call and its emission order, refusal of a foreign `Misc` name, and the hash encodings. The simulator exercises the JavaScript, not the circuits, and it did not run the cross-contract call. Proving time and prover memory were not measured.

The ledger-8 toolchain, 0.31.1 (language 0.23.0), rejects `emit` (`unbound identifier emit`). With its two `emit` statements removed, the Bus Registry compiles with 0.31.1, so `emit` is the Bus Registry's only dependency on ledger 9. The consumer contract does not compile with 0.31.1 at all, because the ledger-8 cross-contract model needs the callee's compiled contract information. Events arrived with toolchain 0.33.0, and the 0.35.0 release notes state that ledger 9 was not yet deployed on mainnet as of 28 September.^[LFDT-Minokawa/compact, doc/release-notes/toolchain-0.33.0.md (events and the `emit` expression) and doc/release-notes/toolchain-0.35.0.md ("targets Midnight ledger 9 which is not yet deployed on Midnight Mainnet"); main branch, accessed 1 October 2026.] Mainnet ran ledger 8.0 on 4 August 2026, and MIP-0002 is live on Stagenet. Anchors and the ledger lane therefore depend on ledger 9, which mainnet does not yet run (`MPE-OPS-044`).

The `Misc` names are 32-byte constants (`mip-xxxx:anchor[v1]` and `mip-xxxx:envelope[v1]`, NUL-padded). In a circuit a constant costs almost nothing: a probe that emits a constant 256-byte payload needs 23 rows.

Most of the proving cost goes into turning circuit values into bytes, and `emit` needs bytes. Compact packs a `Bytes<n>` value into field elements, the numbers the proof system computes with, several bytes to each element. Getting a single byte back out means proving a division of the packed value by a power of two. Every operation that needs individual bytes adds `div_mod_power_of_two` instructions of about 670 rows each; a byte spread, an index, a cast from `Uint` to `Bytes` and the encoding of an `emit` payload that is not a constant all do.

A probe isolates the effect. A constant 256-byte payload costs 23 rows (k = 6), while the same payload passed as a circuit argument costs 166,241 rows (k = 18). Three chained SHA-256 node hashes cost 133,922 rows (k = 18) when the preimage is assembled from byte spreads and 12,674 rows (k = 14) when the preimage is a struct of `Bytes` fields, with identical digests.

One ledger-lane part is therefore a k = 18 circuit. `publishPart` needs 20,177 more rows than `publishClass0` (186,418 against 166,241) because it takes the 32-byte event name as an argument, checks it and emits it, where `publishClass0` emits a constant name. Four parts make a k = 20 circuit, and sixteen parts a k = 22 circuit of 2,651,779 rows, for which the cost model alone took 1,071 s and 2.5 GB of memory to evaluate. `postAnchor` spends most of its 152,138 rows on the payload of its single `emit`, so every Anchor is a k = 18 proof: one per minute per anchorer.

The governance circuits (`pause` at k = 15; `addRelay`, `removeRelay` and `setSteward` at k = 17) are large for the same reason: they do byte work on their governance payloads and `Bytes<32>` arguments. Checking the authority secret is cheap by comparison, and `register` computes two SHA-256 hashes in 6,305 rows.

The verifier side does not grow with k, but the prover side does. Every proof in the model is at most 4,368 B, and every verifying key at most 2,119 B. The k = 18 proving keys, by contrast, are 67 to 76 MB, and key generation at k = 18 took 195 to 264 s and 1.8 GB of memory. At k = 20, key generation took 1,105 s and 7.1 GB and produced a 270 MB proving key. Key generation also downloads the prover parameters on first use: 201 MB for 2^20, and four times that for 2^22.

The consumer is affordable only with struct preimages. `react` evaluates 19 SHA-256 hashes. With struct preimages it is 77,905 rows (k = 17). Written with byte spreads, it needed 703,378 rows (k = 20), and key generation had not finished after 25 minutes.

`persistentHash` over a `Bytes` value, or over a struct of `Bytes` fields, is plain SHA-256 of the concatenated bytes, which the simulator checked against a reference SHA-256. A contract can therefore verify the RFC 6962 batch roots of the Anchors byte for byte. Whether a circuit can also recompute the Envelope Identifier itself was not tested.

A Midnight-native Admission Proof would not fit the admission slot. If an Admission Proof used the proof system of Midnight contracts, the model's proof sizes of 2,800 to 4,368 B would not fit the 512-byte slot, and under `DEC-013` a real slot above 1,024 B removes class 0. The admission proof system is not chosen, so this is a bound on one option.

### Fees

Fees follow the ledger-9 cost model.^[midnightntwrk/midnight-ledger, base-crypto/src/cost_model.rs (fee from normalised cost dimensions; net bytes written as `max(0, written − deleted)`), onchain-vm/src/vm.rs (opcode `Log`, whose size counts as both written and deleted) and ledger/src/construct.rs (transcript gas declared as one re-run times 1.2; placement of a transcript in the guaranteed or fallible phase); release 9.1.0.0-rc.4, accessed 1 October 2026. Prices and limits: midnightntwrk/midnight-node, res/mainnet/ledger-parameters-config.json, `limits.block_limits` and `fee_prices`; main branch, accessed 1 October 2026.] The model has five cost dimensions: read time, compute time, block bytes, net bytes written and bytes churned. Each dimension is divided by its block limit, and the fee is the largest of the first three ratios, plus the write and churn ratios, multiplied by the overall price.

The genesis prices of the mainnet parameter file set an overall price of 10 DUST and per-block limits of 2 s of read time, 2 s of compute, 1,000,000 block bytes, 50,000 bytes written and 50,000,000 bytes churned. Each unit price is the overall price divided by the block limit (for compute, 10 DUST / 2,000 ms = 0.005 DUST per ms), which gives 0.005 DUST per ms of compute or read, 0.00001 DUST per block byte, 0.0002 DUST per net byte written and 0.0000002 DUST per byte churned.

The estimate uses these inputs:

- contract-call costs equal to the simulator's transcript gas times 1.2, the factor the ledger's transaction-construction code declares;
- contract proofs of 4,368 B, verifying keys of 2,119 B and a DUST spend proof of 2,912 B;
- other block bytes estimated at 700 to 900 B per transaction and about 306 B per `Misc` event;
- a wallet margin of five blocks, a factor of 1.2519.

Transcript gas is the cost of the public part of a contract call, the ledger operations that every Midnight node replays when it applies the call, in compute time, read time and bytes written and deleted. It also decides placement. A call enters the guaranteed phase only if its modelled cost fits the time-to-dismiss budget, the work a Midnight node does on a transaction before taking the fee (the larger of 2 µs per transaction byte and 15 ms, less a per-transaction reserve); otherwise it runs in the fallible phase (Table 10.4).

At genesis prices, every single-call transaction in Table 10.2 costs under 0.25 DUST before margin.

The registration row can be checked by hand. Compute is the largest of the first three terms: 26.2 ms × 0.005 = 0.131 DUST, against 8,670 × 0.00001 = 0.087 DUST for block bytes. Net writes add 560 × 0.0002 = 0.112 DUST, and churn adds 17,203 × 0.0000002 = 0.003 DUST. The sum is 0.246 DUST, and the wallet margin makes it 0.246 × 1.2519 = 0.308, inside the registration gate P-ECO-13 of 16 KiB and 0.5 DUST for a transaction of about 8.7 KB.

The last row is a range because an added part raises whichever term dominates. Where compute dominates, 1.33 ms × 0.005 = 0.0067 DUST plus 832 × 0.0000002 = 0.0002 DUST of churn gives +0.0068; where block bytes dominate, 306 × 0.00001 = 0.0031 DUST plus the same churn gives +0.0032.

The Anchor row needs one caveat. The simulator reports more bytes deleted than written for `postAnchor`, because each record replaces an older one, so net written is taken as zero. Each 100 B of real net growth would add 0.02 DUST. Anchors cost about 280 DUST per day at genesis prices if one is posted every 60 s (1,440 × 0.195), or about 351 DUST with margin.

The ledger lane has two shapes, and they trade fees against proving. Sixteen separate part calls in one intent cost 4.6 times as much as one 16-part call (0.866 against 0.188 DUST), because sixteen proofs dominate the block bytes: the proofs alone take 16 × 4,368 = 69,888 B of the 80,260 B. Block bytes then outweigh compute, and 80,260 × 0.00001 = 0.803 DUST, plus 0.045 for net writes and 0.018 for churn, gives 0.866. The one-call shape is the expensive one to prove (k = 22).

Live prices move by up to about 4.6 percent per dimension per block, and mainnet still runs the ledger-8 parameter schema, so these figures are estimates at genesis prices.

DUST cannot be paid to a contract, because it is non-transferable and pays only transaction fees. A contract can neither require a registration price in DUST nor observe one, so the network fee of the registration transaction is the only price, unless a token price in NIGHT or another token is added (`MPE-ECO-056`). Bonds, where used, are Bus Registry contract balances in such a token, and they have no relation to NIGHT staking.

### Ledger-lane capacity

Ledger-lane throughput depends on which block limit binds, and the sources disagree about that. MIP-0002 states that events compete for a shared `bytes_written` budget of 50 KB per block, and it estimates a serialization overhead of about 40 B per event, which makes a part about 328 B (288 B of struct data plus 40) [@midnight-mip-0002]. The simulator charges each additional `Misc` inside a call 693 B of bytes written and deleted.

The ledger-9 release-candidate code points elsewhere. There a `Log` counts its size as both written and deleted, so its net bytes written are zero and the bytes are charged as churn instead. The mainnet parameter file sets the churn limit at 50,000,000 B per block, so if that accounting holds on a live ledger-9 network, `block_usage` binds rather than `bytes_written`. Whether the live `block_usage` limit is 200,000 or 1,000,000 B is itself unmeasured.

Table 10.3 gives whole-chain capacity under each reading. The class-1 and class-2 transactions are the single-call shapes: about 8,000 B before parts plus about 306 B per part, which gives about 9,250 B and 12,920 B. Each cell divides the block limit by what one Message uses under that reading, rounds down, and divides by the 6 s block. Under the first reading a class-1 Message writes 4 × 328 = 1,312 B, so a block holds 50,000 / 1,312 = 38 of them, or 38 / 6 = 6.3 per second.

The proving cost points the same way, independently of the fee question: a 16-part call is a k = 22 circuit, while a 4-part call is k = 20 and has generated keys. Both favour the response Chapter 9 names for a failed Q16, which is to carry classes 0 and 1 on the ledger lane and lower P-FMT-8 from 16 parts to 4. The measurement that settles it is the one `MPE-PRF-040` requires: on a ledger-9 devnet or Stagenet, the serialized size and `bytes_written` of one `Misc` event and of 1-, 4- and 16-part groups, their fees, and which limit binds.

Further requirement consequences follow from what the Bus Registry can express. Membership is one tree per membership period, removed by a permissionless prune one root window after the period ends (`MPE-ECO-026`). ## What the proof of concept must establish

### First-milestone deliverables

The first milestone of the proof of concept will deliver two instruments. The first is a deterministic simulator: one process in which every Bus Node, Store Node, Publisher and Subscriber runs the reference code against a virtual clock and a seeded scheduler, with modelled link delay, loss and bandwidth per edge and CPU charged per Bus Node. Its acceptance test is reproducibility: the same seed and configuration must yield a byte-identical event trace on two runs. The second is a runnable single-implementation harness that drives the same code over real sockets for the ten standard scenarios of Chapter 9, with a configuration echo that records the negotiated protocol identifier and peer kind on every link, the IDONTWANT threshold and predicate, the complete score profile and the cache lifetimes.

Both instruments will apply the controls of Chapter 9 from the start. Each gated run will have the host to itself, use seeds 1 to 5, last longer than the time constant of the rule it tests (at least 90 loaded heartbeats for eviction, 140 s for barrier exit), and attribute every delivered pair to the gossip path, reconciliation, back-fill or the ledger lane (`MPE-VER-041`, `MPE-VER-011`). Positive controls plant each defect on purpose: a canary in a frame of each protocol, the Envelope Identifier as message identifier, a silent restart barrier, IDONTWANT off, and an eviction score that leaves a withholder's total positive. A gate result counts only if its positive control fails.

### Open beyond the first milestone

Several questions need more than the first milestone can provide. The stand-in Admission Proof gives every Bus Node the member secrets, so every relay can link each Envelope to its member and could forge one. No unlinkability claim for admission can be made, and `DEC-007` stays open. The real proof's verification cost, its slot width and therefore `DEC-013`'s slot trigger wait for a chosen proof system, as do whether it runs on the client hardware classes and how the admission secret is kept from any prover outside the client's trust boundary.

Wide-area behaviour needs hardware beyond one host. The IP-colocation penalty P6 [@2020-gossipsub-v11-spec] can only penalise every peer alike on one host, and in production it can penalise honest peers behind shared NAT, hosting or proxies. Eclipse with a fully attacker-controlled dial set, forged IHAVE without IWANT answers, withholding under real delay, and behaviour at 200 and 1,000 Bus Nodes under a 20 percent cold-start Sybil, which is the evidence `DEC-012` waits for, all remain. The published evaluation found cold-boot attacks to be the only partly effective attacks against v1.1 [@2020-vyzovitis-gossipsub-v11-evaluation, p. 11]; at a cold start no peer has yet earned score, so scoring cannot yet tell an honest peer from a Sybil.

Scoring safety across several Shards is unknown. The per-GossipSub-topic score cap applies after positive and negative contributions are combined, so rewards on one Shard can hide invalid-delivery penalties on another. CVE-2022-47547 records an attack of this kind: a peer that throttles delivery in one GossipSub topic while keeping its overall score positive avoids pruning, and such peers can combine into eclipse and partition attacks [@2023-kumar-gossipsub-acl2s, pp. 13-15]. Whether the pinned release and the complete scoring profile close it is unknown until configuration-specific evidence exists. Idle Shards (Q17), the stale chain view in a running overlay (Q7), the storage multiplier (Q10), receipt cost (Q14) and time to `final` with window agreement (Q15) also wait, along with interoperability with go-libp2p-pubsub, whose identifiers, default version lists, outbound-quota check, IDONTWANT predicate and Peer Exchange all differ.

For a contract's private event, in the Foundation's sense, to travel unchanged as a carried event, the client must forward the exact bytes the ledger will record, the chain must keep the binding, the event must fit a size class, recipients must be able to recognise it, Consumers must keep it at `gossip` until the bytes match a finalized transcript (counting a fallible-phase event only if that phase succeeded), and carriage must add no meaning and no protocol fee. None of these has been tested. Once MPS-0005 Part 2 is published [@midnight-mps-0005], one private event should be carried under these conditions.

## Limits of the evidence

The exploratory runs are not evidence for any property of Midnight Express: they informed the design and the order of Table 10.5, and nothing rests on them. The compiled contracts are evidence of a narrower kind. They show that the ledger interface can be written in Compact for ledger 9, and what it would cost under the ledger's published cost model at genesis prices. No proof was generated or verified, and no transaction reached a chain. The toolchain and ledger are release candidates of a generation that mainnet does not run, and the numbers can change before ledger 9 is deployed.

Equal wire lengths hide payload length within a size class, but the class itself, message timing, volume and the full transport transcript stay visible. The ingress Bus Node sees the submitting endpoint's libp2p identity and network address, whatever the pubsub signing mode, and later relays see the identities and timing of their forwarding peers. Neighbours see possession times through IHAVE, IWANT and IDONTWANT; Identify, if enabled, adds public keys, addresses and supported protocols; and peer scoring keeps per-peer and per-GossipSub-topic state at every Bus Operator. Whole-Shard interest privacy holds only if every client action is independent of recognition, including requests, retries, errors, repair, receipts and diagnostics. The stock IWANT handlers do not read Envelope expiry, so whether the send path ever serves an expired Envelope is unknown, and expiry cannot delete ciphertext or metadata that an adversarial store has recorded.
