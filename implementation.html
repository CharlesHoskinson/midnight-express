AVAILABLE NOW / CONFORMANCE & LOCAL PROTOTYPE

What the reference
implements today.

Shared meaning, independently checked

The v0.2 reference defines how a quote, payment observation or sandbox approval must be represented. For RFQ fixtures, independent Python, Rust and TypeScript interpreters agree on canonical bytes. Two declared price formats preserve the same economics and complete intent; ambiguous and unsupported input is refused.

Each immutable contract bundle fixes the meaning of an event independently of the software release that reads it. The original v0.1 remains read-only history.

Failure evidence from a real database

The Umbra/PostgreSQL sandbox commits a report row together with the records needed to recover it: inbox, action, budget, outbox, checkpoint and cursor. Tests that kill the worker recover one report write. If a destination acknowledgement is uncertain, processing waits for reconciliation.

Source evidence and authority come from fixtures, as does the destination acknowledgement. The full protocol still needs genuine admission, signing, sealed-wire integration and live finality. Customer baselines remain uncollected.

FIRST / SHARED MEANING

Shared meaning through
workflow contracts.

A shared JSON format cannot tell a buyer whether a price is per share or per hundred shares. Each participant must agree to the same economic meaning.

Read the Data Model guide

One common mechanism

Every event carries a private identity, source, time and exact contract commitment. Workflow-specific contracts define what its fields mean: quote economics, invoice evidence or agent permissions. Deterministic adapters translate declared source conventions and reject missing units or roles.

100 shares at $123.45 each means $12,345. A cents-per-hundred quote can agree only when its currency, denominator and perspective are explicitly declared.

Designed and reference-tested

The reference checks unsigned fixtures for quotes, payment observations and sandbox approvals. It tests strict parsing, expiry and conflicts between event occurrences and business actions. RFQ agreement covers a finite corpus of accepted examples and refusals. Validation returns executes:false.

The separate recovery sandbox exercises a local effect: one database report row under fixture authority. It does not authenticate production participants or establish consensus evidence, and it has not been integrated with the complete protocol.

Literature basis: Domain-Driven Design · CloudEvents · JSON Schema · FINOS CDM · UBL Invoice · JCS

APPLICATION DOMAINS / ETHEREUM & SOLANA

Application events
and their chain evidence.

Ethereum and Solana applications observe chain activity through different messages and finality rules. The proposed domain contracts preserve those differences so a receiver can interpret an observation without treating it as permission to act.

Ethereum applications

RPC queries and results; blocks and reorgs; pending transactions and receipts; contract logs; balances and storage; ERC-20, ERC-721 and ERC-1155 transfers and approvals; wallet accounts, network changes and signing requests.

Safe and finalized remain Ethereum-specific assertions. Removed logs invalidate prior observations. Replacement and drop claims retain their inference policy; ABI and contract-version commitments identify decoded meaning.

Solana applications

RPC queries and results; slots, blocks and roots; account/program updates; transaction signatures and errors; instruction and CPI evidence; SOL and SPL/Token-2022 balances, transfers and authorities; wallet signing requests.

Processed, confirmed and finalized remain Solana-specific commitments. Preserve cluster identity, mint decimals, account owner and decoder version. Logs alone do not establish successful execution.

Shared coverage and implementation boundary

Simulation, fees, subscription lifecycle, gap/backfill, staking, governance, NFT, oracle, DeFi and bridge observations receive explicit domain families. Protocol-specific swaps, lending and bridge completion require separately pinned ABI/program contracts. Large RPC objects use bounded projections and evidence references. The machine catalog records the proposed vocabulary. Separate read-only ERC-20 and legacy SPL projections test exact raw amounts, instruction outcomes, rollback and replay against supplied fixtures. The full vocabulary, live chain adapters and production execution contracts remain proposed.

SPRINT 01 / PROPOSED TWO-WEEK TIMEBOX

Exchange a quote with agreed meaning.

Start with a quote: a price convention can change the value of a trade. Test whether independently specified participant formats preserve the same economics and complete intent over the proposed transport.

What we build

  • Closed RFQ contract and two deterministic source adapters; independent Rust/TypeScript interpretation.
  • A buyer and two dealers on actual whole-shard GossipSub, with typed requests, offers and off-chain acceptance.
  • Start genuine admission and finalized Registry feasibility immediately; label any mock boundary.

What the demo must show

100 shares at 123.45 USD per share maps identically from two declared price conventions. An expired offer, wrong role or missing price basis fails.

Evidence required to advance
  • Equal canonical economics and intent commitments across implementations; ambiguous mappings reject.
  • Independent wire vectors; trace inspection finds no RFQ topics or upstream business selectors.
  • Candidate proof compatibility and actual encoded size/verification report, or a named blocking mismatch.

SPRINT 02 / PROPOSED TWO-WEEK TIMEBOX

Recover without changing the outcome.

Once participants agree on a quote, test what happens when delivery repeats or a host fails. The accepted meaning and committed progress must survive crashes and reconnect.

What we build

  • Compose authenticated inbox, dedup, local effect, outbox, checkpoint and cursor in one UmbraDB/PostgreSQL transaction.
  • Persistent opaque replay fixture, bounded retries, encrypted quarantine and explicit gap handling.
  • Carry pinned semantic contracts through restart and replay; continue proof, root and privacy checks.

What the demo must show

Kill the host before commit, after commit before acknowledgement and during outbox delivery. Replay and restore one accepted record, with no cursor beyond an uncommitted effect.

Evidence required to advance
  • Real process/crash fault matrix; same business action with a new EID cannot duplicate the effect.
  • Expired redrive, wrong restore context and unsupported contract cannot become actionable.
  • Paired recognition traces show no subscription-dependent acknowledgements or schema fetches.

SPRINT 03 / PROPOSED TWO-WEEK TIMEBOX

Test reuse and the investment case.

After recovery holds, add invoices and human-approved agents. Test whether they can reuse the processing core while keeping their own meaning and authorization rules.

What we build

  • Restricted invoice/payment observations with pending, partial, final and reversed evidence; no payment execution.
  • Separate proposal, approval, execution request and result contracts for one human-approved sandbox action.
  • Reuse fault harness and measure adapter effort; evaluate chain-domain profiles without treating indexer observations as authority.

What the demo must show

A payment fixture cannot become paid by changing its status. A changed target, expired approval, excess budget or repeated action is rejected. Uncertain remote completion goes to reconciliation.

Evidence required to advance
  • Both workflows retain their domain rules through the same durable core.
  • Destination idempotency or explicit reconciliation prevents unsafe automatic retries.
  • Reproducible decision pack: observed results, integration cost, proof status and unresolved production gates.

CROSS-SPRINT / DIFFICULT DEPENDENCY FIRST

Test genuine membership
and quota admission.

Begin admission work in Sprint 1 because it can block the full protocol even when the business demo works. The proof must establish membership and enforce publication quotas; mock admission cannot test that requirement.

Proof compatibility

Check the chosen proof relation, identity commitments, field/hash, tree, class-credit bounds and EID binding against Midnight Registry requirements. Root changes and resealing must not renew quota. One proof combines the claims; library compatibility is an experiment.

Prototype acceptance criteria

MPE-ECO-048 sets prototype measurement gates: encoded slot roundUp64(104 + proofBytes) ≤ 4096 and a 10,000-proof verification benchmark on one core of a 4-vCPU VM. Report median and p99. Use p99 ≤ 10 ms as conservative project acceptance policy; the original requirement does not specify that percentile.

The 4096-byte ceiling does not establish fit in the existing fixed 512-byte fixture slot. Actual relation and wire-profile evidence are required. These results have not been measured here.

AFTER THREE SPRINTS / INVESTMENT DECISION

Continue, revise or stop
with evidence.

Evidence for the next step

Advance when independent formats agree, unsafe ambiguity is refused and process failures preserve the authorized outcome. Adding a workflow must retain its domain rules at an acceptable integration cost. Record commands, commits, toolchains, configuration, fixtures and complete fault results so the decision can be reproduced.

Compare turnaround and manual work with a participant baseline when available. Customer savings require a measured baseline and partner results.

What remains beyond the prototype

Finalized Registry integration, security review, replicated opaque retention, WAN/load evidence, operator economics and hardened operations. Automatic settlement needs separate business authority and anchored-message binding. OpenMLS and mobile discovery require separate validation.

Repair failed recovery checks before adding more workflows. Reconcile uncertain remote effects, and report genuine-proof results separately from mocked workflows.
