# Leakage declaration

This prototype implements the symmetric invitation profile without forward secrecy. Only the application can opt into the ledger fallback; there is no silent fallback.

- **L-R, relays:** Header version, class, shard, reserved byte, expiry and 512-byte admission slot; salt, nonce and salted tag; total length, timing, first ingress and peer connections. The stand-in verifier additionally holds all admission secrets, maps nullifiers to memberships and can forge proofs. Its wire slots have no stable per-member distinguishing value, but that does not give Bus Node unlinkability. Full-shard feed and interval/cursor repair requests carry no recognition keys or tag filters. Seen caches, source topology and class timing remain observable.
- **L-S, stores:** Everything relays see, retention deadline, complete window inventories and signed organization receipts. Receipts are requested by window, not recognized event. Data is retained in volatile memory; receipts make no durable-storage claim.
- **L-I, indexer:** Fallback clients query the common bus contract address, inclusive event cursor and limit. The indexer sees connection/timing and whole-contract reads, without stream-key filters. Only finalized mock blocks are returned; retention coverage is unknown.
- **L-L, ledger:** Registry roots and their publication/supersession times, relay identities/organizations/roles, parameters and pause flag. Anchor transactions disclose the 60-second creation window, combined Merkle root and one count per shard. The fallback discloses emitting contract, transaction/intent grouping, ordinal, constant name `mpe/env/v1`, 256-byte ciphertext parts, fee number and timing; no admission slot or transaction signer. Explicit reactions disclose the signed statement (network, header/prefix hash, stream ID, LEI, sequence, schema/version, creation/expiry, payload hash, destination and action), its publisher signature, consumption nullifier and optional inclusion proof. Reaction contains neither a stream/event secret nor Sealed Body. The mock requires a separately registered statement-hash/EID/nullifier relation, kept outside the transaction; this trusted fixture is not a Compact/ZK proof and does not prove production nullifier soundness. Mock Registry state stores no body.
- **L-C, consumer:** Authorized opened payload, schema, publisher, sequence, logical ID, creation/expiry, exactly one finality label and carrier, and verified stored attribute. Consumer reactions are explicit application calls.

The wire scanner searches all captured envelopes and CBOR protocol frames for fixture payload canaries, LEIs, stream secrets/IDs, recognition/encryption keys, admission secrets/commitments, publisher keys and schema identifiers. Random byte strings of at least eight bytes are considered forbidden. Results are observations over a seeded short run. No paired timing KS test, offset chi-square test or infrastructure log audit was performed. A zero wire-leak count is not a traffic-analysis, source-anonymity or zero-knowledge claim.

Repair retention: normal node/client builds omit per-message validation traces and tag-match EID diagnostics. The simulator explicitly enables the `diagnostics` features, with a 200000-entry validation ring and ten-minute tag-match history. Plaintext deliveries, received-EID observations and sequence/gap state expire after ten minutes. Dedup is separate, capped at 2000000 records and retained through expiry + one hour. Feed rings are capped at 64 MiB and 50000 rows per Shard and pruned at expiry. The canary matcher is cached across frames and refreshed when the append-only fixture list grows.
